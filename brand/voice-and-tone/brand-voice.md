# Brand Voice

> Sources: Brand positioning (Confluence), Brandbook v2

## The Short Version

Ditto sounds like the sharpest person at the dinner table — someone who makes complex things feel obvious, not by dumbing them down, but by thinking them through. Warm when it matters. Precise when it counts. Never dull.

We're a language company at heart. "Care. Clarified." isn't just a tagline — it's a method. We care about words the way a watchmaker cares about gears: every one does something.

## Voice Principles

### 1. Clarity With Intelligence

Clear doesn't mean simple. It means every sentence lands on the first read — whether it's five words or twenty. The goal isn't fewer words; it's zero wasted ones.

A well-crafted longer sentence can be clearer than a choppy short one. Vary the rhythm. Let some thoughts breathe. Then land the point.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "AI-powered healthcare communication platform" | "Understand what your doctor said" |
| "We leverage advanced technology to facilitate..." | "We turn medical conversations into something you can actually use" |
| "Our solution enables seamless information sharing" | "Share your summary with whoever needs to know" |

Ditto is a language product — so the writing should feel like it was *written*, not assembled. A well-placed turn of phrase, a headline that rewards a second look, a sentence structure that mirrors the point it's making — these aren't luxuries. They're on brand.

What's NOT on brand: puns for the sake of puns, wordplay that obscures meaning, cleverness that makes the writer look smart at the reader's expense.

### 2. Human & Warm

We're talking to people navigating something stressful — their health, a parent's diagnosis, a partner's treatment. The tone should feel like a thoughtful friend, not a customer service script. Someone who knows when to be light and when to be serious.

This means:
- Address the reader directly — "you" and "your," not "patients" or "users"
- Contractions are natural. "You'll" beats "You will."
- Starting with "And" or "But" is fine — it keeps things moving
- Rhetorical questions pull people in. Use them.
- Vary paragraph length. A one-line paragraph after a longer one creates emphasis.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Patients often experience difficulty retaining medical information" | "You forget most of what your doctor said. That's not a personal failing — it's how memory works." |
| "Ditto enables care coordination among family members" | "Keep your family in the loop — without repeating yourself every time someone asks" |
| "Users can share summaries with designated contacts" | "Share it with whoever needs to know" |

### 3. Confident, Not Corporate

We're building something that didn't exist before. That takes conviction. Be direct about what we believe and why — but earn it. Confidence without substance is arrogance.

- Make claims you can back up
- Name the problem: "Healthcare communication is broken"
- Don't hedge with "we believe" or "we strive to" — just say the thing
- But don't oversell. Ditto is a powerful tool, not a miracle.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "We strive to improve healthcare communication" | "Healthcare communication is broken. We're fixing it." |
| "Ditto is the world's leading..." | "75,000 people already use Ditto" |
| "We believe privacy should be a priority" | "Your data stays yours. We built it that way." |

### 4. Trustworthy by Default

In healthcare, trust isn't a nice-to-have — it's the whole game. We earn it by being specific about what we do, what we don't do, and how we handle data. No vague reassurances. Specificity is credibility.

- Privacy by architecture, not by promise
- Be specific: "Your recording stays on your phone" > "We take security seriously"
- Name what we DON'T do — often more convincing than what we do
- When in doubt, be more transparent, not less

## Writing Rules

### Do
- Write with intention. Every sentence should do something the previous one didn't.
- Active voice. "Ditto records" not "recordings are made by Ditto."
- Specific numbers. "75,000 users" not "many users."
- Address the reader directly.
- Lead with the benefit, then explain how.
- Vary sentence length and structure. Monotony kills attention.

### Don't
- No jargon unless you're writing for professionals (and even then, less than you think)
- No filler: "very," "really," "actually," "basically," "simply"
- No corporate speak: "leverage," "facilitate," "enable," "solution," "optimize"
- No passive reassurance: "We take X seriously" — show, don't tell
- No walls of text. But don't chop everything into fragments either. Find the rhythm.
- No "Overview" or "Introduction" headers — just start

### Tone Slider

The voice stays the same, but the register adjusts:

| Context | Register | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Homepage hero** | Warm, considered, resonant | "Understand your healthcare. Help your loved ones understand too." |
| **Feature description** | Clear, grounded, precise | "Record your appointment. Get a summary in language that actually makes sense." |
| **Trust & privacy** | Measured, specific, confident | "Your recording is transcribed on your phone. We never hear it." |
| **For Professionals** | Credible, outcome-focused, respectful | "Better-informed patients. Fewer repeated conversations." |
| **Error/empty states** | Friendly, brief, helpful | "No recordings yet. Bring Ditto to your next appointment." |
| **Social media** | Conversational, relatable, a touch of wit | "That moment when you leave the doctor and immediately forget everything 🙃" |
| **Brand storytelling** | Reflective, crafted, intellectual | "We didn't set out to build an AI company. We set out to solve a communication problem that's been hiding in plain sight." |

## Linguistic Identity

Ditto is, at its core, a language product. We clarify. We translate. We turn medical complexity into human understanding. The brand should reflect that care for language:

- **Intentional word choices** — pick the word that means exactly what you mean, not approximately
- **Structural creativity** — a sentence that mirrors its meaning (e.g., "Care. Clarified." — two words, no clutter, does exactly what it promises)
- **Smart callbacks** — referencing the product's function through the writing itself (we clarify healthcare → our writing is clear)
- **Earned wit** — a clever turn of phrase that also makes the point sharper, not just more fun

What this is NOT: puns, dad jokes, forced humor, or wordplay that makes you groan. The cleverness should make someone think "that's well put" — not "that's trying too hard."

## Tagline

**Care. Clarified.** — Two words, period-separated, always together. Used as sign-off, brand anchor, and closing line. Not a headline — it's a punctuation mark on the brand story. Notice the structure: it does what it says. Two words. No clutter. Clarity as form.

## Emotional Territory

The feelings people have around healthcare — that's where our copy starts. Not features, not product descriptions. The emotion first, then Ditto as the resolution.

| Feeling | When it hits | Lines that work in this territory |
|---|---|---|
| **Overwhelmed** | After a diagnosis, during treatment, too much information at once | "Healthcare shouldn't be a puzzle" |
| **Alone in it** | Caregiver coordinating without support, patient with no one to ask | "Because care is something you carry together" |
| **Confused** | Leaving the doctor, reading a letter, Googling symptoms | "You shouldn't need a medical degree to understand your own health" |
| **Worried about someone** | Parent's appointment, partner's results, child's diagnosis | "When someone you love is dealing with something medical, you want to understand — not just hope for the best" |

These lines landed in external review. Use them as reference for the register we're aiming for — emotionally immediate, personal, not product-descriptive.

## Words We Use vs. Words We Avoid

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Understand | Leverage | Corporate speak |
| Share | Disseminate | Corporate speak |
| Summary | Output | Product-team language |
| Your doctor | Healthcare provider | Nobody says "healthcare provider" |
| Keep in the loop | Enable information sharing | Corporate speak |
| Plain language | Accessible communication | Corporate speak |
| Family / loved ones | Care network (on consumer pages) | Jargon |
| Care Circle | Care network (when referring to the feature) | Feature name exists for a reason |
| Recording | Audio capture | Product-team language |
| Private | Secure (unless discussing architecture) | "Private" is personal, "secure" is technical |

### Phrases That Need Care

Not hard avoids — but phrases that tend to underperform and usually have a better version:

| Phrase | Problem | When it works | Better default |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Manage your health" | App-speak. Patients don't frame health as a task to manage. | Caregiver context — they *do* feel like they're managing someone's care | "Understand your care" / "Stay on top of what matters" |
| "Healthcare moments" | Product-team language. Nobody talks like this. | Rarely. | Describe the actual moment: "your appointment," "your diagnosis," "your results" |
| "All in one place" | Generic. Any app can say this. | When paired with something specific that earns it | Name what's actually together: "Your summaries, your letters, your Care Circle — all connected" |
