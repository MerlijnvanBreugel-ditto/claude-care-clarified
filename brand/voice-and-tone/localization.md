# Localization — English & Dutch

Ditto writes in two languages. They share the same voice — direct, warm, intelligent — but the register and rhythm differ. This guide defines how.

## The Core Principle

**Adapt, don't translate.** A sentence that works in English often fails in Dutch when translated word-for-word. Write each version as if it were the original. The feeling should be identical. The words won't be.

## English

English is the international-facing voice. It reaches UK users, international healthcare professionals, press, and investors.

| Trait | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| **Warm but considered** | Longer emotional arcs land well in English. "Because care is something you carry together." |
| **Rhetorical rhythm** | English allows elegant repetition and parallel structure. "Your partner asks. Then your mother. Then your friend." |
| **Contractions** | Always. "You'll", "it's", "don't" — never "you will" or "it is" in consumer copy. |
| **Understated confidence** | "We can't read your summaries. We built it that way on purpose." — quiet conviction, not chest-beating. |
| **Headline style** | Can be abstract/emotional. "Clarity in the moments that matter" works because English allows poetic compression. |

### English pitfalls to avoid
- Corporate filler: "leverage", "facilitate", "enable", "solution"
- Passive reassurance: "We take your privacy seriously"
- Feature-first framing: "AI-powered summaries" — lead with the outcome
- Hedging: "We believe", "We strive to" — just say the thing

## Dutch (Nederlands)

Dutch is the primary market voice. Most users are Dutch. The NL version should feel like home — not like a translation.

| Trait | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| **Directness** | Dutch readers expect you to get to the point faster. Fewer warm-up sentences, less build-up. Where English takes three sentences to land a point, Dutch does it in two. |
| **Informality** | Always "je/jij" in consumer copy. Never "u" — Ditto is a friend, not an institution. B2B professional copy may use "u" contextually, but default to "je" even there unless addressing senior clinicians formally. |
| **Conversational** | Dutch copy should sound like you're talking to someone at the kitchen table. "Je loopt de spreekkamer uit en denkt: wat zeiden ze ook alweer?" — that's how people actually talk. |
| **Emotional, but earned** | Dutch can carry the same emotional weight as English — "Want zorg draag je samen" lands just as well as "Because care is something you carry together." The difference: Dutch earns emotion through directness and specificity, not through build-up. Get to the feeling faster. |
| **Compound words** | Dutch makes longer words (samenvatting, zorgverlener, huisarts). Headlines need to account for ~20% more character length. |

### Dutch word choices

| English | Dutch | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Doctor / healthcare professional | Arts / huisarts / zorgverlener | "Dokter" is fine colloquially. "Arts" for slightly more neutral. "Huisarts" when specifically GP. |
| Appointment | Afspraak | Never "consultatie" — that's formal/medical jargon. |
| Summary | Samenvatting | Direct, clear, everyone knows it. |
| Loved ones | Naasten / dierbaren | "Naasten" is warmer and more common. "Dierbaren" is slightly more formal/emotional. Use "naasten" as default. |
| Care Circle | Zorgcirkel | Keep consistent with in-app terminology. |
| Recording | Opname | Not "opnemen" as noun. |
| Plain language | Begrijpelijke taal | Literally "understandable language" — more natural than "eenvoudige taal" (simple language), which can feel condescending. |
| Healthcare | Zorg / gezondheidszorg | "Zorg" in most contexts. "Gezondheidszorg" only when referring to the system. |
| Share | Delen | Direct. |
| Privacy | Privacy | Keep English — it's adopted in Dutch. |
| Free | Gratis | Direct. |

### Dutch tone slider

| Context | Register | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Homepage hero** | Warm, direct, recognizable | "Begrijp je zorg. Deel het met wie ertoe doet." |
| **Feature card** | Clear, grounded | "Neem je afspraak op. Krijg een duidelijke samenvatting." |
| **Trust & privacy** | Factual, specific | "Je gegevens blijven op je telefoon. Wij kunnen je samenvattingen niet lezen." |
| **For Professionals** | Respectful, evidence-led | "Beter geïnformeerde patiënten. Minder herhaalde gesprekken." |
| **Error/empty states** | Friendly, brief | "Nog geen opnames. Neem Ditto mee naar je volgende afspraak." |

### Dutch pitfalls to avoid
- Direct translation of English idioms: "in the loop" → there's no natural Dutch equivalent. Use "op de hoogte"
- Anglicisms where Dutch alternatives exist: "managen" → "bijhouden" or "regelen"
- Overly formal medical language: "consultatie" → "afspraak", "medicatie" → "medicijnen"
- Diminutives (-je/-tje) unless genuinely colloquial — they can undermine seriousness in healthcare context
- "Wij vinden privacy belangrijk" — same problem as English: show, don't tell

## The Tagline

**"Care. Clarified."** stays in English across both versions. It's a brand mark, not a sentence to translate. "Zorg. Verduidelijkt." loses the rhythm, the double meaning, and the punch.

On Dutch pages, "Care. Clarified." appears as the brand sign-off — like Nike's "Just Do It" in any market.

## Headlines: EN vs NL Comparison

To show the adaptation principle in practice:

| Section | English | Dutch | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero headline | Clarity in the moments that matter | Duidelijkheid wanneer het ertoe doet | Direct translation works here — but shorter in feel |
| Problem card 1 | The details fade | De details vervagen | Clean equivalent |
| Problem card 2 | It gets overwhelming | Het wordt overweldigend | "Overweldigend" is adopted and natural |
| Problem card 3 | Everyone wants to know | Iedereen wil het weten | Direct, natural |
| Product intro | Meet Ditto | Dit is Ditto | "Meet" doesn't translate well — "Maak kennis met" is too long. "Dit is Ditto" is punchy. |
| CTA | Start using Ditto — it's free | Begin met Ditto — het is gratis | Clean parallel |

## Process

1. Write the English version first (current approach for website v1)
2. Adapt to Dutch — rewrite, don't translate
3. Read the Dutch version aloud. If it sounds like a translation, rewrite it.
4. Check character length on headlines — Dutch runs ~20% longer
5. Verify feature names match in-app terminology
