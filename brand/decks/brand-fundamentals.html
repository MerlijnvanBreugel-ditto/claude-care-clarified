The Starting Point

Brand is not what you say.
It's what people feel.

"A brand is a person's gut feeling about a product, service, or company."

Marty Neumeier, The Brand Gap

You can't control a gut feeling directly. You can only shape the inputs that create it: what you say, how you look, what it feels like to use your product, and whether you keep your promises.

Layer 1

Purpose

Ditto's Purpose

Making healthcare understandable.

The test: Would you still do this work if it wasn't profitable? If yes, you've found purpose.

Care. Clarified. — Two words that capture the purpose and the promise in one breath.

Layer 2

Positioning

The space you own in someone's mind. Most startups describe what they do instead of claiming a space. — April Dunford, Obviously Awesome

Positioning is NOT

  • Features
  • Taglines
  • Messaging
  • A mission statement
  • What you say about yourself

Positioning IS

How your product is the leader at delivering something a specific audience cares deeply about.

It's the context that makes your value obvious.

Framework — April Dunford

The 5 Components of Positioning

Work through these sequentially. Never skip a step.

#ComponentQuestionDitto
1 Competitive alternatives What would they do without you? Forget, misunderstand, Google it, ask family
2 Unique attributes What do you have that they lack? AI conversation capture + plain-language + family sharing
3 Customer value So what? Understand care, remember everything, share with family
4 Best-fit customers Who cares most? Chronic/complex conditions, elderly, caregivers
5 Market category What context makes it obvious? Patient empowerment (NOT transcription)
1 Alternatives → 2 Attributes → 3 Value → 4 Customers → 5 Category

Start with competitive alternatives. Your real competition isn't Otter.ai — it's forgetting.

Framework — Marty Neumeier

The Onlyness Test

The most powerful test of a strategic position.

"Ditto is the ONLY _______ that _______."

If a competitor can say the same thing, you haven't found your position.

Try it:

"Ditto is the only patient empowerment tool that turns medical conversations into plain-language summaries families can share."

Can Otter say this? Can Google say this? If not, you've found Onlyness.

Positioning

Four Traps to Avoid

Trap Phantom Competitors

Don't position against companies you never compete with. Ditto vs. Otter is a phantom fight.

Trap Overly Broad

"For everyone who visits a doctor" is too wide. Narrow = sharp. Start with complex conditions.

Trap Category Creation Too Early

Don't invent a new category until you dominate your niche. Position in "patient empowerment" first.

Trap Product Pessimism

Teams that focus on lost deals miss why they win. Talk to users who love Ditto.

Layer 3

Personality

If Ditto were a person, who would they be?

Clear & Direct

Eliminates jargon. Rejects fluff. When healthcare feels like a maze, we're the straight path.

Empathetic & Personal

We've been the confused patient. We've been the worried family member. We get it.

Bold & Trustworthy

Conventions that don't serve patients? We challenge them. But we earn trust through privacy, not marketing.

The sharpness test: "Friendly and professional" could be any brand. "The calm, confident friend who explains your medical visit over coffee" — that's a real personality.

Verbal Identity

The Apple Whisper

In an attention economy where everyone shouts, Apple whispers. Say less. Mean more.

Too much

"Our advanced AI-powered transcription and summarization engine processes medical conversations in real-time to generate comprehensive patient-friendly summaries."

vs.

Just enough

"You talk to your doctor. We make sure you remember."

Writing rules

Plain language → Short sentences → Active voice → Specific numbers → Evidence for bold claims

The Principles

  • Navy for text & UI — not backgrounds. It anchors through weight, not volume.
  • Photography carries emotion — color supports, never competes
  • Max 3 brand colors per composition
  • Weight 500+ for patient-facing type
  • The flexible 'o' — signature, not wallpaper
Layer 5

Experience

Not what you say about yourself — what it feels like to use your product, read your email, see your ad.

Brand breaks at the weakest touchpoint

A beautiful website means nothing if the app feels like a different company. A warm onboarding means nothing if the error state is cold and confusing.

The DixonBaxi / Heidi Health Principle

Heidi Health's brand is "centred on the humanity it serves." Not the AI. Not the technology.

Ditto equivalent: Every touchpoint should make a tired, anxious patient feel understood and cared for. That's the experience. Everything else is aesthetics.

Framework — Arielle Jackson

The Bar Test

Arielle Jackson (Google, Square) uses this to validate any positioning or messaging.

"Read it aloud. If someone at a bar wouldn't immediately understand what you do and why it matters, rewrite it."

Apply it

Fails the bar test

"Ditto leverages proprietary AI to transform unstructured medical dialogue into actionable, patient-centric health summaries."

Passes the bar test

"Ditto records your doctor visit and gives you a summary you can actually understand. You can share it with family."

Messaging Architecture

How it works

  • Promise is emotional and enduring — rarely changes
  • Pillars are strategic (3-4 max) — every message maps to one
  • Proof points are specific, updated as you grow
  • Audiences get the same pillars, different emphasis

If a message doesn't connect to a pillar, the message is wrong.

Patterns

What Separates Great Brands

Apple, Headspace, Heidi Health — three patterns.

1. Radical clarity about who they serve

Apple isn't for everyone. Headspace isn't for skeptics. Heidi builds for clinicians. Narrowing sharpens everything.

2. Brand = Product

No gap between promise and experience. Headspace's app feels like a hug for your mind. When brand and product are one, you don't need marketing.

3. Discipline over inspiration

Built by saying no — to the off-brand campaign, the clever name, the color that doesn't fit. Consistency compounds.

  • Subtraction — Is anything removable?
  • Emotion — What does the reader feel?
What's Next

Where to Start

Highest leverage first.

1. Formalize positioning

Run the Dunford exercise. Fill in all 5 components. Write the positioning statement. Test with Onlyness and Bar tests.

2. Sharpen personality

Move from "Clear & Empathetic" (generic) to something only Ditto would say. Write the "person at a dinner party" description.

3. Close the visual gaps

Photography guidelines. New color roles. Illustration boundaries. Typography rules documented.

4. Audit the experience

Walk through every touchpoint as a patient. Does it feel like "Care. Clarified."? Flag the gaps.
