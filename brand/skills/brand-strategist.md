# Brand Strategist

Every brand decision answers one question: does this make Ditto more distinctive and more trusted? If it doesn't do both, rethink it.

## When to Use

- Positioning or repositioning decisions
- Messaging for a new feature, partnership, or audience
- Naming features, products, or initiatives
- Reviewing copy/comms for brand alignment
- Brand-level decisions for stakeholders

## 1. Positioning

### The Onlyness Test (Neumeier)

Every positioning decision must pass this filter:

> **"What is the ONE thing only Ditto can claim?"**

If competitors can say the same thing, it's not positioning — it's a feature list. Ditto's Onlyness lives at the intersection of: healthcare AI + patient-first design + family sharing + insurer distribution.

### Positioning Components (Dunford via Lenny's Newsletter)

Work through these sequentially. Never skip a step.

| # | Component | Question | Ditto's Current Answer |
|---|-----------|----------|----------------------|
| 1 | **Competitive alternatives** | What would customers do if we didn't exist? | Ask the doctor to repeat, take notes, Google it, ask family who was there, forget |
| 2 | **Unique attributes** | What do we have that alternatives lack? | AI-powered medical conversation capture + plain-language summaries + family sharing |
| 3 | **Customer value** | So what? Why does that matter? | Patients understand their care, remember everything, share with family — reducing anxiety and improving outcomes |
| 4 | **Best-fit customers** | Who cares most about this value? | Patients with chronic/complex conditions, elderly patients, caregivers managing family health |
| 5 | **Market category** | What context makes our value obvious? | Patient empowerment / health literacy (NOT transcription, NOT medical records) |

### Positioning Statement Template

```
For [best-fit customers]
who [situation/need],
Ditto is the [market category]
that [key value proposition].
Unlike [competitive alternatives],
Ditto [key differentiator].
```

### Positioning Traps to Avoid (Dunford)

- **Phantom competitors**: Don't position against Otter.ai or generic transcription. Our real competition is "do nothing" — patients forgetting, misunderstanding, or not sharing.
- **Overly broad**: "For everyone who visits a doctor" is too wide. Start narrow, expand later.
- **Category creation too early**: Don't invent a new category until you dominate the niche. Position in an existing space (patient empowerment, health literacy) first.
- **Product pessimism**: Don't dismiss what makes you win. Talk to users who love Ditto — they'll tell you why.

## 2. Messaging Architecture

### The Hierarchy

```
Brand Promise (emotional, enduring)
  "Care. Clarified."
    │
    ├── Value Pillars (3-4 max, strategic)
    │     ├── Pillar 1: Understand your care
    │     ├── Pillar 2: Remember everything
    │     ├── Pillar 3: Share with family
    │     └── Pillar 4: [Trust/Privacy — earned, not claimed]
    │
    ├── Proof Points (evidence per pillar)
    │     ├── "AI summaries in plain language"
    │     ├── "35,000+ patients already use Ditto"
    │     └── "Privacy-by-architecture, not policy"
    │
    └── Audience Adaptations (same pillars, different emphasis)
          ├── Patients → Understand + Remember
          ├── Caregivers → Share + Trust
          ├── Insurers → Outcomes + Scale
          └── Providers → Informed patients + Efficiency
```

### Building a Messaging Framework

For any new feature, initiative, or audience:

1. **What's the job-to-be-done?** Not the feature — the outcome the user hires us for.
2. **What pillar does it reinforce?** Every message must connect to a value pillar. If it doesn't fit any pillar, either the message is wrong or you've found a new pillar (rare).
3. **What's the one sentence?** If you can't say it in one sentence, you don't understand it yet.
4. **What's the proof?** Bold claims need evidence. "We help you understand your care" needs "35,000 patients already do."
5. **What's the emotional hook?** People remember feelings, not features. Headspace doesn't sell meditation — they sell "a hug for your mind."

### The Bar Test (Arielle Jackson via Lenny's Newsletter)

Read your positioning/messaging aloud. If someone at a bar wouldn't immediately understand what you do and why it matters, rewrite it.

## 3. Verbal Identity

### Voice Scaling Model (inspired by Headspace)

Ditto's voice needs to flex across contexts without breaking. One voice, many tones.

| Context | Tone Dial | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Onboarding** | Warm, encouraging | "Your first summary is ready. Take a look — we think you'll be surprised how much you caught." |
| **Summary delivery** | Clear, calm, confident | "Here's what your doctor discussed today." |
| **Care Circle invite** | Personal, gentle | "Your care matters to the people around you. Share your summary with someone you trust." |
| **Error/failure** | Honest, reassuring | "We couldn't process this recording. This happens sometimes with background noise. Try recording in a quieter spot next time." |
| **Marketing/external** | Bold, direct, human | "You shouldn't need a medical degree to understand your own health." |
| **Insurer/B2B** | Professional, evidence-led | "Ditto improves health literacy for 35,000+ patients, reducing follow-up calls and improving treatment adherence." |

### Voice Guardrails

**Always:**
- Plain language (8th-grade reading level for patient-facing)
- Active voice ("We created your summary" not "Your summary was created")
- Specific over generic ("75K users" not "many users")
- Short sentences. Then shorter.

**Never:**
- Medical jargon in patient-facing copy
- Corporate speak ("leverage," "synergy," "ecosystem")
- Hedging when we should be direct ("We think maybe this could help")
- False urgency ("Don't miss out!", "Act now!")
- Talking about ourselves when we should talk about the user

### The Apple Whisper (inspired by Apple)

Say less. Mean more. Control every word.

Apple doesn't flood with specs. They say "The best iPhone we've ever made" and let the product prove it. Ditto should follow the same instinct:

- **Bad**: "Our advanced AI-powered transcription and summarization engine processes medical conversations in real-time to generate comprehensive patient-friendly summaries."
- **Good**: "You talk to your doctor. We make sure you remember."

### Naming Principles (Arielle Jackson via Lenny's Newsletter)

When naming features or initiatives:

| Principle | Rule | Ditto Example |
|-----------|------|---------------|
| **Memorable** | 2-3 syllables, easy to say | "Care Circle" (good), "Patient Health Summary Sharing Network" (bad) |
| **Distinctive** | Can't be confused with competitors | Avoid generic: "Health Notes," "Med Summary" |
| **Evocative** | Suggests the benefit, not the mechanism | "Care Circle" suggests warmth + connection, not "share button" |
| **Extensible** | Works as the feature grows | "Care Circle" can expand beyond summaries |

## 4. Brand Narrative

### The Story Arc

Every brand has a founding story. Ditto's follows this structure:

```
Tension:     Healthcare is complex. Patients leave confused. Families are left out.
Villain:     The system — built for providers, not patients.
Hero:        The patient (NOT Ditto — we're the guide, not the hero).
Gift:        Clarity. Understanding. Connection.
Resolution:  Care. Clarified.
```

Ditto is the guide, not the hero. Patients are the heroes. This is the Headspace model — they don't position themselves as the meditation expert, they position the user as someone on "a lifelong journey of better mental health."

### Narrative Dos and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Center the patient's experience | Center our technology |
| Show the emotional outcome | List features |
| Acknowledge the problem is systemic | Blame doctors or insurers |
| Position family involvement as natural | Make sharing feel like surveillance |
| Let real stories carry the message | Over-rely on statistics |

## 6. Multi-Perspective Brand Review

When reviewing brand-level decisions, evaluate from these lenses:

| Perspective | Key Questions |
|---|---|
| **Patient** | Does this make me feel understood? Would I trust this? Is it clear? |
| **Caregiver** | Does this make me feel included, not surveilled? Does it reduce my worry? |
| **Insurer/Partner** | Does this sound credible? Can I put this in front of my board? |
| **Competitor lens** | Could any competitor say this? If yes, it's not positioning. |
| **5-year test** | Will this still be true and relevant in 5 years? |
| **Privacy lens** | Does this accidentally promise or imply data access we don't have? |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Positioning against transcription tools (Otter.ai, etc.) | Our real competitor is "do nothing" — forgetting, misunderstanding, not sharing |
| "For everyone who visits a doctor" | Too broad. Start narrow (chronic/complex conditions), expand later |
| Centering Ditto as the hero | Patient is the hero. Ditto is the guide. Always. |
| Feature-first messaging ("AI-powered...") | Outcome-first. What changes for the person? |
| Inventing a new category prematurely | Dominate the niche first (patient empowerment, health literacy) |
| Copy that any health app could claim | Fails the Onlyness test. Rewrite. |
| Hedging: "We believe..." / "We strive to..." | Just say the thing. Conviction without arrogance. |
