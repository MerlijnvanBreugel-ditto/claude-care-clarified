# Brand Designer

Every visual choice either calms the user or adds to their anxiety. When in doubt, choose calm.

## When to Use

- Evaluating visual assets for brand alignment
- Color, typography, or photography decisions
- Reviewing designs, mockups, or marketing materials
- Briefing designers or agencies
- Resolving visual inconsistencies across touchpoints

## Before Starting

Read these:
- `brand/visual-design/brand-evolution.md` — where we came from, where we are
- `brand/voice-and-tone/brand-voice.md` — visual must match verbal
- `brand/strategy/values-and-culture.md` — personality traits to express
- `brand/assets/Ditto_Brandbook_v2.pdf` — original brandbook (partially outdated)

## Quick Reference

| Decision | Default |
|---|---|
| Photo or illustration? | Photo for external, illustration for in-product |
| Clever or calm? | Calm. Always calm. |
| Warm or cool? | Warm. Gold > gray. |
| Add or remove? | Remove. Fewer elements, more impact. |
| Product or person? | Person is visually dominant. Always. |
| New color needed? | No. Work with existing palette. Expansion is a brand decision. |
| Stock photo? | Never. Commission or create a brief. |

## 1. Design Philosophy

### The Ditto Design Stance

Ditto's visual identity expresses four things simultaneously:

| Quality | What it means visually | What it's NOT |
|---------|----------------------|---------------|
| **Clarity** | Clean hierarchy, generous whitespace, nothing competes | Minimal for minimal's sake. Stripped of warmth. |
| **Warmth** | Photography of real people, warm tones, soft textures | Cutesy, childish, or "wellness brand pastel" |
| **Confidence** | Bold typography, decisive color, no visual hedging | Loud, aggressive, or attention-seeking |
| **Trust** | Consistent, precise, professional | Corporate, cold, institutional |

This is the same tension Heidi Health solved: calm, precise, and human all at once. DixonBaxi built Heidi's brand around the humanity it serves — not the AI it runs. Ditto must do the same.

### The Inevitability Test (Jony Ive)

Before approving any visual:

> "Does this feel inevitable? Like the only solution that could exist?"

If a design could belong to any health app, it's not Ditto. If it requires explanation, it's not clear enough. If anything can be removed without losing meaning, remove it.

### The Calm Test (Headspace)

> "Does this reduce cognitive load or add to it?"

Users open Ditto during stressful moments — before, during, or after a doctor visit. Every visual choice either calms them or adds to their anxiety. When in doubt, choose calm.

## 2. Visual Identity System

### Color

**Current palette** (from brand-evolution.md):

| Role | Color | Hex | Usage |
|------|-------|-----|-------|
| **Anchor** | Navy | `#0D164F` | Backgrounds, headers, primary brand surfaces |
| **Primary accent** | Teal | `#58B6D2` | CTAs, links, interactive elements |
| **Warmth** | Gold | `#D4A832` | Highlights, warmth accents, celebration moments |
| **Light accent** | Light blue | `#7DD4E8` | Secondary surfaces, backgrounds |
| **Alert/Pain** | Red | `#D94F4F` | Errors, medical urgency, pain indicators |
| **UI only** | Yellow | `#F3DE70` | Buttons, small UI elements (NOT brand-level) |

**Color Principles:**
- Navy does the heavy lifting. It's the constant.
- Teal signals action and interactivity.
- Gold adds warmth — use it where you want the user to feel cared for.
- Photography carries emotion. Color supports, never competes.
- **Never** use more than 3 colors in a single composition (excluding neutrals).

**Color Pairing Rules:**

| Combination | When to use | Watch out for |
|-------------|-------------|---------------|
| Navy + White | Default. Clean, trustworthy. | Can feel cold without photography or gold accent |
| Navy + Teal | Interactive, digital-first surfaces | Don't overuse — teal needs breathing room |
| Navy + Gold | Warm, premium, editorial | Gold at large scale can feel financial, not health |
| White + Teal + Gold | Light, approachable, patient-facing | Needs navy anchor or looks ungrounded |

### Typography

**Current stack** (from brand-evolution.md):

| Role | Font | Usage |
|------|------|-------|
| **Headings** | Bricolage Grotesque | Display, headers, emphasis. Bold, expressive, distinctive. |
| **Body** | Inter | Running text, UI, long-form. Clean, readable, neutral. |

**Typography Rules:**
- Bricolage Grotesque is the personality. Use it boldly — large sizes, strong weights.
- Inter is the workhorse. Never try to make it decorative.
- Minimum body text: 16px on web, 14px in app.
- Line height: 1.4-1.6 for body, 1.1-1.2 for headlines.
- **Weight matters**: Use 500+ for patient-facing content. Thin type (300/400) feels weak and uncertain — the opposite of what patients need. (Jessica Hische via Lenny's Newsletter: weight conveys confidence.)
- **Hierarchy through type, not just size** (Paula Scher principle): Use weight, case, and spacing to create hierarchy before reaching for size differences.

### The Flexible 'o'

The organic 'o' from the Ditto wordmark is the brand's signature element. It represents:
- The dynamic nature of healthcare (always in motion)
- Flexibility and continuity of care
- Connection between elements of care

**Usage rules:**
- Can be used as a standalone graphic element
- Never distort, rotate, or change its proportions
- Can be filled with brand colors or photography
- Works as a framing device, container, or accent
- Don't overuse — it should feel like a signature, not wallpaper

### Photography

**Current direction** (from brand-evolution.md): Photography-forward, editorial, warm, film-grain quality.

**Photography Principles (to formalize):**

| Principle | What it means | Reference |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Real people, real settings** | No stock photo energy. Genuine moments of connection, care, conversation. | Heidi Health: "brand centred on the humanity it serves" |
| **Warm light** | Golden hour, soft natural light, warm interior tones. Never flat/clinical lighting. | Headspace: illustrations carry warmth; for Ditto, photography does this job |
| **Film quality** | Slight grain, soft focus on background, analog feel. Not over-processed. | Distinguishes from sterile stock photography |
| **Dignity** | Subjects are active participants, not passive patients. Making eye contact, in conversation, engaged. | Apple: subjects always empowered, never diminished |
| **Connection** | Show relationships — patient and family, patient and doctor, parent and child. Care is relational. | Core to "Care Circle" narrative |
| **Life moments** | Healthcare doesn't happen in a vacuum. Show the kitchen table, the car ride home, the phone call to family. | Airbnb approach: the story is in the setting |

**Photography Don'ts:**
- No hospital/clinical settings as primary imagery (unless showing the contrast with home)
- No stock-style "diverse group smiling at camera"
- No isolated screens/devices without human context
- No sad/worried patients as the hero image — show empowerment, not anxiety
- No over-saturated or over-filtered processing

## 3. Design System Governance

### The Consistency Principle (Apple/Bob Baxley via Lenny's Newsletter)

> "Consistency breeds familiarity. Familiarity breeds usability."

Every touchpoint should feel unmistakably Ditto within 2 seconds. This means:

**Cross-Touchpoint Checklist:**

| Touchpoint | Navy present? | Typography correct? | Photography style? | Tone match? |
|------------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| App | | | | |
| Website | | | | |
| Email | | | | |
| Social | | | | |
| Pitch deck | | | | |
| App Store listing | | | | |
| Support/docs | | | | |

### When to Deviate vs. When to Hold

| Situation | Decision | Why |
|-----------|----------|-----|
| New feature needs a color not in palette | Hold. Work with existing palette. | Palette expansion is a brand decision, not a feature decision. |
| Marketing campaign wants a different font | Hold. Use Bricolage + Inter. | Type is identity. Changing it breaks recognition. |
| Photography doesn't exist for a context | Create a brief, commission a shoot. Don't use stock. | Stock undermines the entire editorial photography stance. |
| A partner/insurer co-brand asset | Adapt. Give them space, but Ditto's navy + type must be present. | Co-branding requires flexibility, not surrender. |
| Product experiment needs UI flexibility | Adapt within system. New component patterns OK, new colors/type NOT. | Innovation happens in layout and interaction, not identity. |

### Design Tenets (not just principles)

Bob Baxley (Apple/Pinterest, via Lenny's Newsletter) distinguishes **tenets** from platitudes. Tenets are decision-making tools — they tell you what to do when two good options conflict.

| Tenet | What it resolves |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Photography over illustration** for external brand | When choosing between an illustration or photo for a campaign, choose photo. Illustration lives in-product. |
| **Calm over clever** | When a design is innovative but adds cognitive load, choose the calmer option. |
| **Warm over cool** | When a neutral option exists alongside a warmer one, choose warm. Gold > gray. |
| **Fewer elements, more impact** | When in doubt, remove. Don't add a badge, label, and icon when one will do. |
| **The patient is the subject** | In any composition with both product UI and people, the person should be visually dominant. |

## 4. Visual Audit Checklist

Run this against any visual asset before it ships.

| # | Check | Pass/Fail |
|---|-------|-----------|
| 1 | **2-second test**: Would someone recognize this as Ditto without the logo? | |
| 2 | **Navy anchor**: Is navy present and doing structural work? | |
| 3 | **Typography**: Bricolage for display, Inter for body, weight 500+? | |
| 4 | **Color discipline**: Max 3 brand colors (excluding neutrals)? | |
| 5 | **Photography**: Real people, warm light, film quality, dignity? | |
| 6 | **Calm test**: Does this reduce visual stress or add to it? | |
| 7 | **Inevitability test**: Could anything be removed? | |
| 8 | **Consistency**: Matches other Ditto touchpoints? | |
| 9 | **Accessibility**: WCAG AA contrast, touch targets 44px+, readable at scale? | |
| 10 | **Emotional**: What does the viewer FEEL? (Should be: calm, cared for, confident) | |

## 5. Briefing Templates

### Photography Brief

When commissioning photography or directing a shoot:

```
Subject: [Who/what]
Setting: [Where — prefer home, everyday, non-clinical]
Mood: [Warm, connected, empowered — never clinical or anxious]
Light: Natural, warm, golden hour or soft interior
Grain: Light film grain in post — analog feel
Composition: Subject dominant, environment contextual
Key moment: [The specific human moment to capture]
What to avoid: Stock energy, clinical settings, isolated devices
Reference: [Link to 2-3 mood images]
```

### Design Review Brief

When reviewing designs for brand alignment:

```
Asset: [What's being reviewed]
Touchpoint: [Where will this live?]
Audience: [Who sees it?]
Run visual audit checklist (section 4)
Flag: Any deviation from brand system with reasoning
Recommend: Approve / Revise with specifics / Reject with alternative
```

## 6. Multi-Perspective Design Review

When reviewing visual work, evaluate from these perspectives:

| Perspective | Key Questions |
|---|---|
| **Jony Ive** (Simplicity) | Is it inevitable? Can anything be removed? Does form express function honestly? |
| **Paula Scher** (Typography) | Is the type working hard enough? Is there visual courage? Distinctive point of view? |
| **Headspace** (Calm) | Does this reduce cognitive load? Adequate breathing room? Soothing or jarring? |
| **DixonBaxi/Heidi** (Humanity) | Is the human the center? Does the tech serve the person, not the other way around? |
| **Patient** | Would I trust this in a moment of health anxiety? Does it feel credible AND warm? |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Using thin/light font weights (300/400) | Weight 500+ for patient-facing. Thin feels uncertain — opposite of what patients need. |
| More than 3 brand colors in one composition | Discipline. Navy + one accent + neutrals. Let photography carry emotion. |
| Clinical/hospital imagery as hero | Show the kitchen table, the car ride home, the phone call. Healthcare in life context. |
| Stock photo "diverse group smiling at camera" | Real people, real settings, editorial warmth, film grain. Commission or don't use. |
| Device screenshots without human context | The person is the subject. Product UI is supporting actor. |
| Adding illustration to external brand materials | Illustration is in-product only. External = photography. This is a tenet. |
| Trying to make Inter decorative | Inter is the workhorse. Bricolage Grotesque is the personality. Don't cross them. |
