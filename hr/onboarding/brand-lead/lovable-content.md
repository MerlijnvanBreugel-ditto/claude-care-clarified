## Welcome to Ditto, April

My grandmother Riet used to say it plainly: "I remember less than half of what the doctor said." So she brought one of her kids to every visit. Not because she couldn't cope, but because healthcare handed her information in the worst possible way and expected her to carry it home alone.

We exist to fix this. Ditto is building the consumer layer European healthcare has been missing: the right information, in the right format, at the right time, for you and the people who care about you. Today that's an app that clarifies every healthcare encounter and connects your people through the Care Circle. Tomorrow it's the platform you navigate your whole health journey with.

"Clarity when it matters most". That is the ambition and a crazy purposeful one. We hired you to help us realize this.

## Why this role exists

Healthcare has never had a great consumer brand. Not in Europe, not anywhere. Clinical portals feel clinical. Wellness apps feel shallow. Nobody has built something that earns trust when the stakes are highest and still feels warm, human, and beautiful.

That's the job. We believe you shouldn't have to choose between clinical-and-trusted and warm-and-shallow. The brand should be human without being soft, innovative without losing credibility, personal enough to sit with someone during a hard diagnosis, and universal enough to work across cultures and generations. It has to earn trust in living rooms, waiting rooms, and boardrooms alike. Right now our why is pretty solid and our how we show up is wide open.

The current brand has grown quite organically, not strategically and definitely not consistently. We feel that, as a consumer brand, there is a massive upside in doing this better in the Netherlands but also abroad. Given the sensitivity of healthcare, finding the right balance can make all the difference. We also notice that towards consumers, differentiation becomes ever harder and being noticed too, so really solid, recognizable design, tone of voice, and positioning are both needed to build trust with individuals but also to reach your growth objectives.

## Why we hired YOU

Before starting the whole recruitment process, we consulted with various brand leads and experts that also worked for the likes of Miro and Airbnb. They said it looks like Ditto was looking for a unicorn that can take multiple roles at once. And it really feels like, with you, we might just have found one. There are a few things that really stood out that make us very excited and happy to have you on board:

1. **Terribly broad skill set:** both on the strategic side and on the hands-on side, from digital design to marketing campaign design, motion, video, and probably there are many things that we don't even know of that you are incredibly good at.
2. **Thinker:** you are clearly a really intelligent, fast thinker. We seen you switch from concrete actions and initiatives to a high-level, almost philosophical contemplation on a brand level. I think spanning that whole breadth is really key for this role and being able to make high-level concepts tangible and impactful.
3. **Instant culture match:** we are a small, closed-knit team running after a bold mission. That means that every new hire should bring something novel, but also fit into the culture and team seamlessly. Everyone you talked to was convinced right away.
4. **Brand vision:** during the case, we noticed that you thought about brand at a level we don't. After sharp questions and one hour of work, alread bringing forth a solid and consistent brand vision that could just be where Ditto could be heading. We want to be humbled and confronted by your expertise and we trust that you'll do that in the most British way.

### [reading · week1] The product, our users, and the market

*Source: `readings/ditto-product-and-users.md`*

**What the product does**

Ditto turns a medical conversation into something a person can actually understand and act on. Record or capture an encounter, and the app produces a patient-friendly summary: what was said, what it means, and what to do next. Then the Care Circle lets you share it with the people around you, with the explanation adapted to each of them.

**Two ideas matter most:**

- **Understanding, not just access.** Plenty of products give you your records. We make them make sense.
- **Health is never solo.** Up to 40% of summaries get shared. The Care Circle is the part Big Tech health AI doesn't build, because they design for individuals. That's our moat.

**Who we serve**

We've made a deliberate focus call: critical health moments, where clarity and the care circle matter most.

- **Primary, short term:** oncology.
- **Also in scope:** neurodegenerative conditions (e.g. MS) and sudden severe diagnoses (e.g. cardiology).
- **The through-line:** moments that are complex, emotional, and shared.

This supersedes an older "older people" framing. The person navigating cancer treatment, and their family, deserve better design than someone booking a holiday home gets.

**Traction (public)**

- Launched July 2025.
- Hit #1 in the App Store.
- Backed by major players Heal, Rubio Impact Ventures, Chris Oomen
- Around 100,000 users installed the app

These are the proof points we use in public. They tell you the bet is landing.

### [reading · week1] Where the brand stands today (Merlijn's take)

*Source: `readings/brand-current-state.md`*

Treat this as one founder's brain dump, not gospel. It's how I see the brand today. Ask Tobias or Bart and you'd get a different cut, and that's exactly the point: almost nothing here is formalized. Most of it lives implicitly, in our heads and our habits, not in a document. Pulling it into one shared, explicit picture is a big part of your first month.

**What Ditto is today**

We clarify every healthcare encounter and connect the people around you through the Care Circle. Capture, clarify, connect. The public line is "the consumer layer European healthcare has been missing: the right info, in the right format, at the right time, for you and your loved ones." That story works for investors. It is not yet a brand a normal user would recognize.

**The three that matter most, high level**

- **Purpose (why we exist).** No one should go through healthcare in the dark. This is the most settled thing we have: felt, consistent, public. The wording can still be sharpened.
- **Positioning (what we are).** The "consumer layer" line is real but vague. We've never pinned down what it actually includes. Care management? A care companion? A coordination platform? A personal health intelligence? Today that's more an open company-strategy question than a settled brand position, and it's an investor narrative, not a crisp user-facing line.
- **Personality (who we are).** Barely defined. Beyond "positive, uplifting, present," we haven't articulated a character. The only place personality really lives right now is our voice and tone.

**Where it's solid, drafty, or contradictory**

- **Mission (solid, public):** "We clarify and connect care journeys for you and your loved ones."
- **Tagline (two lines, not one):** "Care. Clarified." is the mark we use, but the website leads with "Clarity when it matters most." We haven't decided which is the tagline.
- **Audience (decided in strategy, not in the brand):** we've chosen to focus on critical health moments, oncology first, also MS and sudden severe diagnoses like cardiology. The brand hasn't caught up. It still speaks broadly, not to those moments or the families in them.
- **Functional, not emotional:** today we mostly explain the function, clarity and what the app does. What it feels like to be the patient or the family on a hard day is underdeveloped.
- **Values (drafty):** four convictions exist, the patient unlocks everything; health is never solo; simplicity is the feature; earn trust, don't extract it. Decent direction, very rough wording.
- **Brand promise:** we don't really have one. There's a draft sentence on file, but nothing ratified or used.
- **Voice and principles (the strong bit):** the most developed part of the brand, and the principles are actually okay, clarity with intelligence (clear isn't simple), human and warm, confident not corporate, trustworthy by default. Still a draft, not a locked standard.
- **Localization (EN/NL):** the intent is "adapt, don't translate," but calling it aligned would be overselling. It's implicit at best.
- **Visual (inconsistent):** the website is moving into photography; the app hardly uses it. The system grew organically and isn't where we want it. Colour and type aren't formalized.
- **The app as brand:** never audited. Nobody has asked whether the product itself feels like Ditto.

**Your calls (don't solve these in week one, start forming a view)**

- A crisp, user-facing positioning, distinct from the investor narrative.
- A defined personality, and whether the register should be calmer for someone just diagnosed.
- The one ownable visual idea (the flexible "o" is the front-runner).
- The visual system: photography, illustration's role in the app, colour, type.
- Whether the app feels like Ditto.

There's no finalized brandbook for the current direction. Synthesizing all of this, from the decks, the files, and what's only in people's heads, into one explicit picture is itself a month-one deliverable. Producing the first real brand direction off the back of it is likely your first big win.

You can read the latest but heavily outdated brand positioning here: <https://dittocare.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/DG/pages/10682380/Brand+positioning>
