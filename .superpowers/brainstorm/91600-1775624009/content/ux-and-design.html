Design Principles: Magic, Not AI

The interface must feel like "Ditto understands me," never "I'm using artificial intelligence." Every principle serves the 50+ audience who didn't grow up with technology.

1. Intelligence is Invisible

The AI never announces itself. No "AI-generated," no robot icons, no loading spinners that say "AI is thinking." The output just appears, like a knowledgeable friend wrote it.

✓ "Ditto prepared this for you" / "Based on your conversation"
✗ "Our AI analyzed your transcript" / "Generated by Claude"

2. Suggest, Don't Demand

The brain offers. The patient chooses. Suggestion chips are invitations, not obligations. Nothing happens without the patient's tap. No auto-actions on medical data.

✓ Suggestion chips below content / Confirmation cards for voice input
✗ Auto-updating medications without confirmation / Chat-style "How can I help?"

3. Source Everything

Every piece of intelligence traces back to a real source. "Dr. van der Berg mentioned..." not "According to our analysis." Trust comes from attribution, not capability.

✓ "From your appointment on April 4" / Doctor name + date on every answer
✗ Floating medical facts with no origin / Generic health information

4. Big, Clear, Calm

Minimum 16pt body text. High contrast. Generous whitespace. One primary action per screen. No information density that requires scrolling to understand the gist. Calm colors, no urgency red unless truly urgent.

✓ Card-based layouts / Single-column / Large tap targets (min 44pt)
✗ Dense tables / Small toggles / Multi-column layouts / Hamburger menus

5. Progressive Depth

Surface layer is simple. Detail is one tap away. A summary is scannable in 15 seconds. Tap a suggestion to go deeper. Tap a profile item to see its history. Never force depth on someone who just wants the headline.

✓ Summary → tap for explanation → tap for source quote
✗ Everything visible at once / Expandable sections with no visual cue

6. Familiar Patterns Only

Use patterns the audience already knows: lists, cards, buttons. No gestures beyond tap and scroll. No swipe-to-dismiss, no long-press, no drag. If Corrie (81) can't figure it out in 3 seconds, redesign it.

✓ Tap buttons / Scroll lists / Back arrow / Clear labels
✗ Swipe actions / Floating action buttons / Gesture-driven navigation

Trust Signals for 65+ Users

🏥

Doctor Attribution

Every summary, answer, and suggestion names the doctor and date. The brain's authority comes from the doctor's words, not its own.

🔒

Your Data, Your Control

Profile shows exactly what Ditto knows. Patient can correct anything. "Ditto got this from your conversation" vs. "Ditto thinks this."

⚕️

Medical Humility

Every answer ends with "For medical advice, contact your care team." The brain never says "you should" about treatment. It explains, it doesn't prescribe.

✅

Confirmation Before Action

Voice input shows what it heard before updating. Document extraction asks "Is this right?" Profile changes show what changed and why.

📍

Source Transparency

Every profile item shows its source: "From your appointment on April 4" or "You told Ditto on March 15." Nothing appears from nowhere.

🤝

Consistent Tone

Warm, clear, never clinical. The same "voice" across all surfaces. Like a trusted friend who happens to have been at all your appointments.

Risk Register

Risk
Impact & Likelihood
Mitigation
Detection
Medical hallucination
Patient acts on fabricated medical information. Catastrophic if it happens even once. Likelihood: medium without strict grounding.
Strict grounding rules in every worker prompt. Source attribution required. "Cannot answer from available data" fallback. Eval harness checks grounding on every prompt change.
Automated grounding check in CI. Manual review of 50 random answers/week post-launch.
Silent wrong profile update
Brain updates medication incorrectly without patient knowing. Could cause medication error. Likelihood: low with confirmation flow.
All voice/document extractions require user confirmation. Low-confidence extractions flagged, never auto-applied. user_verified fields survive future extraction.
Audit log review. Automated check: no profile mutation without corresponding confirmation event.
Extraction accuracy degrades
Dutch medical terminology, code-switching, specialist abbreviations cause extraction failures. Likelihood: high for edge cases.
Eval harness with diverse Dutch medical conversations. Extraction worker tested against labeled ground truth. Confidence scoring: low-confidence items require confirmation.
Weekly eval run. Accuracy dashboard per entity type (medications, conditions, care team).
Model API downtime/latency
Claude/Anthropic API goes down, summaries and answers stop working. Likelihood: low but unpredictable.
Graceful degradation: recording saved, summary generated when API recovers. Queue-based retry. User sees "Processing your recording, we'll notify you when ready."
Health check endpoint. Alert on >5s average latency or >1% error rate.
Prompt regression
Prompt improvement for one worker degrades another. Or model update changes behavior. Likelihood: medium, increases with prompt complexity.
Prompt regression eval suite run on every change. Version-controlled prompts. Model pinning (specific model version, not "latest").
CI pipeline: prompt change triggers full eval suite. No merge without passing.
Over-reliance on brain
Patient trusts Ditto answers more than they should. Stops asking doctor questions. Likelihood: low in MVP, increases at scale.
Medical disclaimer on every answer. "Contact your care team" language. Brain explicitly says "I don't know" when context is insufficient. Never uses imperative language about treatment.
Track: ratio of "could not answer" responses. If near 0%, grounding rules may be too loose.
Scope creep in 3-month window
Feature additions push timeline beyond 12 weeks. Likelihood: high in any ambitious project.
Slice boundaries are hard cuts. Gate criteria are pass/fail. Scope that doesn't fit a slice moves to post-MVP backlog. Product owner enforces at gates.
Weekly scope review against sprint plan. Any unplanned work tracked as "unplanned" in sprint.
3-month timeline too aggressive
Infrastructure or prompt engineering takes longer than estimated. Likelihood: medium.
Slices are priority-ordered. If timeline slips, Slice 3 features (prep, sharing adaptation) are the cut candidates. Slice 1 + 2 = a viable product alone.
Gate reviews include timeline assessment. If Gate 1 is late, re-scope Slice 3.
