# Patient Population Sizing: Netherlands & Europe

Strategic sizing for Ditto Care's addressable market across major medical life moments.
Last updated: May 2026. For internal planning only.

---

## Methodology notes

- **Incidence** = new diagnoses per year
- **Prevalence** = total people living with the condition at a given point in time
- **Addressable** = patients actively seeing a specialist (relevant for in-consultation moments)
- Europe figures refer to EU-27 unless noted; "Western Europe" estimates used where EU-27 data is unavailable
- Numbers flagged with ⚠️ are estimates or extrapolations from older data — treat as indicative, not precise
- Sources named inline; no publication year older than 2017 used unless no more recent data exists

## 1. Cancer

### Netherlands

| Cancer type | Annual incidence (NL) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| All cancers combined | ~132,000 (GLOBOCAN 2022) | Excludes basal cell carcinoma; IKNL projects 156,000/yr by 2032 |
| Colorectal | ~17,700 | Largest single type by volume |
| Breast | ~15,900 | |
| Lung | ~14,800 | |
| Prostate | ~12,000 | |
| Melanoma (skin) | ~8,000 | Non-melanoma skin cancers add ~60,000+ cases but are excluded from standard registries |

**Prevalence (NL):** The IKNL tracks 5-year prevalence (living patients diagnosed within the past 5 years) and complete prevalence (all living survivors). Based on IKNL 2020 data and rising incidence trends, approximately **700,000–800,000** people in the Netherlands are living with or after a cancer diagnosis (complete prevalence). The 5-year prevalent population (those most likely still in active follow-up) is estimated at **~450,000–500,000**. IKNL has noted the total will cross 1,000,000 within the next several years.

Source: GLOBOCAN 2022 / IARC; IKNL Netherlands Cancer Registry (NCR); OECD EU Country Cancer Profile Netherlands 2025

### Europe (EU-27)

| Cancer type | Annual incidence (EU-27) | Source / Year |
|---|---|---|
| All cancers combined | ~2.74 million | ECIS / GLOBOCAN 2022 |
| Breast | ~558,000 | GLOBOCAN 2022 |
| Colorectal | ~538,000 | GLOBOCAN 2022 |
| Lung | ~514,000 | GLOBOCAN 2022 |
| Prostate | ~473,000 | GLOBOCAN 2022 |
| Melanoma | ⚠️ ~130,000–160,000 | Estimated; not separately detailed in GLOBOCAN EU summary |

**Prevalence (EU-27):** Estimated **22 million** people alive in the EU had ever received a cancer diagnosis (ECIS/EUROCARE-6, 2020 data). The 5-year prevalent population — those most likely still in specialist care — is estimated at **~9–10 million**.

Source: ECIS / European Cancer Information System (JRC); EUROCARE-6; OECD Health at a Glance Europe 2024

### Addressability

In-active-follow-up population (NL): Roughly **60–70%** of 5-year prevalent patients are in structured specialist follow-up at any given time — derived from Dutch cancer registry data showing ~55% in follow-up, ~22% in active treatment. This means **~300,000–350,000** cancer patients in the Netherlands are in regular specialist contact at any point in a year.

Follow-up duration varies significantly: breast cancer surveillance typically runs 5 years post-treatment with 2–4 clinic visits per year in year 1, declining thereafter; colorectal cancer patients attend at least biannually for 5 years; melanoma patients follow a structured surveillance schedule based on stage.

## 2. Neurodegenerative diseases

### Alzheimer's disease / Dementia

#### Netherlands

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Total prevalence (population estimate) | ~310,000 (2025) | Alzheimer Europe / Alzheimer Nederland |
| Total prevalence (RIVM estimate) | ~278,000–290,000 (2023) | RIVM / vzinfo.nl |
| Annual incidence (new GP diagnoses) | ~23,300 (2024) | Nivel / vzinfo.nl |
| Under-diagnosed gap | Significant — only ~278,000 known to care vs ~310,000 estimated | vzinfo.nl |

Alzheimer's accounts for ~60–70% of all dementia cases. Vascular dementia is the second most common subtype.

Projected growth: 390,000 by 2030; 520,000 by 2040 (Alzheimer Nederland). The Netherlands has a National Dementia Strategy that includes improved diagnosis pathways.

#### Europe (EU-27)

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Total prevalence (EU-27) | ~7.9–9.1 million | Alzheimer Europe 2022–2025 |
| Total prevalence (EU-27 + non-EU) | ~12 million | Alzheimer Europe 2025 |
| Annual incidence (EU-27) | ⚠️ ~500,000–700,000 | Estimated from age-specific incidence rates applied to EU population |

Source: Alzheimer Europe; RIVM / Nivel (NL-specific); vzinfo.nl

#### Addressability

Most dementia patients are managed initially by neurologists or geriatricians, then transitioned to primary care. The specialist contact window is most intense in the 1–2 years post-diagnosis (for assessment, medication titration, care planning). ⚠️ Rough estimate: 30–40% of the prevalent population is in active specialist follow-up at any given time, with the remainder managed in primary care or residential care. For NL: ~90,000–120,000 patients in specialist contact.

### Parkinson's disease

#### Netherlands

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Total prevalence | ~63,000 | Utrecht University / Radboudumc 2025 (Lancet Regional Health) |
| Annual incidence | ~3,700–3,800 new cases/yr | Same study, 2017–2022 average |

Source: Lancet Regional Health — Europe (Radboudumc / Utrecht University, 2025); based on 2017–2022 nationwide data

#### Europe (EU-27 / Western Europe)

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Total prevalence (Europe) | ⚠️ ~1.2–1.5 million | Estimated from EUROPARKINSON data + GBD 2021; no single current EU-27 figure |
| Annual incidence (Europe) | ⚠️ ~60,000–80,000 | Estimated from 100–130/100,000 age-standardized incidence applied to EU age structure |

Global prevalence was 11.77 million in 2021 (GBD 2021). Europe accounts for roughly 10–13% of global burden. Prevalence is rising due to aging populations; cases projected to double globally by 2050.

Source: Global Burden of Disease 2021; Parkinson's Europe; Lancet (2023) epidemiology review

#### Addressability

Parkinson's patients typically remain in neurologist care throughout the disease course — this is one of the highest-specialist-contact neurodegenerative conditions. Estimated 70–80% of NL Parkinson's patients are in active specialist follow-up at any given time. NL addressable: ~44,000–50,000 patients.

### ALS / Motor Neuron Disease (MND)

#### Netherlands

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Annual incidence | ~450–470 new cases/yr | Derived: 2.64/100,000 × 17.9M population |
| Point prevalence | ~1,700 | Derived: 9.5/100,000 (NL study) × 17.9M |
| Annual MND deaths | ~466 (2017) | Neurology journal, NL MND Center data |

Source: Neurology (2021) — "Incidence, Prevalence, and Geographical Clustering of Motor Neuron Disease in the Netherlands"; data from Netherlands MND Center and Patient Association of Neuromuscular Diseases

ALS is a rapidly progressive disease. Median survival from symptom onset is 2–4 years, which is why prevalence is low relative to incidence. The Netherlands has relatively high ALS rates compared to Southern Europe.

#### Europe (EU-27)

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Prevalence rate | 7–9 per 100,000 (Europe) | Meta-analysis; higher than global average |
| Total EU-27 prevalence | ⚠️ ~32,000–40,000 | Estimated: 8/100,000 × ~450M EU population |
| Annual incidence (EU-27) | ⚠️ ~12,000–15,000 | Estimated: 2.6–3.1/100,000 × EU population |

Source: Global Prevalence and Incidence of ALS — PMC systematic review; German Society of Neurology guideline (2023)

#### Addressability

ALS is 100% specialist-managed. All diagnosed patients are under neurologist care. NL addressable = ~1,700 (full prevalent population).

### Multiple Sclerosis (MS)

#### Netherlands

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Total prevalence | ~36,000+ | Amsterdam UMC / Nivel / Rijnstate, 2024 study |
| Prevalence rate | 210.4 per 100,000 (2019) | MS and Related Disorders journal |
| Annual incidence | ~1,270/yr | 7.1/100,000 × 17.9M population |

Previous estimates were ~18,000 (1 in 1,000); updated 2024 research doubles this to 36,000+.

Source: "Prevalence and incidence of multiple sclerosis in the Netherlands" — Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders (2024); Amsterdam UMC press release

#### Europe (EU-27 / Western Europe)

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Total prevalence (Europe, broader) | ⚠️ ~700,000–900,000 | Estimated; global figure is ~2.8M (Atlas of MS 2020); Europe is ~25–30% |
| Prevalence rate range | 43–376 per 100,000 (varies by country) | Lancet Regional Health Europe 2024 |
| Incidence | Varies; higher in northern countries (up to 11/100,000 in Denmark) | ECTRIMS |

MS prevalence is highest in Northern Europe and drops significantly in Southern Europe. The Netherlands sits at the mid-to-high range.

Source: Lancet Regional Health — Europe (2024); Atlas of MS; ECTRIMS

#### Addressability

MS patients are under neurologist care throughout their lives, with monitoring frequency tied to disease activity and treatment. Virtually all diagnosed patients have regular specialist contact. NL addressable: ~36,000 (full prevalent population, ongoing).

## 3. Severe cardiac events

### Acute myocardial infarction (STEMI / NSTEMI)

#### Netherlands

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Annual MI hospitalizations | ~69,200 (2024) | Nivel / vzinfo.nl (men: 48,400; women: 20,800) |
| Historical comparison | ~29,700 hospitalizations in 2012 | Netherlands Heart Journal 2014 |
| Coronary heart disease prevalence | ~792,700 | vzinfo.nl 2024 |

The large jump from 2012 to 2024 figures partly reflects expanded diagnostic criteria for NSTEMI (high-sensitivity troponin), which captures more events previously not classified as MI.

Source: vzinfo.nl (Nivel Primary Care Registrations 2024); Netherlands Heart Journal (2014 historical); hartenvaatcijfers.nl

#### Europe

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Annual MI incidence | ⚠️ ~1.5–2.0 million/yr | Estimated; no single current EU-27 MI figure; extrapolated from country-level rates |
| STEMI incidence (per country) | 64–66/100,000 (Scandinavia) | SWEDEHEART 2015; NORMI 2015 |
| NSTEMI incidence (per country) | 132–184/100,000 (Scandinavia) | SWEDEHEART/NORMI 2015 |

Source: European Heart Journal; IntechOpen "Epidemiology of Myocardial Infarction"; ESC Guidelines 2023 ACS

#### Addressability

Post-MI patients typically enter a 12-month intensive follow-up protocol with cardiologist visits at 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months post-event, plus cardiac rehabilitation. After year 1, most transition to primary care unless complications arise. Annual "active specialist follow-up" population for MI in NL: ⚠️ **~60,000–80,000** (patients within 1 year of event + those with ongoing complications).

#### Netherlands

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Annual incidence (new cases) | ~45,800/yr | vzinfo.nl 2024 |

Source: vzinfo.nl (Nivel Primary Care Registrations 2024); RIVM; JMIR Cardio (2022)

#### Europe

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Total prevalence (ESC countries, 51 nations) | ≥15 million | ESC-HFA Position Paper 2022 |
| Total prevalence (EU-27 estimate) | ⚠️ ~8–10 million | Estimated from 1–2% prevalence rate × EU adult population |
| Annual incidence rate | ~3–5 per 1,000 person-years | ESC / Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine 2026 |
| Prevalence rate | 1,937 per 100,000 (median) | ESC-HFA 2022 |

Source: ESC-HFA Position Paper (Rosano et al., 2022); European Heart Journal

#### Addressability

Heart failure is one of the highest-follow-up cardiac conditions. ESC guidelines recommend structured heart failure clinic follow-up with regular echocardiography, medication titration, and nurse-specialist support. In the Netherlands, integrated heart failure care programs have been established. Estimated 60–70% of prevalent patients have regular specialist contact. NL addressable: **~150,000–180,000**.

### Major cardiac surgery

#### Netherlands

| Metric | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Annual open-heart surgeries | ~14,900–15,000 | Netherlands Heart Registration (2017 data) |
| Breakdown | ~65% CABG, ~35% valve surgery | NL Cardiac Surgery Database |
| Annual CABG | ⚠️ ~8,000–10,000 | Historical NL data |

Note: 2022+ figures not found in public registries; 2017 data is the most recent publicly available comprehensive figure. Volume is unlikely to have changed dramatically.

#### Addressability

Post-cardiac surgery patients are in intensive specialist follow-up for at least 12 months. Cardiac rehabilitation is standard (12 weeks) immediately post-surgery, followed by regular cardiologist/cardiothoracic surgery follow-up. All ~15,000 annual surgical patients are in active specialist care in the year following their procedure.

## 4. Total addressable "life moments" population — Netherlands

This is the estimated number of people in active specialist follow-up for one of these serious conditions in a given year. It represents Ditto Care's maximum reachable population through the in-consultation channel.

| Condition | Estimated in specialist follow-up (NL, annual) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cancer (treatment + active surveillance) | 300,000–350,000 | Medium (derived from IKNL prevalence + follow-up literature) |
| Dementia / Alzheimer's | 90,000–120,000 | Low-medium (specialist contact phase) |
| Parkinson's disease | 44,000–50,000 | Medium-high (high specialist retention) |
| ALS / MND | ~1,700 | High (100% specialist-managed) |
| Multiple sclerosis | ~36,000 | High (lifelong specialist care) |
| Acute MI (within 12 months) | 60,000–80,000 | Medium |
| Heart failure (active management) | 150,000–180,000 | Medium |
| Major cardiac surgery (within 12 months) | ~15,000 | High |
| **Total (deduplicated estimate)** | **~700,000–800,000** | Low-medium |

**Deduplication note:** Some patients carry multiple diagnoses (e.g., heart failure complicating post-MI, cancer patient with cardiac comorbidity). The deduplicated total is estimated at ~700,000–800,000 unique individuals per year in the Netherlands in active specialist follow-up for at least one of these conditions.

For context: the Netherlands has ~17.9 million people. This addressable population represents roughly **4–4.5% of the total population** — which aligns with reasonable estimates for the prevalence of serious condition active management at any one time.

## Key uncertainties and gaps

1. **Cancer follow-up duration varies widely by type** — post-curative surveillance for some cancers (e.g., early-stage breast) is intense in years 1–3 but becomes GP-managed thereafter. The 300,000–350,000 figure assumes a broad definition of "active specialist contact."
2. **Cardiac surgery 2022+ data unavailable publicly** — the 2017 NHR figure is used. Actual 2024 volume may be slightly higher or lower due to TAVI uptake replacing open valve surgery in older patients.
3. **MI incidence jump (29,700 in 2012 → 69,200 in 2024)** warrants scrutiny. This likely reflects high-sensitivity troponin adoption expanding the NSTEMI category significantly, not a genuine doubling of cardiac events. Interpret the 69,200 figure as a broad ACS hospitalization count rather than strictly "heart attacks" in the colloquial sense.
4. **Dementia specialist contact** is the hardest to estimate. Many patients are managed primarily by GPs after initial specialist assessment. The 90,000–120,000 figure may overstate ongoing specialist contact.
5. **MS prevalence revision** (from ~18,000 to 36,000+) is recent (2024). Older market sizing documents using the prior estimate are obsolete.
6. **No European cardiac surgery aggregate** was found in public sources. EU-level MI/heart failure incidence estimates are extrapolations.
