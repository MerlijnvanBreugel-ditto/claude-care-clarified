## Shreyas Doshi: "This is high-velocity PM, not high-leverage PM"

The master synthesis is 12,000 words and the Care Wiki spec is another 5,000. Impressive output. But the LNO test is brutal: which paragraphs move the needle (Leverage), which are table stakes (Neutral), and which are vanity (Overhead)?

My read: maybe 20% of the synthesis is leverage. The contrarian reframe (caregiver as underserved customer), the pipeline plugin architecture, the demotion of similarity — those are leverage. They change what the team does Monday.

The other 80% is well-written Overhead masquerading as rigor. A 45-JTBD list isn't a strategy; it's a surface area inflation. The 22 wow moments isn't a product; it's a mood board. The founder panel commentary isn't insight; it's committee-writing dressed as wisdom.

The hardest question this synthesis doesn't answer: **what is the ONE thing you are doing this quarter that, if you did nothing else, you'd still be in business?** A high-leverage PM can answer that in one sentence. This doc cannot.

**Prescription**: Strip the document to one page. One positioning line. Three JTBDs (not 45). Two wow moments (not 22). One architectural decision. Everything else moves to an appendix called "context." If the one-pager doesn't convince a cold reader in 90 seconds, the vision isn't yet a vision — it's still notes.

## Marty Cagan: "You wrote a feature roadmap. You called it a vision."

The vocabulary is product. The substance is project management.

A real vision is outcome-anchored: what will be true about our users that isn't true today? The master synthesis instead has **capability tiers** (Tier 0 foundation, Tier 1 magic, Tier 2 compounding...). Tiers are deliverables. Deliverables are not outcomes. "We shipped the plugin framework" is not something a patient cares about.

Worse: the doc is prescriptive. It tells the team: here are the 45 jobs, here are the 22 moments, here are the 8+ pipelines in Tier 1, here are the workers to build. This is the anti-pattern I spent twenty years warning about. **You do not empower product teams by handing them a spec. You empower them by stating the problem, the constraints, and the outcome, then trusting them to discover the solution.**

Where's the discovery? What did the team learn this month from patients that surprised them? What hypothesis got falsified? The synthesis makes zero reference to any evidence from real users beyond the existing 35K. It cites founder personas more than it cites customer observation.

**Prescription**: Rewrite Section 5 from "Capability Milestone Map" to "Outcome Staircase." Each tier expressed as: *"By achieving this, X% of [specific user segment] will be able to [specific job] that they can't today."* If you can't state the outcome in user-observable terms, you don't have a vision — you have a build plan.

Then, for each outcome, name the discovery work that will validate it. Not eval suites. Real users. This month.

## April Dunford: "You have a brand line, not a position."

"Ditto is the open assistant for health — specialized, not general-purpose." Lovely. Aspirational. Does not survive five minutes with a VP of Product at Anthropic.

Here's the positioning test. Fill in these four blanks:

> For [specific user segment] who [specific need], Ditto is the [market category] that [unique value]. Unlike [competitive alternative], Ditto [key differentiator].

Try it with the synthesis's framing:

> For [patients and their care circles] who [need shared understanding across a serious health journey], Ditto is the [???] that [understands the patient and their people together]. Unlike [ChatGPT Health / Claude for Healthcare / Copilot Health / Apple Health], Ditto [adapts information per audience via the care graph].

What's the market category? "Open assistant for health" is a metaphor, not a category. "Care intelligence platform" is the business-case doc's answer, but it's abstract. You haven't chosen a category. You have been *hoping one emerges*.

And the competitive alternative framing is weak. You list Big Tech competitors and say they're building individual health AI. True. But the REAL competitive alternative isn't another app — it's **WhatsApp with a shared family doc**. That's what a 55-year-old Dutch family does today. A good health insurer portal with a notes app. A Google Doc. The daughter calling her mother at 9 PM. Those are the alternatives you beat, or you don't exist.

The 45-JTBD list is a positioning disaster. It signals to the market: we are many things to many people. The best category creators are obsessively narrow at first. Tableau was for business analysts, not everyone. Figma was for designers, not anyone who makes files. What's Ditto's equivalent narrow beachhead? "Cancer patients + their primary caregiver"? "Elderly patients in the Netherlands with chronic conditions"? Pick.

**Prescription**: Run a Dunford positioning workshop this week. Force the team to fill in the four blanks with ONE set of answers. Then stress-test the uniqueness with real prospects in the beachhead segment, not virtual founders.

## Teresa Torres: "Zero real users were harmed in the making of this document."

I read the synthesis. I read the wiki spec. I read the magic vision. I read the founder panels.

You know what I didn't find? **One sentence starting with "When we interviewed [patient name/code], they told us..."**

Not one. The entire 17,000+ words rests on:
1. Three virtual founder panels (simulations of dead or unavailable people)
2. Existing specs that reference five synthetic patient personas (fictional)
3. A Granola conversation between two people on the Ditto team
4. A Karpathy gist written by a person who does not use the product

The closest you get to a user is Merlijn's own diagnosis that the summary "feels useful but not magical." Which is *his* hypothesis. It could be wrong. Patients might adore the current summary and would hate the adapted-share feature for reasons we haven't heard. You don't know. You haven't asked.

The virtual-founders move is confirmation bias wearing a Halloween costume. You cast the personas to critique your own plan. They critique your plan within the bounds you set. They never produce something you hadn't thought of. This is why panel sessions where everyone agrees are dangerous — they feel like insight, they're theater.

Here's the continuous discovery test: what's the last piece of evidence that changed your mind about something meaningful in this plan? If you can't name one from the last four weeks, you are not doing discovery. You are rationalizing.

**Prescription**: Before committing to ANY of Tier 1, interview five real patients. Not synthetic. Not user-researcher-agent-summaries. Real. Weekly cadence. Build an opportunity solution tree from what you hear. The outcome (reduce the burden on cancer patients' families, say) sits at the top. The opportunities (specific unmet needs you've heard, verbatim) are the middle layer. The solutions (your pipelines) are the bottom. Right now you have a tree made entirely of solutions. That's not a tree; that's a feature list on its side.

## Brian Balfour: "You're building the pipe before testing the retention."

Four Fits framework. Let's walk it.

**Market/Product fit**: The synthesis asserts this is true. Ditto has 35,000 users and some insurer traction. But where's the retention data? What percentage of users who start a journey on Ditto are still active 90 days later? 12 months? If the answer is "we haven't published the retention curve," you have not proven market/product fit. You have proven there's a curious user base.

**Product/Channel fit**: Your distribution plan is (a) Menzis B2B2C, (b) organic circle invites, (c) eventually other insurers. The 0.14% daily circle sharing rate is not a viral loop — it's a trickle. A product/channel fit that works on circle invites needs at least 1-2% daily activation per patient. You're off by 10x. The synthesis hand-waves this by saying "Circle Member Dashboard will fix it." Maybe. Untested.

**Channel/Model fit**: You're B2C premium at €7.99 AND B2B2C insurer PMPM. Those are fundamentally different motions. B2C premium requires low CAC and monthly retention. B2B2C insurer requires enterprise sales cycles and minimal engagement requirements. A 10-person team cannot do both well. Pick.

**Model/Market fit**: Premium at €7.99 × 5-10% conversion on 100K users gets you ~€500K ARR. That's a feature, not a business. To hit the €50M ARR Series-A-worthy number, you need either 10x the users at current conversion, or 10x the price, or insurer deals that print. The math in the business case assumes all three happen. Each is an independent bet. Compound probability of all three: low.

**The killer question the synthesis avoids**: What's the retention curve for current Ditto users? Because if it's below the "PMF line" (I'd want to see >25% of cohort still weekly-active at month 6 for a consumer health product in this category), you are building Tier 2, 3, 4 on a leaky bucket.

**Prescription**: Before Tier 0 starts, publish the retention curve. If it's below PMF, the real Tier 0 is fixing retention, not building plugin frameworks.

## Gibson Biddle: "Delight is done on paper. H and M are wishes."

DHM test. Delight / Hard-to-copy / Margin-enhancing.

**Delight**: The 22 wow moments are a strong delight thesis. If even 6 of them land as described, Ditto has a delight product. I'll grant you this.

**Hard to copy**: You claim the care graph is the moat. I don't yet believe it. Here's why:
- Apple or Google could ship a "Share your Apple Health record with family members, adapted per relationship" feature in 6 months. They have the distribution, the wearable context, and the model capability.
- The care graph is hard to copy ONLY IF it's dense. Ditto's graph is currently 1 patient per 1.4 circle members (50 invitations/day on 35K users, and most of those don't activate). That is not a network. A network starts at 3-5 active circle members per patient. You are 4x short.
- The "longitudinal memory" claim is also untested as a moat. Apple has longitudinal wearable data for millions of users already. OpenAI just bought Torch to unify medical records. The memory moat is hard to copy only if Ditto's memory is BETTER, not just present.

So H is a hypothesis, not a proof.

**Margin-enhancing**: Your unit economics model shows great margins at scale. But scale is the question. The synthesis doesn't stress-test CAC for B2C premium. €7.99/month at 12-month retention = €95 LTV. CAC via paid acquisition in health-tech consumer is typically €30-80 in the EU. LTV:CAC of 1.2-3x is not margin-enhancing — it's marginally-breakeven. The real margin story requires the care graph to drive organic acquisition at near-zero CAC. Which is the same bet as H. So if H fails, M fails.

**The proxy-metric problem**: Netflix obsessed over "% of members who rated 5+ shows in month 1" as a PMF proxy. What's Ditto's? The synthesis names suggestion tap rate (20%+) and profile accuracy (90%+). Those are feature metrics, not PMF proxies. A PMF proxy predicts retention. Tap rate probably doesn't.

**Prescription**: Two deliverables the synthesis is missing.
1. A defined PMF proxy metric, tested against the current retention curve. If tap rate doesn't predict retention, it's a vanity metric. Find one that does.
2. A CAC:LTV forecast for B2C that stress-tests the no-care-graph-network case. If the business doesn't work without the network effect, you need to be honest that the whole bet is on the network effect.

## What All Six Would Agree On (the consensus critique)

Independently, six different product thinkers converge on the same six points:

1. **The document privileges comprehensiveness over focus.** 45 JTBDs and 22 moments is a liability, not an asset. A tight product does three things so well you'd fight for it.
2. **Real user evidence is absent.** The founder panels are intellectually entertaining and strategically dangerous. They produce a synthesis that feels rigorous without being grounded.
3. **Positioning is warm but not operational.** You cannot walk into a Menzis negotiation or a Series A pitch with "open assistant for health." You need a category name and a competitive alternative statement that you're willing to defend.
4. **Retention and unit economics are hand-waved.** The business case projects revenue but does not anchor the plan to the current retention curve. If retention is weak, everything else is a fantasy on top.
5. **The care graph moat is assumed, not proven.** The one piece of data we have (0.14% daily invitation rate) actively contradicts the thesis. This should be the bet at the top of the strategy, not an assumption in the middle.
6. **The plan is prescriptive where it should be exploratory.** Committing to 45 pipelines, 22 moments, and 5 tiers as if the right answer is known. The right answer is not known. It should be discovered.

## The Five Hardest Calls the Synthesis Refuses to Make

These are the decisions the document flinches on. A sharper strategy names them.

| # | The flinch | What a sharper version would say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Patient vs. caregiver as the primary customer | "We are building for the daughter managing her mother's cancer care. The patient is the data source, not the buyer. All design, growth, and monetization is caregiver-first for 18 months." |
| 2 | The 45-JTBD list | "We will master three JTBDs this year. Everything else is on the table for 2027+. Here are the three: [X, Y, Z]. If we nail these, everything after compounds. If we don't, the 45 list was never the right surface." |
| 3 | The B2C vs. B2B2C bet | "We are a B2B2C company. B2C premium is a vestigial revenue line we will end if it distracts the team. Menzis-model wins or we don't ship." (Or the reverse: "We are B2C. The Menzis deal is marketing, not revenue.") |
| 4 | The retention truth | "Our 90-day retention is X%. It's below / at / above PMF line. If below, all Tier 0+ work stops until we raise this to Y%." |
| 5 | The summary's future | "Our current summary is 80% of what a native iOS feature will do in 12 months. We are explicitly killing the summary as a standalone product line. Everything is downstream of the share, the memory, and the graph." |

A sharper strategy is a list of such commitments, signed and dated. Not a synthesis of possibilities.

## What This Critique Is NOT Saying

Let's be precise. The panel is sharp, not dismissive.

- The synthesis is NOT wrong. It contains strong thinking. It is the right *shape* of document for a founder's internal brainstorm.
- The architectural contributions (plugin framework, wiki projection, three/two-mode retrieval, review loop) are real and correct. This is leverage work.
- The emotional targeting in the magic moments is genuinely good PM taste.
- The threat analysis is honest about commoditization.

The critique is: this is a **first draft of a vision, presented as if it were a committed strategy.** Strategy demands choice. This document has not yet chosen. The flinches (the five above) are the choices the team is avoiding. Making those choices will produce a document that's 30% shorter and 3x sharper.

## The Panel's Joint Prescription

One page. Literally one page.

On that page:
- One positioning line (Dunford-compliant)
- One beachhead user segment (named)
- Three JTBDs (the 42 others go to an appendix)
- One PMF proxy metric (with the current number and the target)
- One retention commitment (with the trigger point that stops everything else)
- One core moat thesis (specific, with the proof point that will validate or kill it in 90 days)
- Three open questions that will be decided this quarter, with decision-makers named

If the one page is impossible to write, the longer documents aren't a vision. They're notes masquerading as strategy.

Do the one-pager. Then come back and either:
(a) Slim the master synthesis to align with the one-pager, or
(b) Accept that the synthesis is a brainstorming artifact (not a strategy), file it as such, and move on.
