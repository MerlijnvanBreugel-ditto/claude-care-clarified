Executive Summary

What we have: Rich qualitative research (15+ interviews, Lyssna panels, Typeform feedback). NotebookLM reveals significantly more evidence than local docs captured — cardiology IS mentioned, neurodegenerative has 3 sub-conditions, caregivers split into 4 distinct types with 5 proxy usage examples, and chronic patients report specific AI accuracy issues. Several capabilities are more mature than documentation suggests: PDF upload, multi-language translation (TR/AR/EN), section-level sharing controls, and basic medication management are all shipped.

What's missing: No formal persona documents exist anywhere. Disease-specific user profiles are absent but evidence exists for all segments in the research — it was just never structured. The gap is in strategy and product positioning, not in raw research. Four caregiver sub-types need distinct product treatment.

Key insight: The research is richer than anyone realized — NotebookLM surfaced evidence that was buried in interviews but never extracted into strategy docs. The real gap is structuring what we already know into actionable personas and translating that into differentiated product experiences.

Cancer / Severe Care Patient
High info load, frequent specialist visits, life-changing decisions
Focus Area: Oncology Partially Documented Moderate Coverage
Core problems (validated by NotebookLM):
  • Overwhelmed by volume and complexity across many specialists — long consultations hard to recall
  • Granular sharing needs: want to hide vulnerable details (audio, organ removal, stoma) from broader circle
  • Chemo medication schedules: "half an hour to puzzle out what to take next" from paper lists
  • Emotional exhaustion from "dumb questions" — need to inform people without repeating themselves
  • AYA patients (young adults) want a "summary of summaries" — consolidated journey overview after treatment ends
Jobs to be Done:
1. Understand what my doctors tell me across multiple specialties
2. Keep my family informed without repeating traumatic information
3. Control exactly what vulnerable information I share with whom
4. Make sense of my chemo medication schedule
5. Have one place where my entire care journey lives (especially post-treatment for AYA)

Awareness stage: Most Aware — they know they need help managing care complexity

Evidence (NotebookLM): Extensive — cancer mentioned across most sources. Breast cancer chemo patients need help with complex med schedules. Granular privacy: "want to hide organ removal, stoma issues from broader circle." Emotional boundaries: "suffer from being asked too many dumb or exhausting questions." AYA-specific: "need a consolidated overview of their entire healthcare journey so they can archive it without sifting through 50 separate appointment notes." Father records on behalf of adult son with cancer.

What Ditto covers today: Recording, AI summaries, section-level sharing controls, basic medication, Care Circle v1
What's in pipeline: CC v2, voice health check-in, who-to-share-with controls, advanced medication
What's missing: No "summary of summaries" (journey consolidation), no chemo schedule parser, no disease-specific privacy presets, no peer connection (lotgenoten), no treatment timeline visualization

Pregnancy Patient (+ Partner)
Positive journey, high eagerness to share, frequent checkups
Focus Area: Pregnancy Partially Documented Partial Coverage
Core problems:
  • Many checkups with lots of new information each time
  • Want to share pregnancy updates with extended family (95% share naturally)
  • Complicated pregnancies create anxiety and information overload
  • Partner often can't attend all appointments
  • Want visual milestones (echo photos) alongside medical information
Jobs to be Done:
1. Remember what the midwife/OB said at each checkup
2. Share pregnancy updates with family without repeating
3. Keep track of measurements, test results, and milestones
4. Inform my partner about appointments they missed
5. Understand medical terminology in my own language

Awareness stage: Problem Aware — know info gets lost, but may not know there's a solution

Evidence (NotebookLM + Confluence): Named as #1 focus disease area. High information load across many appointments (e.g., 20-week ultrasound). 95% share pregnancy updates; over 50% share with 6+ people — highest sharing intent of any segment. Marketing segments defined (25-38, Instagram). BIGB-56 mentions pregnancy echo photos.

What Ditto covers today: Recording, AI summaries, basic sharing
What's in pipeline: CC v2 (good fit for family sharing), photo previews (BIGB-56)
What's missing: No pregnancy-specific journey template, no milestone tracking, no partner-specific experience, no midwife integration, no trimester-aware content. This is named a focus area but has no tailored product experience.

Neurodegenerative Disease Patient
Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, MS — memory loss is the core challenge
Focus Area: Neurodegeneration Major Gap Weak Coverage
Core problems:
  • Progressive memory loss makes recalling medical info impossible
  • Caregiver (partner/child) often becomes the primary information manager
  • Need a "single source of truth" that loved ones can always reference
  • Disease progression means increasing dependency on others for app usage
  • Want social circle to understand the basics of their condition
Jobs to be Done:
1. Have a record of what was discussed because I will forget
2. Let my family access my care information when I can't manage it myself
3. Review and process summaries at my own pace (slower information processing)
4. Have an emergency profile that explains my condition to others
5. Maintain dignity and independence as long as possible

Awareness stage: Solution Aware — know they need memory support, may not know Ditto exists

Evidence (NotebookLM): Richer than local docs suggested. Dementia: User's mother has dementia, "cannot understand the care process herself" — family needs to manage on her behalf. MS: User with MS for 24 years needs "organized, to-the-point visit reports" for long-term care tracking. Brain injury: User says "information processing is slower" — specifically needs to review summaries at own pace because they "cannot easily retain information on the spot." Also: Parkinson's patient wanting "one place where friends can find the basics about my disease."

What Ditto covers today: Recording, AI summaries (general)
What's in pipeline: Emergency info (BIGB-98), profile page (CC v2)
What's missing: No caregiver-as-primary-user flow, no progressive access handover, no cognitive accessibility features (low-stimulus UI, simplified output), no "review at own pace" mode. This is arguably Ditto's most natural use case (memory loss → memory tool) but has zero dedicated investment.

Cardiology Patient
Heart conditions, cardiac rehab, ongoing monitoring with medication-heavy regimens
Evidence Exists (NotebookLM) No Tailored Features Generic Coverage
Core problems (from NotebookLM research):
  • Stressful consultations — recording provides "peace of mind" during cardiac visits
  • Need to understand lab results: blood tests, blood pressure, cholesterol numbers
  • Strict medication timing — "a few hours' delay every day significantly reduces effectiveness"
  • AI medication errors are dangerous: heart/vascular meds misinterpreted as psychiatric drugs
  • Remote sharing with siblings/relatives who can't attend appointments
Jobs to be Done:
1. Understand what my cardiologist told me and what the numbers mean
2. Keep my medication schedule precisely — timing is critical
3. Share updates with family members who can't attend
4. Track blood pressure, cholesterol, and other metrics over time
5. Understand medication interactions (cardiac patients often on 5+ meds)

Awareness stage: Problem Aware — know care is complex, may not know Ditto exists

Evidence (NotebookLM): Cardiology IS mentioned across research — heart clinics, cardiologist visits, blood pressure monitoring. One user's heart/vascular medications were misinterpreted by AI as psychiatric drugs. Medication timing quote: "Certain medications must be taken neatly every 24 hours... if you vary that by a few hours every day, it has much less effect." Peace of mind during stressful consultations specifically mentioned.

What Ditto covers today: Recording, AI summaries, basic medication feature, translations
What's in pipeline: Advanced medication management, health integrations (Apple Health for heart rate/BP)
What's missing: No cardiology-specific content, no metric tracking/visualization, no medication timing precision. AI medication accuracy is critical — misidentifying cardiac meds has real safety implications.

Chronic Disease Patient
Diabetes, COPD, autoimmune, rheumatology — ongoing management, not crisis
Not Specifically Documented Gap Generic Coverage
Core problems (validated by NotebookLM):
  • AI accuracy issue: COPD/ACOS patient reports AI focused on chronic background instead of actual medication changes discussed
  • Long COVID: cognitive overload — need low-stimulus UI, calm colors, highly simplified text
  • Diabetes patients ask if app can explain their disease-specific documents
  • Multiple providers with fragmented info — patients maintain their own Excel spreadsheets
  • Disease fatigue — "want to stop being a patient" resonates strongly here
Jobs to be Done:
1. Track my condition over time without it being a burden
2. Have a clear overview across all my care providers
3. Manage medications across multiple conditions (some maintain Excel sheets)
4. Get AI summaries that focus on what changed THIS visit, not my chronic background
5. Keep my medical history in one place for new providers

Awareness stage: Problem Aware — managing complexity daily, seeking tools

Evidence (NotebookLM): Richer than local docs. COPD/ACOS: AI "focused too heavily on chronic ACOS background rather than the actual medication changes discussed during that specific visit." Long COVID: patients need "low-stimulus interfaces, calm colors, and highly simplified text" due to cognitive overload. Diabetes: user asks "can I use this to explain documents related to diabetes?" ADHD: "I would like extra tips on how to remember my medicines." Multiple patients manage medications in Excel because they see multiple specialists.

What Ditto covers today: Recording, AI summaries, PDF upload, basic medication management, multi-language translations
What's in pipeline: Voice health check-in (BIGB-62), advanced medication features, health integrations (Apple Health), event labeling
What's missing: No chronic-disease positioning (Ditto appears as "appointment tool," not "daily companion"), AI doesn't prioritize "what changed" over background, no cognitive accessibility mode (Long COVID), no condition-specific tracking, no symptom diary
🌍
Language Barrier Patient
Refugees, immigrants, non-native speakers struggling with medical Dutch/English
Named, Partially Served Research Gap Partial Coverage
Core problems (validated by NotebookLM):
  • Healthcare providers report patients need A1/A2/B1 reading levels — current B1 may still be too complex
  • Non-native speakers and lower-educated patients: specific need to toggle language output down
  • Cultural differences in healthcare navigation (unfamiliar with Dutch system)
  • Often rely on family members (including children) as interpreters
  • Reverse use case: Dutch patients abroad want foreign-language consultations translated back to Dutch
Jobs to be Done:
1. Understand what my doctor said in my own language
2. Navigate the Dutch healthcare system
3. Explain my symptoms accurately to providers
4. Share medical information with family who may speak a different language
5. Keep records I can reference and share with interpreters

Awareness stage: Unaware — may not know tools like Ditto exist

Evidence (NotebookLM): Named in brand positioning. Healthcare provider specifically requests toggle for A1/A2/B1 reading levels: "many of their patients are non-native speakers or have lower education levels." Reverse use case discovered: Dutch patients abroad in Spanish-speaking countries want foreign → Dutch translation. Farsi, Polish, German in development.

What Ditto covers today: Summaries translated to Turkish, Arabic, and English. Transcription supports Dutch and English conversations. PDF upload and document scanning.
What's in pipeline: Farsi, Polish, German translations in development
What's missing: Adjustable reading level (A1/A2 option below current B1), culturally adapted content, healthcare system navigation help, dedicated onboarding for non-native speakers, transcription beyond Dutch/English, reverse translation (foreign consultations → Dutch). Translation exists but the experience isn't tailored to this segment's actual literacy needs.

Self-Manager (25-55)
Digitally savvy adults navigating their own diagnoses and treatments
Documented (B2C Playbook) Good Coverage
Core problems:
  • "You deserve to understand what's happening to you"
  • Want to be informed and empowered, not passive
  • Expect digital-first experiences (frustrated by paper processes)
  • Need fast, clear information they can act on
Jobs to be Done:
1. Understand my diagnosis and treatment options clearly
2. Be prepared for every appointment
3. Have my medical history organized and accessible
4. Make informed decisions about my care

Awareness stage: Problem/Solution Aware — actively looking for tools

Evidence: B2C Content Strategy segment. AYA interview. This is the segment that Ditto's core product best serves today — recording, AI summaries, document scanning.

What Ditto covers today: Core product fits well — record, summarize, store, share, PDF upload, multi-language translations, section-level sharing controls, basic medication management
What's in pipeline: Onboarding-to-aha, voice profile, Ditto Chat, who-to-share-with controls
What's missing: Activation gap (90% drop-off). The product is good once they use it — getting them to first value is the problem.

Elderly / Low-Digital Patient
55+, comprehension difficulties, often set up by children
Well Documented Partial Coverage
Core problems:
  • Difficulty understanding medical conditions and instructions
  • Digital insecurity — feel lost on platforms
  • Fear of authority — afraid to ask doctors for permission to record
  • Children often manage the app on their behalf (proxy usage)
  • Long text-heavy summaries overwhelm — need simpler output
Jobs to be Done:
1. Remember what the doctor said (or have my child help me)
2. Not have to repeat information to family members
3. Use the app without complex navigation
4. Feel confident in appointments without asking "dumb questions"

Awareness stage: Unaware to Problem Aware — often introduced by children

Evidence: Strong. Proxy recording documented (children/neighbors). Fear of authority documented. Cognitive load issues documented (Long Covid patients, seniors). Facebook marketing segment (55+). QR-to-record (BIGB-104) directly addresses "can't find the app."

What Ditto covers today: Voice recording (no typing needed), AI simplification
What's in pipeline: QR-to-record, voice-first onboarding, narration improvements
What's missing: No simplified UI mode, no assisted setup flow, no visual/infographic summaries, no audio summary playback
🫂
Primary Caregiver
Partner, adult child managing a loved one's care — 5 million in NL
Well Documented CC v2 Target Partial Coverage
Core problems (validated by NotebookLM — 4 distinct sub-types identified):
  • Partners: Need "shared memory" — attend appointments together, review later. Often act as tech proxy for less digital spouse
  • Adult children: Rotate attending visits across siblings — need shared agenda ("who is going to which appointment") + centralized summaries
  • Friends/neighbors/volunteers: Need practical updates (when is chemo?) to offer help. Term "dierbaren" feels wrong — prefer "naasten"
  • Professional caregivers: Need specific clinical data (e.g., lung function tests). Suffer from "massive administrative gap" between institutions
  • 5 explicit proxy usage examples: father records for son, husband installs on wife's phone, child preps for parent, volunteer manages everything for elderly neighbor
Jobs to be Done:
1. Get accurate, timely information about my loved one's care
2. Coordinate care logistics with other family members (sibling rotation)
3. Set up and manage the app for someone who can't do it themselves
4. Have one place where everything about their care lives
5. Filter information by role — partners get full detail, friends get high-level

Awareness stage: Problem Aware — actively frustrated by information fragmentation

Evidence (NotebookLM): Strongest evidence across all segments (10+ of 15 sources). Four distinct caregiver sub-types identified with different needs. 5 explicit proxy usage quotes: "I record this for my son" (father), "app installed unasked on wife's phone" (husband), "I arranged everything for her... I was the first point of contact" (volunteer for elderly neighbor). Professional caregivers (physiotherapists, home care) need objective clinical data, not emotional context. Terminology friction: "dierbaren" rejected for volunteer/neighbor relationships.

What Ditto covers today: Care Circle v1, section-level sharing
What's in pipeline: CC v2 (invitation cards, reactions, profiles, enhanced sharing), Care Circle Pulse, who-to-share-with controls
What's missing: Proxy account management (set up on behalf of someone else), logistics coordination (shared agenda, task rotation), role-based information filtering, professional caregiver access to clinical data, caregiver-specific dashboard

Extended Network (Friends, Neighbors, Colleagues)
Want to show they care, don't need clinical detail
Partially Documented Weak Coverage
Core problems:
  • Don't know what to say or how to help
  • Want to show support without being intrusive
  • Need brief, filtered updates — not clinical details
  • "Safe to call" — want to know when it's okay to reach out
Jobs to be Done:
1. Stay informed about my friend/colleague's health at a high level
2. Show I care without demanding emotional labor from the patient
3. Offer practical help (cooking, transport) when it's actually needed
4. Know when it's appropriate to reach out

Awareness stage: Unaware — this persona doesn't seek tools; they arrive through invitations

Evidence: Lyssna research: "send hearts, flowers without expecting reply" (8 mentions). "Show when I'm available to talk" (6 mentions). Information tiering (inner vs. outer circle) requested across interviews.

What Ditto covers today: Very little — basic CC v1 sharing
What's in pipeline: CC v2 reactions (emoji), support cards, help offers, profile page
What's missing: Follower-first activation (see summary without installing), lightweight web experience

Gap Analysis
Prioritized gaps ranked by estimated impact and alignment with Ditto's mission
1. No disease-specific user profiles exist anywhere
Three focus areas (pregnancy, neuro, oncology) are named in strategy but have zero dedicated research, zero tailored features, and zero differentiated content. Cardiology and chronic disease aren't even named. Ditto treats all patients identically — a cancer patient and a pregnant woman get the same experience.
Impact: Critical Effort: Medium (research-first) Suggestion: Disease-specific interview rounds → persona cards → feature differentiation roadmap
2. Language barrier patients: translations exist, but no dedicated experience
Turkish, Arabic, and English translations are shipped — this is more than most competitors offer. But the experience isn't tailored: no dedicated onboarding for non-native speakers, no culturally adapted content, no healthcare system navigation, transcription limited to Dutch + English. The gap is in UX and positioning, not core capability.
Impact: High (large underserved population in NL) Effort: Low-Medium (UX + content, not technical) Suggestion: Test targeted onboarding variant for non-native speakers → measure activation difference
3. Neurodegenerative is the most natural use case but least invested
Memory loss patients are the quintessential "need to remember what the doctor said" users. Yet Ditto has no proxy management, no progressive access handover, no cognitive accessibility features, and no caregiver-as-primary-user flow. One anecdote total.
Impact: High (natural product-market fit) Effort: Medium Suggestion: Interview 5 Parkinson's/Alzheimer's caregivers → validate proxy management need
4. No formal persona documents — research is feature-centric, not user-centric
Segments are defined across 11+ Confluence pages with different granularity and framing. No single persona card or reference exists. This makes it impossible to prioritize features by user impact or to onboard new team members on who we serve.
Impact: High (foundational) Effort: Low Suggestion: Turn this canvas into the canonical persona reference → publish to Confluence
5. Cardiology: evidence exists, strategy doesn't
NotebookLM reveals cardiac patients ARE using Ditto — heart clinics, blood pressure monitoring, medication timing concerns. AI has misidentified cardiac meds as psychiatric drugs. But cardiology isn't named in strategy, has no tailored features, and no positioning.
Impact: Medium-High Effort: Low (validate with targeted interviews) Suggestion: Add to focus areas alongside oncology/pregnancy/neuro, or explicitly deprioritize
5b. AI summary accuracy for chronic patients — "focused on background, not changes"
COPD/ACOS patient reports AI focused on chronic background rather than the medication changes actually discussed. Cardiac meds misidentified as psychiatric drugs. For chronic patients who see specialists regularly, summaries that rehash known conditions instead of highlighting what changed are actively unhelpful.
Impact: High (trust + utility) Effort: Medium (AI prompt engineering) Suggestion: "What changed this visit" prioritization in AI summary logic
6. Caregiver logistics coordination — validated need, no solution
"Who is going with mom? Who is cooking?" — explicit quotes from primary caregivers. CC v2 addresses emotional sharing but not practical coordination. This is what differentiates a "sharing tool" from a "care network."
Impact: Medium Effort: Medium Suggestion: Could be Later roadmap — evaluate after CC v2 adoption data
7. Pregnancy: named #1 focus area, treated generically
Strategy document lists pregnancy as the most well-defined focus area. 95% sharing intent is the highest of any segment. Yet zero pregnancy-specific features exist or are in pipeline. No milestone tracking, no partner experience, no trimester awareness.
Impact: Medium (high adoption potential) Effort: Low-Medium Suggestion: Pregnancy journey template + partner sharing optimization as quick wins
8. Quantitative research is thin — most insights from 6 super user interviews
Findings largely come from 6 super users + 34-person Lyssna panel. No large-scale survey, no behavioral data analysis supporting persona definitions. Awareness stage mapping is explicitly flagged as "hypothesis needing validation."
Impact: Medium (confidence gap) Effort: Low Suggestion: Mixpanel behavioral segmentation + 200-person survey to validate personas

Persona Framework Used

Each persona follows a standardized structure combining three established frameworks:

  • Jobs-to-be-Done (Christensen) — What is this person trying to accomplish? What progress are they seeking?
  • Five Stages of Awareness (Schwartz) — How aware are they of the problem and solution? This determines messaging and activation strategy.
  • Disease/Context Layer — What medical context shapes their needs? This is the Ditto-specific dimension that makes generic personas actionable.
