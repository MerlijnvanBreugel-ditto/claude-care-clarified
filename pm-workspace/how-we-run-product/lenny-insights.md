# Lenny & Friends: Insights for Ditto

> Curated frameworks from Lenny's Newsletter and podcast guests, filtered for relevance to Ditto's stage and context. Not a summary of everything Lenny ever said — only what's actionable for a 10-person healthcare startup with 35,000 users.

## Frameworks We Use

### Explore vs. Exploit (Brian Balfour, Reforge)

Featured on Lenny's Podcast. Core insight: the goal of testing many ideas is NOT to find many things that work. It's to find a small number of **leverage points** — areas with huge ceiling potential that compound over time.

**Applied to Ditto:** A feature area can be both Explore and Exploit at the same time. Care Circle is a good example:
- **Exploit**: Extending sharing with personal messages — proven mechanic, doubling down on what works
- **Explore**: Profile page with FAQ for patients — entirely new capability, unproven
- **Polish**: UX improvements, bug fixes, performance

The line is: does this extend a proven mechanic (Exploit) or introduce a capability that didn't exist before (Explore)? Polish is neither — it's making existing things smoother.

The discipline: don't prematurely exploit a tactic that merely works. Stay in Explore until you find a *leverage point*. But once you find one (like Care Circle sharing), aggressively exploit it while exploring adjacent capabilities under the same umbrella.

### The Racecar Growth Framework (Lenny Rachitsky)

Mental model: a product grows like a racecar. Components:
- **Engine** — the core growth loop (for Ditto: summary → share → invite → new user)
- **Turbo boosts** — one-time accelerants (Menzis partnership, PR, launches)
- **Lubricants** — reduce friction (onboarding optimization, UX improvements)
- **Fuel** — what powers the engine (content, users, data)

**Applied to Ditto:** Our engine is the Care Circle sharing loop. Menzis is a turbo boost. Onboarding improvements are lubricants. The risk: over-investing in turbo boosts (partnerships) while under-investing in the engine (making sharing so good it's irresistible).

Source: [Lenny's Newsletter — Racecar Growth Framework](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-racecar-growth-frameworkexpanded)

### North Star as Tiebreaker (Lenny Rachitsky)

> *"The best product teams I've worked with have a single metric that everyone can recite."*

Not just a KPI — a decision-making tool. When choosing between two features, the North Star breaks the tie.

**Applied to Ditto:** Our North Star is **Monthly Active Users (MAU)**. Every feature must connect to this — either directly (drives activation/retention) or indirectly (improves the experience that keeps users coming back). When debating two features, ask: which one moves MAU more?

### Problem-First, Not Solution-First (Lenny Rachitsky)

> *"Spend more time researching the problem space and less time brainstorming solutions."*

**Applied to Ditto:** With a small team, the temptation is to jump to building. The Idea Template and Discovery process exist to force problem-first thinking. If you can't articulate the pain in one sentence, you're not ready.

### Minimum Lovable Product, Not MVP (via Lenny's Newsletter)

Build the smallest thing that users *love*, not just the smallest thing that works. For a subset of users, the experience should be complete and delightful.

**Applied to Ditto:** Our summary experience is the MLP. It needs to be excellent for the primary segment (patients with specialists) before we expand. Half-built features for everyone are worse than a complete experience for someone.

## Principles from Lenny's Guests

### Marty Cagan — Discovery Risk

> *"The biggest risk in product isn't shipping slowly — it's shipping the wrong thing confidently."*

We use this to justify our evidence bars. Skipping discovery for Explore bets is the most expensive shortcut.

### Melissa Perri — The Power of No

> *"The most important product decision is what NOT to build."*

Our Go/No-Go gate exists because of this. Without an explicit "no," we build everything that gets proposed.

### Teresa Torres — Continuous Discovery

> *"Discovery shouldn't stop when you start building."*

We require weekly user touchpoints during Build, not just at kickoff.

### Shreyas Doshi — Mechanism Over Prediction

> *"Most hypotheses fail because they describe WHAT will happen but not WHY."*

Our hypothesis template requires the "because" — the mechanism. Without it, you can't tell whether failure was caused by a bad solution or a bad assumption.

### Gibson Biddle — Small Effort, High Delight

> *"A 2-day feature that makes users say 'wow' shouldn't wait behind a 2-month table-stakes project."*

Our Quick Filter (Delight x Effort matrix) captures this. Low effort + high delight = ship this week, don't wait for the next sprint.

## What Ditto Should Watch

Based on Lenny's recent observations:

1. **PM as bottleneck** — As engineering velocity increases (especially with AI-assisted coding), product management risks becoming the constraint. For a team our size: stay lean in process, rich in decision-making speed.

2. **Learning velocity > shipping velocity** — The fastest-growing companies don't have the best ideas. They have the fastest learning loops. Our Week 1/2 evaluation cycle is the mechanism for this.

3. **Value-first communication** — When communicating features, lead with customer outcomes ("now you can..."), not technical accomplishments ("we built..."). This applies to release notes, social, and investor updates.
