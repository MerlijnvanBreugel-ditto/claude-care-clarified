# Decision-Useful Perspectives, Not Echo or Contrarian Theater

**Date:** 2026-06-04
**Trigger:** Merlijn, while commissioning the Lenny's-guest perspectives doc for the long-term product vision.

## What he said

"You can always find a perspective that's opposing yours, or that is complementary, and with that it doesn't really help you to make better decisions. I'm not looking for opinions that agree with me, also not things that are always against me. I'm interested in which perspectives help me now to make better decisions on where to go next," like a real conversation that brings you more than you would have gotten alone.

## The rule

When assembling outside perspectives (panels, guest viewpoints, multi-perspective reviews, research voices):

1. **Anchor every voice to a live, named decision fork.** If it doesn't bear on an open decision, cut it.
2. **Selection test:** does this perspective change the default answer, or sharpen the criteria / kill conditions? If it only restates the current thinking or only negates it, it is decoration.
3. **In synthesis, separate convergence from nuggets.** Agreement across genuinely independent voices is signal. Nuggets must be specific, actionable, and non-obvious.
4. **One honest opposing-view section at the end** beats token disagreement scattered throughout.

## Why it matters

Panels and reviews are cheap to generate and easy to make feel rigorous. The failure mode is confirmation bias in costume (Torres' critique of our own virtual panels) or its mirror image, performative contrarianism. Both leave the decision exactly where it was.
