# Evidence-Regime Confusion (Pre-AI Graveyard ≠ AI-Native Verdict)

**Date:** 2026-06-05
**Trigger:** Merlijn's challenge on source 12: "Daily check-in with smart synthesis, context-aware Q&A, symptom tracking based on voice and automatic rather than manual. All seem completely new since AI. Anything pointing in that direction?"

## What went wrong

I concluded "episodic beats daily" from ten product autopsies, but every product in that graveyard was pre-AI: they assigned patients daily *work* (forms, timed tests, structured diaries) and gave *silence* back. The mechanics Merlijn named (proactive voice check-in, synthesis, grounded Q&A, automatic capture) did not exist in that evidence base, so the graveyard could not falsify them. My own campaign data even contained the counter-signals (94% voice check-in engagement, passive > active capture, Tucuvi >90%, 47.8 min/day memory-care companion) and I filed them under "what AI enables" instead of letting them challenge the headline conclusion.

## Why it happened

1. The graveyard narrative was coherent and satisfying; I let pattern strength substitute for pattern *scope*.
2. I did not date-stamp the evidence: "no daily product retained" silently became "daily products don't retain," when the honest version is "no pre-2024 manual daily product retained, and AI-native ones are too young to have 6-month data."
3. Absence of evidence (no published AI-companion retention) got blended into evidence of absence.

## Rules going forward

1. **Date-stamp the evidence regime.** When a capability shift (LLMs, voice latency, vision) lands mid-corpus, split every conclusion into pre-shift and post-shift columns before generalizing.
2. **Failures must share the mechanism, not just the category.** "Symptom tracking failed" is true only for the mechanism tested (manual entry). A new mechanism (voice, passive) inherits the category's demand evidence, not its failure verdict.
3. **Let your own counter-signals attack the headline.** If the same sweep contains findings that cut against the emerging conclusion, they belong in the bottom line as a tension, not in a later section as color.
4. **Distinguish "unproven" from "refuted" explicitly** in every verdict table; they demand opposite product strategies (experiment vs avoid).
