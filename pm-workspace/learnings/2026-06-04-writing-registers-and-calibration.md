# Writing Registers & Calibration

**Date:** 2026-06-04
**Trigger:** Merlijn's feedback while reading `sources/10-rethinking-the-core-action.md`. No rewrite yet; v2 comes after he finishes reading (backlog bl-008).

## What he flagged

1. **Jargon that sounds smart but says nothing.** "Trust artifact." "The periodic crystallization of a frequent, multi-player relationship." "A single-player utility with a rare external trigger." His words: "that's just a lot of blah blah."
2. **A factual overstatement hiding inside the jargon.** "Rare external trigger" is wrong for the core segment: cancer patients on active treatment can have appointments weekly. For them the summary already fires near-weekly and builds a real accumulating record. The frequency problem is real but narrower than the doc claims: it lives *between* visits, in lighter segments, and after treatment ends.
3. **The fake middle register.** Be either genuinely visionary (open, but plain language anyone gets on first read) or super concrete (numbers, names, dates, mechanisms). Abstract framework-speak that *sounds* precise is "fake."
4. **"Hard requirement" is not a hard requirement.** He used the phrase once; I engraved it in caps. Reality: the 20 interviews are the wise way to decide, not the only way. The decision could be made on existing research or judgment, just less well.
5. **Confidence miscalibrated.** The doc reads like settled strategy. It is ideation. Say so.

## Why it happened

1. I imported the external research's vocabulary wholesale ("crystallization," "single-player utility") instead of translating it into plain Ditto language.
2. Metaphor-compression feels precise but only decodes for someone who already knows the source framework.
3. I amplified Merlijn's own casual emphasis into doctrine. That is flattery of the brief, not rigor.
4. Strategy-doc register defaults to confident prose; the content was hypothesis.

## Rules going forward

1. **No decoder-ring phrases.** If a smart colleague outside product wouldn't get it on first read, rewrite it. Translate source vocabulary; never transplant it.
2. **Two registers only.** Visionary: plain, open, honest sketch ("imagine the place your family checks how dad is doing today"). Concrete: numbers, names, dates, mechanisms ("20 interviews, 2/day, screener ready Friday"). The abstract-precise middle gets cut.
3. **Stress-test borrowed claims against the beachhead before asserting.** A generalization that fails for on-treatment oncology fails, full stop.
4. **Modality discipline.** "Must" = real constraint (legal, regulatory, explicitly decided). "Should" = wise. "Could" = option. Don't promote casual emphasis (his or mine) into a gate.
5. **Label ideation as ideation.** Confidence gradient on claims: validated / evidenced elsewhere / hypothesis / gut. Hedged claims still need a plan to resolve (existing CLAUDE.md rule, now applied to strategy docs too).
