Leverage — marginal MAU per standardized lift, ranked

Standardized lifts: rates = +5 percentage points · inflow = +1,000 installs/mo · invites-per-inviter = +0.5. All held independently (one parameter at a time, from baseline). Inflow ranks highly because +1,000 installs is ~20% of baseline while +5pp on a 35% rate is ~14% relative. Volume is not a weak lever in pure math — it's only "weak" per unit effort, which requires effort estimates (v2).

Formula & assumptions

MAUactivated = Inflow × P(reg) × P(act|reg) × Lifetime × 1/(1 − K)
Lifetime = 1 + r₁ / (1 − r₂)
K = invite_rate × invites_per_inviter × P(invite→install) × P(invited→activate)
Calibration note: Formula predicts ~1,283 activated MAU at baseline; observed is ~1,230 (~4% overestimate). The model uses the tight Care Circle loop (K ≈ 0.012), not Porygon's K ≈ 0.09 which includes untracked broader sharing (WhatsApp forwards, organic word-of-mouth). That extra ~8pp of virality isn't represented in v1 — to model it, either nudge the loop sliders up or wait for v4 (multi-loop).

What this model is for

  • Primary: sensitivity narrative — show where MAU leverage lives so the team invests in the right stage.
  • Secondary: initiative prioritization — compare initiatives by MAU lift (costs/effort to be added in v2).
  • Not for: forecasting a specific future MAU number.

Key insights from running the model

  • Retention is two-tier, not geometric: r₁ = 54% (M0→M1 cliff), r₂ = 66% (stable core). Mathematically, r₂ has higher leverage than r₁ because 1/(1−r₂) compounds unboundedly. In practice r₁ has more headroom — fixing the cliff is easier than pushing stable-core to 80%+.
  • Under standardized +5pp / +1,000-install lifts, ranking is roughly: Inflow ≈ Activation > Registration > r₂ >> r₁ >> loop params. This honest answer contradicts the common "volume is waste" intuition — volume is a linear lever in the math, not a wasted one. The "is it wasted?" question only makes sense with effort/cost estimates (v2).
  • The Care Circle loop sliders barely move MAU individually (K ≈ 0.012). To matter, Care Circle v2 must lift multiple loop parameters together — toggle the Care Circle v2 initiative to see the combined effect.
  • Porygon's broader K-factor ≈ 0.09 includes untracked WhatsApp / word-of-mouth sharing. The v1 model deliberately excludes this — the sliders represent the measurable Care Circle loop only.

Baseline inputs (confirmed by Porygon)

  • Monthly installs: 5,000 (steady-state, blended iOS + Android + iPadOS)
  • Install → Registration (7d): 35%
  • Registration → Activation (14d, any of manual appt / recording / doc scan): 28%
  • r₁: 54% (M0→M1 survival, summary_created cohort)
  • r₂: 66% (stable-core monthly survival, computed from 33% M2 absolute / 54% M1)
  • Invite rate: 24% of newly activated users invite within 30d
  • Invites per inviter: 1.48 avg
  • Invite → Install: 15% (untracked — slider estimate, 10–20% plausible range)
  • Invited → Activation: 22% (~2× native; directional, measured on follower-accept data)

Known limitations (v1)

  • Non-activated viewers (447 / 27% of MAU) not modeled — these are registered+viewing but not activated, mostly Care Circle family members. Adds ~36% passive MAU in reality.
  • No per-channel split (Adjust attribution is 94% broken). "Quality of inflow" lives implicitly in the conversion rates.
  • No Life Moment segmentation. Single blended funnel.
  • Steady-state only — does not model transient dynamics, seasonality, or migration artifacts.
  • Only one loop (Care Circle). Retention loops (e.g. notifications, habit), partner loops (Menzis onboarding), and content loops not modeled.
