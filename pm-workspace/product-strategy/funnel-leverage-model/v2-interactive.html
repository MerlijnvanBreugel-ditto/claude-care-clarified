Standardized lifts: rates = +5 percentage points · inflow = +1,000 installs/mo · invites-per-inviter = +0.5. Output-composition sliders (passive multiplier, committed floor) excluded — these aren't "strategies," they're assumptions about model structure.

Formula & assumptions

// Three-component decomposition (v2, Direction B)
MAUtotal = MAUnew-cohort + MAUpassive + MAUcommitted

// New-cohort flow (steady state of current rates)
MAUnew-cohort = Inflow × P(reg) × P(act|reg) × Lnew × 1/(1 − K)
Lnew = 1 + r₁ / (1 − r₂)
K = invite_rate × invites_per_inviter × P(invite→install) × P(invited→activate)

// Passive MAU — Care Circle viewers, non-activated (scales with activated base)
MAUpassive = MAUnew-cohort × passive_multiplier

// Committed floor — long-tenure loyal users (not affected by scenario rates)
MAUcommitted = slider (user-supplied belief)

Calibration at baseline: new-cohort 1,283 + passive 462 (1,283 × 0.36) + committed 0 = 1,745. Observed total MAU ≈ 1,677 (~4% overestimate). The committed floor defaults to 0 because current Porygon data (retention through M3 only, Easter-distorted) doesn't yet support a principled estimate for long-tenure loyalty. Set it if you have a belief.

What this model does and doesn't do

  • Does: sensitivity analysis for MAU under hypothetical rate changes; initiative comparison via stacked parameter deltas; forces explicit assumptions about passive viewers and loyal-user protection.
  • Doesn't: forecast a specific MAU number at a specific date; simulate transient dynamics (it's steady-state); model heterogeneity across Life Moments; handle marketing channels (attribution is broken); produce causal ROI per initiative (that needs experiment binding — see research memo).

Why the three components?

  • New-cohort flow captures users activated recently whose retention follows the observed r₁/r₂ curve. Moves with the sliders.
  • Passive MAU captures non-activated viewers (mostly Care Circle family). Observed at 27% of activated. Scales with activated base via multiplier.
  • Committed floor captures users active 6+ months whose retention is likely much higher (~85–95% monthly) than the r₂ average suggests. The current retention data doesn't isolate them, so this is a belief slider. Raise it if you believe there's a protected base of loyal users who wouldn't churn from a scenario regression.

Known limitations in v2

  • Retention heterogeneity is approximated. The two-tier r₁/r₂ is blended across cohorts. The committed floor is a workaround, not a proper cohort-conditional retention curve. Fix in v4 (Markov chain or survival model).
  • Activation window is 14 days. Late activators (install → ever-activate at 90+ days) are not counted. Porygon doesn't yet provide this number.
  • Invite rate is one-off (measured within 30d of activation). If loyal users keep inviting in later months, the loop is stronger than K ≈ 0.012 implies.
  • No channel split. Adjust attribution is 94% broken. Quality-of-inflow lives implicitly in conversion rates.
  • Single blended segment. No Life Moment / cancer / neuro split.
  • Steady state. Doesn't show the arc from today's MAU to scenario MAU (which can take 12–24 months).

Scenario interpretation — reading the numbers correctly

  • If scenario = 900, it means: if these rates held forever with no loyal base, MAU would eventually settle at 900. Today's MAU would decay toward 900 over many months.
  • Set the Committed floor (e.g. 400) to protect loyal MAU from decay. Then scenario = 900 + loyal_floor = ~1,300 long-run.
  • For forecasting trajectory over time, you'd need a cohort-time-dynamic model (v4+). This is sensitivity analysis, not forecasting.
