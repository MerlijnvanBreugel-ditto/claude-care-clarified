# Brief K2 — EU AI-for-health adoption, WhatsApp-for-health, and Hello 24/7

Research analyst brief for Ditto Care. Every claim carries an inline source URL. Dates flagged throughout. Fact, inference, and estimate labeled separately. Compiled 2026-05-31.

---

## 1. Executive summary

**The European equivalent of the US "1 in 4 used AI for health" stat exists, and it is stronger in NL than the US.** The closest direct match is a Dutch survey: **~32% (nearly 1 in 3) of Dutch adults consulted an AI chatbot about health complaints in the past year** ([Univé / PanelWizard, Oct 2025](https://www.unive.nl/zorgverzekering/onderzoek-raadplegen-ai-bij-gezondheidsvragen)). The UK reads almost identically to the US headline: **24% (1 in 4) of UK patients use AI or social media for health guidance** ([Semble, Aug 2025 survey, published Nov 2025](https://www.semble.io/en/blog/dr-chatgpt-patients-turning-to-ai-for-health-information)). For context, the US benchmark is **1 in 4 (~66M) US adults, West Health–Gallup, fieldwork Oct–Dec 2025** ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/707789/americans-turning-supplement-healthcare-visits.aspx)). Bottom line: the behavior Ditto is betting on is already mainstream in its home market.

**WhatsApp-for-health evidence is real but lopsided.** WhatsApp's *reach* in NL is overwhelming (~88–93% of internet users — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1224092/leading-social-networks-netherlands/)), and there is solid academic evidence it is *already used* to share health/medical information with caregivers and families. But there is **no clean, large-N consumer survey quantifying "X% of families use WhatsApp to discuss health."** Most quantified studies are clinician-side or small disease-specific cohorts. Report this gap honestly: the platform ubiquity is proven; the specific "families coordinating care over chat" number is an inference, not a measured stat.

**Hello 24/7 is NOT a digital-GP / telehealth service.** The internal premise is wrong. Hello 24/7 is a **caregiver-coordination ("mantelzorg" / "Samenzorg") app** — shared calendar, tasks, family map, chat, file sharing — competing in *exactly* Ditto's family-care-circle space, not in teleconsultation ([hello247.nl](https://www.hello247.nl/en/)). The "€6–12/month" internal benchmark is roughly right in shape: **Premium starts at €5.83/month for a whole family group, billed annually, and is reimbursed 100% by Zilveren Kruis and Menzis** via supplementary insurance ([Hello Premium pricing](https://www.hello247.nl/en/hello-premium/)). This makes Hello 24/7 the most relevant Dutch competitor/comparable in this brief, and the insurer-reimbursement model is the strategically important finding.

## 2. European AI-for-health adoption

### The best EU equivalent stat (ranked by directness to the US "1 in 4")

| Rank | Stat | Country | Source & date | URL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 (best, NL)** | **~32% (nearly 1 in 3) of Dutch adults consulted an AI chatbot about health complaints in the past year** | NL | Univé, survey by PanelWizard, fieldwork 8–13 Oct 2025, n=1,042 | https://www.unive.nl/zorgverzekering/onderzoek-raadplegen-ai-bij-gezondheidsvragen |
| **2 (UK, mirrors US headline)** | **24% (1 in 4) of UK patients use AI or social media for health guidance**; 30% would consult AI/social rather than wait for a clinician | UK | Semble, "Beyond the search bar," n=2,000 UK patients, fieldwork Aug 2025, published 25 Nov 2025 | https://www.semble.io/en/blog/dr-chatgpt-patients-turning-to-ai-for-health-information |
| 3 (UK, mental health) | 37% (more than 1 in 3) of UK adults turn to AI chatbots for mental health/wellbeing support | UK | Mental Health UK | https://mentalhealth-uk.org/help-and-information/health-and-wellbeing/artificial-intelligence-and-mental-health/ |
| 4 (UK, motivations) | Among UK adults using chatbots for medical advice: convenience 46%, curiosity 45%, unsure if serious enough for GP 39%, NHS wait times 25% | UK | King's College London, n>2,000 adults | https://www.kcl.ac.uk/policy-institute/assets/use-of-ai-in-healthcare.pdf |

**US baseline (for direct comparison):** 1 in 4 US adults (~66M) have used AI tools/chatbots for physical or mental healthcare information or advice. West Health–Gallup Center on Healthcare in America, Gallup Panel study, fieldwork **27 Oct–22 Dec 2025**, n=5,660 adults, released **April 2026**. https://news.gallup.com/poll/707789/americans-turning-supplement-healthcare-visits.aspx

### Why the Dutch number is the headline for Ditto

- **Fact:** The Univé survey measures the *same behavior* (consulting an AI chatbot about health complaints), in *Ditto's home market*, with *overlapping fieldwork dates* (Oct 2025) to the West Health–Gallup study. The NL rate (~32%) is *higher* than the US rate (~25%). https://www.unive.nl/zorgverzekering/onderzoek-raadplegen-ai-bij-gezondheidsvragen
- **Fact:** It also captures the care-substitution risk Ditto can position against: **21% of Dutch respondents said an AI chatbot reassured them enough to postpone or avoid a GP visit**, while **37% said a chatbot might prompt them to contact a GP sooner**. https://www.unive.nl/zorgverzekering/onderzoek-raadplegen-ai-bij-gezondheidsvragen
- **Caveat (label = limitation):** n=1,042 commercial insurer panel survey (PanelWizard), not peer-reviewed; "past year" recall and "health complaints" framing are broader than the US "information or advice." Directionally robust, not a like-for-like methodological twin.

### Authoritative EU-wide context (NOT health-specific — use as denominator, not the headline)

- **Fact:** **32.7% of people aged 16–74 in the EU used generative AI tools in 2025** (first year Eurostat measured this); highest in Denmark 48.4%, Estonia 46.6%, Malta 46.5%; lowest Romania 17.8%, Italy 19.9%. 64% of 16–24-year-olds vs 7% of 65–74s. [Eurostat, 16 Dec 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20251216-3). Underlying table: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/isoc_ai_iaiu/default/table?lang=en
- **Inference (clearly labeled):** Eurostat measures *any* genAI use, not health use. The coincidence that EU-wide genAI adoption (~33%) and NL health-specific chatbot use (~32%) are nearly identical is *suggestive that health is among the dominant personal use cases in NL*, but Eurostat does not break out health, so this is an inference, not a measured fact.

**Germany / Nordics:** No clean health-specific consumer stat surfaced in this pass. Eurostat country data gives the genAI denominators (Denmark 48.4% is the Nordic standout; Germany sits mid-table within the EU 32.7% average — see Eurostat table above). If a DE/Nordic *health-specific* number is needed, that is a known open gap requiring a dedicated search (Bitkom DE and Nordic health-authority surveys are the likely sources but were not confirmed here).

## 3. WhatsApp / messaging for health

**Honest headline: the evidence is asymmetric.** Platform ubiquity is rock-solid. Direct "families discuss health over WhatsApp" consumer surveys are thin — most quantified studies are clinician-side or small disease-specific cohorts. Do not overclaim a specific percentage here.

### What is solid (platform reach)

| Stat | Source & date | URL |
|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp reaches ~88.8% of NL internet users aged 16+ (most-used social/messaging platform in NL; a second source cites ~92.9%) | Statista (NL leading social networks 2025) | https://www.statista.com/statistics/1224092/leading-social-networks-netherlands/ |
| WhatsApp is the top messenger by brand among Dutch consumers | Statista | https://www.statista.com/forecasts/1226830/most-used-messenger-by-brand-in-the-netherlands |

**Implication (inference):** With ~9 in 10 Dutch adults on WhatsApp, the *channel* for family health coordination is effectively universal. This supports Ditto's WhatsApp-distribution thesis on reach grounds. It does *not* prove how many use it *for health* specifically.

### What exists on WhatsApp-for-health (mostly academic, mostly not consumer-representative)

| Finding | Population | Source & date | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| 99.1% of caregivers of children with neurodisabilities found WhatsApp useful for sharing medical info; n=242 responding caregivers (94.5% response) | Caregivers, Sudan | PMC (experience from Sudan) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8026000/ |
| 97% of surveyed doctors shared sensitive patient info over instant messenger without patient consent | Clinicians, Ireland (Univ. Hospital Limerick) | "WhatsApp Doc?", PMC | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4975831/ |
| 43% of physicians use WhatsApp academic groups for CME content; 32% access >4x/day; n=581 | Physicians | PubMed/PMC, 2024 | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39227936/ |
| RCT: WhatsApp support groups used to deliver responsive-caregiving / caregiver-mental-health parenting intervention (chosen because WhatsApp is near-universal and works on basic phones) | Parents/caregivers | PMC, COVID-era RCT | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10624105/ |
| Diabetes RCT delivering education to patients AND family-member caregivers via individualized WhatsApp groups | Patients + family, Lebanon | ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06476495 | https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06176495 |
| WhatsApp used by palliative-care patients and relatives to express gratitude to care teams (qualitative) | Patients + relatives | ResearchGate, 2022 | https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362753852 |

### The honest gap

- **Fact:** Multiple peer-reviewed studies confirm WhatsApp is *actively used* to share medical information among patients, families/caregivers, and clinicians, and that it is repeatedly *chosen as the delivery platform* in health interventions precisely because of near-universal adoption.
- **Gap (stated plainly):** No large, nationally representative consumer survey was found that quantifies "what % of people use WhatsApp to discuss health / coordinate care / share health updates with family." The existing numbers are either (a) platform-reach stats, (b) clinician-to-clinician use, or (c) small disease-specific or single-country cohorts. Treat any "X% of families coordinate care over WhatsApp" claim as an **inference**, not a sourced fact.
- **Adjacent supporting data (US caregiving, not WhatsApp-specific):** 26% of US family caregivers report difficulty coordinating care, up from 19% in 2015 ([AARP, Caregiving in the US 2020](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/caregiving-in-the-united-states/)). This sizes the *coordination pain* Ditto/Hello 24/7 address, independent of channel.

**Recommendation for the team:** If a defensible "WhatsApp-for-family-health" number is needed for a deck or investor doc, commission a single quick panel question (e.g., via the same PanelWizard route Univé used) rather than citing a borrowed clinician study. The borrowed studies will not survive scrutiny as consumer evidence.

## 4. Hello 24/7 profile

**Major correction up front:** Hello 24/7 is **not** a digital-GP / telehealth / teleconsultation service. It is a **family caregiver-coordination app** — direct conceptual neighbor to Ditto's "family care circle," not a video-GP product. Sources below.

### What it is

- **Fact:** A Dutch "Samenzorg" (shared-care) / "mantelzorg" (informal care) app. Lets family, friends, neighbors, and care professionals coordinate caregiving in one place: shared calendar, task management, family map (address book), chat, file/photo storage, push notifications, appointment coordination. https://www.hello247.nl/en/
- **Fact:** Two audiences: (1) individuals organizing informal care for a loved one; (2) care organizations coordinating professional care with informal networks. https://www.hello247.nl/oplossing-voor-zorgaanbieders
- **Fact:** Launched ~2019; founded by Arthur Clement (ex-Netaspect) with co-founder Victor Terpstra (ex-Osudio). iOS app live (App Store ID 1547226419). https://apps.apple.com/nl/app/hello-24-7/id1547226419 ; company profile: https://nl.linkedin.com/company/hello-247

### Model & pricing (the "€6–12/month" internal benchmark)

- **Fact:** Free tier always available (limited calendar/contacts, 1 GB storage, basic care-sharing). https://www.hello247.nl/en/
- **Fact:** **Premium starts at €5.83/month for the whole family group, billed annually** (unlimited calendar, unlimited contacts, unlimited file storage, Google/Outlook/IMAP calendar sync, priority support). https://www.hello247.nl/en/hello-premium/
- **Fact (the strategic one):** **Zilveren Kruis and Menzis reimburse Hello Premium 100%** through supplementary insurance with no impact on the deductible ("eigen risico"); IZZ and others offer discounts. https://www.hello247.nl/en/hello-premium/ ; Menzis: https://www.menzis.nl/extra-voordelen/hello-24-7 ; Zilveren Kruis 2026: https://www.zilverenkruis.nl/consumenten/vergoedingen/hello-247
- **Assessment of internal "€6–12/month" benchmark:** The *consumer list price* (~€5.83/mo annualized) sits at the bottom of that range. The "€6–12" band likely reflects either older pricing or a per-user/B2B-care-organization rate; this brief could only confirm the €5.83/mo family price from the public pricing page. Flag for internal reconciliation. (Label: the higher end of the band is **unverified** against a public source.)

### Scale (flag: figures are vendor-reported, dates vary)

| Metric | Value | Source & date | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| App installs | 72,289+ | hello247.nl site (as fetched May 2026) | https://www.hello247.nl/en/ |
| Active users | ~21,000 | hello247.nl site | https://www.hello247.nl/en/ |
| Families using the app | ~10,000 | reporting via search (Aug 2025) | https://www.hello247.nl/en/ |
| Users gained in a 6-month window | ~40,000 | press, Nov 2022 and again cited Aug 2025 | https://www.marketingtribune.nl/bureaus/nieuws/2022/11/mantelzorg-app-hello-24-7-lanceert-campagne-deel-de-zorg/index.xml |
| Self-reported user benefit | 68% report reduced stress / feeling supported | hello247.nl site | https://www.hello247.nl/en/ |

**Caveat:** Scale numbers are self-reported by the vendor and the "40,000 in 6 months" figure appears in both 2022 and 2025 reporting, so it may be recycled marketing copy rather than a fresh metric. Treat as order-of-magnitude (tens of thousands of users / ~10k families), not audited.

### Partnerships

- **Fact:** **Menzis** partnered with Hello 24/7 to support informal caregivers; from **1 Jan 2025**, Menzis insured members with the relevant supplementary cover get Hello Premium free, bundled with Menzis caregiver services. https://fmtgezondheidszorg.nl/menzis-en-hello-24-7-lanceren-zorgplatform-voor-mantelzorgers/ ; https://www.skipr.nl/nieuws/menzis-komt-met-app-om-mantelzorgers-te-helpen/
- **Fact:** **Thebe** (large NL care provider) signed a partnership **15 Jul 2025** to build the "Thebe Samenzorg-app" on Hello 24/7's platform; rolled out October 2025. https://www.thebe.nl/nieuws/2025/thebe-en-hello-247-lanceren-app-voor-informele-zorg/ ; https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/zorgtechapp-hello247-sluit-partnership-thebe
- **Note on a.s.r.:** The internal note suspected an a.s.r. link. **Not confirmed in this research pass** — the confirmed insurer relationships are **Zilveren Kruis** and **Menzis** (reimbursement) plus **IZZ** (discount). Treat the a.s.r. link as **unverified / likely incorrect** until a primary source is found.

### Relevance to Ditto (a patient-facing health app)

1. **It is a direct comparable for the "family care circle," not a telehealth competitor.** Hello 24/7 owns the coordination layer (who does what, when) that sits adjacent to Ditto's clarification layer (what did the doctor actually say). Inference: there is both *overlap risk* (they could add summary/explainer features) and *partnership/positioning opportunity* (Ditto could be the "understanding" layer feeding their "coordination" layer).
2. **The reimbursement playbook is the headline takeaway.** A consumer caregiving app at ~€5.83/mo got two major NL insurers (Zilveren Kruis, Menzis) to reimburse it 100% via supplementary cover. Inference: this is a proven distribution/monetization path in the Dutch market that Ditto should study closely — insurer reimbursement turns a €6/mo consumer price into a zero-cost-to-user, insurer-funded channel.
3. **Care-provider channel exists.** The Thebe deal shows the B2B2C route — embedding the app inside a care organization's offering — is live and recent (2025).
