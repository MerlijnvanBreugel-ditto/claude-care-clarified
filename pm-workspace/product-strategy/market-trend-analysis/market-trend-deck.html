Wearables own the sensor data. Nobody owns what the doctor said.

The strategic frame

Three axes, three different games

Clarity

Understand your care

Contested, fast-moving. ChatGPT, scribes, Onno, OpenEvidence. Where the bet must be made.

Connection

Family / care circle

Fragmented, low-tech, no winner. CaringBridge, Caren, Hello 24/7, Kin's loop, WhatsApp. Our core moat.

Convenience

Access / scheduling

Won and consolidated. Doctolib, ZorgDomein, portals. Integrate, don't compete.

The thesis: concede Convenience. Win Connection (the family layer) and a trust-and-UX version of Clarity ("understand your care"), not raw intelligence.

The consumer "curated-source chat" slot is open in the EU. Imagine a chat grounded only in vetted sources and the patient's own care: that is a credible Clarity mode, and nobody scaled is doing it for EU consumers.

Clarity · the moat verdict
Don't compete on "understanding health." Win on understanding your care.

You cannot out-intelligence ChatGPT (40M/day, citation-grounding now table stakes after OpenAI's clinician release [OpenAI ↗]), and consumers under-value privacy. The defensible Clarity mode is the compound: healthcare-specific UX (tracking, journaling, visualization) × clinician-grounded trust × EU-native compliance × your own longitudinal record × the family circle. Generic medical Q&A loses; "your care" wins.

The money & the rules

Reimbursement (two routes now), the device-class logic, and the regulatory clock.

The money · reimbursement

Two routes: clinical DiGA, and the NL Connection route

Clinical (DiGA)

~56–59 apps, >1M prescriptions [BfArM ↗]. 95% are Class I; the wall is a clinical RCT, not the device class [VWS ↗].

Connection (NL, now)

Hello 24/7 is reimbursed 100% by Zilveren Kruis + Menzis from €5.83/mo as a caregiver app [Hello 24/7 ↗]. NL insurers already pay for a family-care-circle product. A nearer route than DiGA.

The DiGA class logic, precisely (your question answered)
DiGA requires a CE-marked medical device of risk class I or IIa. IIa is not required, but the app must be at least a registered Class I medical device — non-devices are excluded [BfArM ↗]. Floor = Class I, ceiling = IIa. The real one-way door is device vs. non-device. A pure summary tool with no claimed medical purpose is a non-device and is not DiGA-eligible as-is.
The money · the fork

Stay light, or go clinical — sequential, not exclusive

Path 1 — stay light

Class I or non-device, patient-owned. Monetize at home via B2B inside hospital/insurer routes, the Hello-24/7-style insurer reimbursement for a care app, and the emerging Dutch diga.nl scheme [VWS ↗].

Path 2 — go clinical

CE Class I + a clinical RCT opens DiGA. Est. €1.2–3M, 2–3 years [DUX ↗]. Poor oncology base rate.

Note: precise B2B pricing isn't knowable from public data; validate willingness-to-pay with real deals (Hello 24/7's €5.83/mo reimbursed is the only firm NL anchor).

Why Mika is a cautionary tale, not a green light
Mika (Class I, structural/procedural track) had an RCT that hit distress but missed quality-of-life [JMIR ↗]; it withdrew from DiGA twice and Fosanis sold it to US Outcomes4Me (Jun 2025) [Outcomes4Me ↗]. No live permanent oncology-companion DiGA exists today.
The money · what you owe yourselves

Three decisions

1. Ratify the intended-purpose statement

It controls device classification and whether the "care intelligence layer" drifts into high-risk by accident.

2. Decide the home-market route

B2B inside hospital/insurer funding, the Hello-24/7-style insurer-reimbursed care app, and/or diga.nl. Validate willingness-to-pay with real deals.

3. Set the clinical RCT go / no-go gate

Define when the 2–3 year DiGA bet is worth it, given the poor oncology-companion base rate.
