# Brief B — EU Reimbursement of Digital Health Apps

> Workstream B of the outside-in Market & Trend scan. Phase 1: broad-but-sourced map, not exhaustive. Created 2026-05-30.
> Convention used below: **[F]** = fact (sourced), **[I]** = inference, **[R]** = recommendation, **[est]** = estimate.
> Source numbers in `[n]` map to the numbered URL list in Section 8.

## 1. Executive summary

- **Germany's DiGA is the only mature, scaled, app-on-prescription reimbursement scheme in Europe** — ~56-59 listed apps, >1M prescriptions by end-2024, statutory cover for 73M+ people. Everyone else is copying it or still piloting. **[F]** [1][6][16]
- **The German scheme already reimburses a non-treatment, patient-facing cancer companion (Mika)** under a "structural and procedural improvements" pathway that explicitly counts *patient sovereignty, health literacy, adherence, and care coordination* as valid benefits — not just clinical cure. **This is the single most Ditto-relevant fact in this brief.** **[F]** [22][24]
- **France (PECAN, live since 2024) and Belgium (mHealthBELGIUM M3, first regular reimbursements Feb 2025) are the next two live EU schemes**, both DiGA-inspired, both small in volume so far. **[F]** [2][3][20]
- **The Netherlands has no app-on-prescription scheme.** Digital care is funded *inside* existing care tariffs (NZa) and is insurer-led; a new "Reimbursement within Research" route (ZIN, expected Q4 2025) is the closest thing to a fast-track. **[F]** [13][14]
- **The UK does not reimburse apps directly.** NICE Early Value Assessment (EVA) conditionally *recommends* technologies (e.g. 7 digital therapies for depression/anxiety, 2023) for NHS use while evidence is gathered; local NHS bodies fund. DTAC is a gate, not a payment. **[F]** [9][12][26]
- **Trend = expanding access but rising evidence bar.** Germany's Feb-2026 "DiGA 2.0" reform shifts to continuous real-world evidence, mandatory outcome reporting, and ties ≥20% of price to performance. Access is broadening (e.g. a new "earning-capacity" benefit pathway); the proof burden is tightening. **[F]** [10]
- **Nordics: evaluation frameworks (NordDEC), not reimbursement.** Denmark endorses apps nationally but pays locally with no national tariff. **[F]** [8]
- **Honest gap for Ditto:** every scheme still requires a CE-marked medical device plus a prospective study showing a measurable patient benefit. A pure "recorded-conversation summary" is likely Class I / borderline-non-device and would not qualify as-is. The realistic play is the German *structural/procedural* pathway (the Mika precedent), which demands Ditto position as a CE-marked oncology care-companion with a trial on adherence/health-literacy/distress outcomes — a 18-36 month, six-figure-plus undertaking. **[I][R]**

## 4. Trend direction

**Access is broadening; the evidence bar is rising.** These move together, not in opposite directions.

- **Germany DiGA 2.0 (in force 1 Feb 2026)** is the headline reform. It shifts from one-off "selective" evidence to **continuous, data-driven, real-world evaluation**: mandatory "accompanying success measurement" (usage duration/frequency, patient-reported satisfaction, patient-reported health status) reported to BfArM every 6 months from 2027, **and ≥20% of price tied to performance** from Jan-2026. New standardized questionnaires (Annexes 3/4) remove free-form study design. **[F]** [10]
- **Access broadened in two ways:** a new **"maintaining earning capacity"** benefit pathway (opens mental-health/MSK/rehab use cases), and explicit accommodation of AI apps via **EU AI Act conformity declarations** in the DiGA application. **[F]** [10]
- **Evidence bar rising elsewhere too:** systematic review (npj Digital Medicine, 2025) found significant effect sizes for DiGAs *but high risk of bias* in 21/23 studies; physician skepticism persists; cost-effectiveness evidence is scarce. **6 of 11 delistings (by Jun-2025) were for failure to prove a positive effect** — the "earn-while-you-prove" provisional route is genuinely culling apps. **[F]** [4][25]
- **Patient-facing / non-DTx / AI-companion eligibility:** *Yes, partially, and this is the key trend for Ditto.* Germany's "structural and procedural improvement" track has always allowed non-curative benefits (adherence, health literacy, sovereignty, coordination); Mika proves a companion app can be reimbursed. France's PECAN and Belgium's M3 remain more DTx/telemonitoring-centric so far. **[F]** [20][22][24]
- **EU-level (EHDS, Reg. 2025/327, in force 26 Mar 2025):** Not a reimbursement scheme. It mandates interoperability (patient summaries, e-prescriptions by 2029), data portability, and secondary data use. It *lowers integration friction* and makes "patient summary" a first-class EU data object, but does **not** create a pan-EU pay route. National schemes stay national. **[F]** [18]
- **Contrarian read:** the DTx reimbursement thesis is under pressure. Negotiated DiGA prices fell ~46% vs. launch; physician uptake is uneven; some commentators openly ask whether to "give up on DTx." Reimbursement is real but the economics are thinner and the compliance load heavier than the 2020 hype implied. **[F]** [15][17]

## 5. Ditto-relevance — realistic pathway vs. honest gap

**The gap (be honest):** Ditto's core product — recording a doctor-patient conversation and producing a patient-friendly, shareable summary — is, as-is, most likely a **Class I or borderline non-medical-device** tool. Every reimbursement scheme above requires (a) a CE-marked medical device and (b) a prospective study showing a *measurable patient benefit*. A summary tool with no claimed clinical/structural outcome qualifies for **none** of them today. **[I]**

**The one realistic pathway — German DiGA, "structural and procedural improvements" track:**
- The DiGA framework explicitly accepts benefits in **patient sovereignty, health literacy, treatment adherence, and coordination of treatment processes** — *exactly* Ditto's value proposition. **[F]** [24]
- **Mika is the live precedent:** a cancer *companion* (not a treatment) reimbursed as a DiGA. Ditto's oncology focus and "care intelligence layer" map closely onto this. **[F]** [22]
- **What it would take [R][est]:** (1) define Ditto as a CE-marked Class IIa medical device with a specific medical purpose; (2) pick a tight indication (e.g. oncology patients post-consultation); (3) run a prospective comparative study (RCT or comparable) showing a measurable structural/procedural improvement — e.g. higher treatment adherence, better health literacy, lower decisional distress; (4) clear BfArM's data-security (BSI TR-03161), interoperability (ePA), and now AI-Act conformity bars; (5) under DiGA 2.0, commit to continuous outcome reporting and ≥20% performance-linked pricing. Realistically **18-36 months and a six-to-seven-figure** evidence + regulatory program. **[est]**
- **Secondary options:** France PECAN (faster, lower entry bar, but DTx/monitoring-oriented and €780/yr cap) as an earn-while-proving entry; Belgium M3 only if Ditto fits a funded care pathway (today: heart failure — not Ditto's space). **[I]**
- **Adjacent monetization that avoids the evidence wall [R]:** B2B sale to hospitals/insurers funded *inside* existing care tariffs (the Dutch NZa model) sidesteps app-on-prescription entirely. This is likely Ditto's faster revenue route than chasing DiGA, and the two are not mutually exclusive.

**Bottom line [R]:** There *is* a plausible EU reimbursement pathway (German DiGA structural/procedural track, Mika precedent), but it requires Ditto to become a regulated, evidence-backed oncology care-companion — a deliberate strategic commitment, not a side effect of the current product. Treat reimbursement as a 2-3 year bet, not a near-term revenue line.

## 6. Risks & caveats

- **Device classification risk:** claiming a medical purpose to qualify for DiGA pulls Ditto into MDR Class IIa, AI-Act, and clinical-evidence obligations it does not have today. This is a one-way door — coordinate with Workstream A (regulation). **[I]**
- **Price erosion:** German negotiated prices fell ~46% vs. launch and DiGA 2.0 adds performance-linked clawback. Even if reimbursed, unit economics are modest. **[F]** [10][15]
- **Delisting risk:** 6 DiGAs were removed for failing to prove benefit. Provisional listing is not a safe harbour. **[F]** [25]
- **Single-market dependence:** the only mature scheme is Germany; building the whole reimbursement thesis on one country is concentration risk. **[I]**
- **Price-transparency gap:** French and Belgian *per-app negotiated* prices are largely not public; figures here are framework caps/bands, not confirmed per-app tariffs. **[F/est]**
- **Date staleness:** DiGA count drifts (56 vs 59 across sources, May-2026); Dutch ZIN route was still pending Ministerial sign-off at last check (Q4-2025). Re-verify before any decision. **[F]**

## 7. Confidence & gaps

- **High confidence:** mechanism design per country; DiGA as the flagship; the structural/procedural benefit track and Mika precedent; DiGA 2.0 reform direction; EHDS is not a reimbursement route.
- **Medium confidence:** exact live app counts (sources disagree by date); French/Belgian volumes; whether Austria's scheme is live.
- **Low confidence / not verified:** per-app *negotiated* prices outside Germany; Dutch ZIN route final status; Nordic local-payment specifics.
- **Phase-2 dives that would pay off:** (1) **DiGA structural/procedural deep-dive** — pull 2-3 listed apps' published study designs to scope exactly what evidence a Ditto oncology companion would need and the realistic cost/timeline; (2) **classification call with Workstream A** — does claiming a DiGA-qualifying medical purpose force MDR IIa + high-risk AI-Act, and is that worth it; (3) **Dutch insurer/NZa B2B route** — the faster, non-DiGA revenue path, validated with 1-2 insurer conversations.
