## How we started
We have started building from the insight that medical scribes are gaining traction, because technology has caught up, but that the real problem was that the patient was still using a notepad at best or a failing memory in most cases. EHRs have been letting patients down with providing clunky software, enormous fragmentation and Latin documents disclosing that you have cancer.

From the start, we have held the contrarian belief that the patient is massively underserved and that the patient will be the unlock for a completely stuck healthcare system. For now, we have together decided that we focus on severe diseases with primary focus on oncology and secondary neurodegenerative. 
## Summaries cannot be the goal
Summarizations tools have become a commodity and increasingly reliable. On the business side they become standard for all conversations.
- Making better and more reliable summaries could be defensible IF you have something no one else has. Proprietary models, unique validation data, a very specific niche. So far, Ditto does not truly have that. We make most out of the underlying models we can.
- EHRs and medical scribes are also catching up. Within 1 year time, patient summaries will be available natively in there. When ChatGPT or Gemini offer dedicated conversation summaries, this directly attacks Ditto. Saying that 'doctors will never allow this' is stretching reality.
- Also, we summaries are relevant when you have an appointment and potentially the day after. Even if you are severly ill, this might give you a reason to open Ditto weekly at most, with today's product. Unless you at that moment deliver incredible value - i.e. healing you perhaps? - you are a convenient functionality rather than something people grow attached to and will continue using
- From a monetization perspective, low frequency is challenging. Consumers not pay easily for healthcare apps, let alone when they use it only monthly. For insurers they will require a certain activity to warrant the reimbursement.
- Pushing hard on frequency by itself is a dead-end. Same for growth features. Unless we solve actual and real user problems, we can only so much nudge behaviour in a direction that *we* want.
### The good part
- Our summaries are really better than other solutions, and people trust them. This is key
- Currently, we are 10x better than the alternative of forgetting
- Summaries and conversation recordings bring much more potential than we currently unlock.
- Summaries are the (or a) way in. We have to maximize the value we bring to users in that episodic moment
- What we still can do: auto-update your profile, nudges with follow-up actions, helping you with actual follow-up tasks, informing the family dynamically, updating your health profile, linking you to peers, explaining your treatment in podcast/visual form, linking you to a second-opinion, finding a fitting trial for your disease (and the list goes on)
## The undecided vision
We have been discussing and envisioning different versions of a future Ditto. Currently, I think we have at least 5:
1. Care Companion
	1. Vision: Ditto supports you along every step of your care journey
	2. Core focus: understanding, making sense of what is happening, knowing what you can expect
	3. Feature scope: clarifications, overview, visualization, disease-specific partnerships, context-aware AI Q&A, symptom-tracking; heavy AI
	4. Monetization routes: reimbursement due to more efficient healthcare processes and improved communication, maybe consumer-paying, pharma partnerships possible
	5. Defensibility: 
		1. Strong: disease-specific features, better-than general-purpose AI assistants, holistic place; unlock AI capabilities for boomers
		2. Difficult: EHRs improving, data limitations, adherence and commitment of users needed to leverage potential
2. Social Care Platform
	1. Vision: Ditto is the place for shared understanding among loved ones and peers about how you're doing 
	2. Core focus: emotional support, not being alone
	3. Feature scope: support-gestures, sharing daily updates, blogging, finding peers, creating communities
	4. Monetization routes: not trivial unless very high volumes and frequency
	5. Defensibility: 
		1. Strong: network-effects, go-to place for sharing anything health-related
		2. Difficult: competing with existing social media platforms; healthcare maybe so specific that you compete with WhatsApp
3. Care Coordination Management
	1. Vision: Within Ditto, families manage disease and arrange practical support 
	2. Core focus: coordinating of tasks, keeping core care groups on the same page
	3. Feature scope: factual medical updates, overview of everything that is happening, agenda management, task allocation, flexible managed accounts; low AI most likely
	4. Monetization routes: reimbursement as caregivers are enabled to more effectively contribute, potentially B2B if they are onboarded to the platform
	5. Defensibility: 
		1. Strong: Increasingly important with healthcare trends, care too complex for WA group chats
		2. Difficult: when care groups include professionals they need to be onboarded (Caren), depends on whole group being convinced to make atomic network
4. Patient-Owned Medical Record
	1. Vision: You own and understand all your healthcare data in Ditto
	2. Core focus: ingest all available medical data and make it understandable and shareable
	3. Feature scope: data integrations, EHDS launching partner, data vault management
	4. Monetization routes: reimbursement for the right to access your data, potential B2B when making portals redundant
	5. Defensibility: 
		1. Strong: if superior solution in EU could become the go-to with immense lock-in
		2. Difficult: data itself is low value without insight, competing with governmental solutions, high dependencies on data integrations
5. Trusted Health Assistant
	1. Vision: Ditto prevents illness or gets you recovered as best as possible
	2. Core focus: giving advice on activities and nudging you on medical behaviour you can control
	3. Feature scope: personalized nudges, smart reminders, wearable integrations, full AI assistant; super heavy on AI 
	4. Monetization routes: reimbursement due to reduced pressure on healthcare and professionals (outsourced partly to Ditto), potential B2B when improving treatment adherence, direct paying patients
	5. Defensibility: 
		1. Strong: highest technology IP, once trusted by patients super strong lock-in
		2. Difficult: compete with consumer big tech, instant MDR and high-risk

#### How they relate
- These are not excluding end-states. They can be combined and merged. Potentially, they are layers that will be stacked together.
- However, doing so also creates the risk of a confused and split focus and positioning.
- 1, 3, and 5 directly link to our contrarian take on the patient being the key to better healthcare. 4 requires intelligence to truly change things. 2 improves the perception and experience, maybe not the outcome or efficiency. I propose that we really explore this vision more as I feel like there we have the biggest upside and impact, which opens up monetization routes, expected or unexpected ones
- For the short-mid term, I believe that we can deliver most value for oncology via 1, 2, 5. Neurodegenerative favours 3 more. 
- 4 to me seems to be not a goal in itself, but potentially a requirement to truly nail 1 and 5.

### Why this matters
Every day, we make decisions on what we build and how we aim to grow Ditto. Directionality in this is important, to avoid a fragmented and unclear product and proposition in the market. Features we ship might be discontinued - that is fine - but till that time it comes at the cost of maintenance and a less-clear product experience.

Of course, bug fixes and polishing of core features will always take a chunk of our dev capacity, but in the end we should focus on what differentiates us and what delivers most value for our users. If we cannot sharply articulate who those users are, which problems we solve, why we should do that better than anyone, we miss out on focus and efficiency.

### Why this might not matter
- Many successful companies did not articulate the mission statement they would have 10 years later. However, I do think that in their core there was a north start or vision that underpinned their category.
- The field is moving so fast, that we need to remain adaptable. 
- The question is to what extent commitments to a direction now exclude other options or make it less likely that we can meaningfully succeed in those. E.g. now going all in on data integrations and anticipating EHDS means we slow down on adding true health intelligence to whatever data users already put into Ditto
