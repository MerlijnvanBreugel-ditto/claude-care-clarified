## Netherlands — active specialist-follow-up population (the core addressable)

People actively seeing a specialist with regular appointments, the core in-consultation use case:

| Condition | In active specialist follow-up (NL) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cancer (treatment + active surveillance) | ~300,000-350,000 | Medium |
| Heart failure (active management) | ~150,000-180,000 | Medium |
| Dementia / Alzheimer's (specialist phase) | ~90,000-120,000 | Low-medium |
| Acute MI (within 12 months) | ~60,000-80,000 | Medium |
| Parkinson's | ~44,000-50,000 | Medium-high |
| Multiple sclerosis | ~36,000 | High |
| Major cardiac surgery (within 12 months) | ~15,000 | High |
| ALS / MND | ~1,700 | High |
| **Deduplicated total** | **~700,000-800,000 / year** | Low-medium |

That is ~4-4.5% of the NL population in serious-condition active management at any time. **Annual inflow**: ~180,000-200,000 people enter one of the three big categories per year (~15,000-17,000/month) — the funnel refresh.

## What this means for the targets

- At ~2,080 Core MAU, Ditto has ~0.3% penetration of the NL addressable population.
- 50K MAU = ~10% penetration of NL active specialist-care. Ambitious for one product in one country in one year, but not structurally impossible with the care-circle multiplier (non-patient MAU).
- **The NL TAM is sufficient for 50K, but barely.** The real long-term story is EU expansion.

## Europe (EU-27) — the long-term ceiling

| Category | EU prevalence | Active specialist (est.) |
|---|---|---|
| Cancer (5-year) | ~22M | ~9-10M |
| Dementia/Alzheimer's | ~8-9M | ~3M |
| Heart failure | ~8-10M | ~6-7M |
| Parkinson's | ~1.2-1.5M | high retention |
| MS | ~700k-900k | lifelong |

Rough EU specialist-care TAM ~30-35M, roughly 60x the NL equivalent. 0.1% EU penetration = 30-35K MAU; 1% = 300K+.

## Strategic reads (from the TAM work)

1. **Cancer is the right beachhead**: highest appointment frequency, highest stakes, strongest circle activation, largest current group.
2. **The caregiver angle in neuro is underexploited**: dementia patients don't record their own visits; the adult child/partner does. The recorder and the recipient are the same person, which changes the value proposition and is a *stronger* care-circle pattern. (Directly supports the caregiver-as-customer reframe in the vision's Route A.)
3. **Cardiac is a sleeper**: post-MI and heart-failure patients are motivated, have regular contact, involve family, and aren't yet explicitly targeted.
4. **International is the real story**: even modest penetration in 2-3 more countries beats dominating NL alone.

## Implications for the long-term vision

- The "active treatment" sub-populations (oncology on-treatment, heart-failure management) are where frequency is genuinely high. This is the empirical basis for **Route C** (treatment-window companion) and for picking high-intensity windows over the episodic survivorship tail.
- The neuro/dementia caregiver pattern is the empirical basis for **Route A** (caregiver as the daily user and buyer).
- TAM is sufficient to justify a venture-scale ambition, but only with the circle multiplier and EU expansion. Single-country single-product math is tight.
