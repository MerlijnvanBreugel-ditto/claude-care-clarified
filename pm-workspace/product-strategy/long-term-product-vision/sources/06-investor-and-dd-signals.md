# Source Synthesis 06 — Investor & Due-Diligence Signals

> Distills: `context/260223-expert-call-summaries-dd-investment.md` (SpringTide Ventures, Jaap Maljers, Bupa UK, Novartis, Feb 2026).
> A synthesis for the long-term vision, not a copy. These are external, skeptical views, useful precisely because they pre-date and corroborate the commoditization thesis.

## The convergent message

Four independent experts, one refrain: **summaries are not a business; pick a tight wedge; build a habit/"so-what" layer; don't underwrite reimbursement as the spine.** This aligns exactly with the long-term vision's framing (frequency + non-reimbursement monetization).

## SpringTide Ventures (Michael Levy, venture partner)

- **Don't boil the ocean.** Pick a tight persona with willingness to pay and repeat need. Caregiver / sandwich generation / aging is the best wedge.
- **Paid acquisition in D2C patient tools is getting expensive** (a comparable saw CAC rise from ~$12 toward unsustainable). Shift to **channel acquisition** (revenue-share with associations/partners/communities).
- **Measure time-to-aha.** Pull the aha moment forward; if value only shows at the next appointment weeks later, you lose them.
- **You likely need a daily/weekly reason** even if core use is episodic. ~50-60 meaningful engagements/year is a low-end bar.
- **Consider transaction pricing** (pay per visit/episode) until subscription behavior is earned.
- Insurer "critical mass" heuristic: 100-500K registered users. Pharma (60-100K users): ~$50-150K/yr contracts, but tactical and churny.

## Jaap Maljers (would not invest)

- **"Usefulness ≠ business."** Pre-revenue, no proven monetization. Doubts insurers/pharma as structurally stable.
- **Provider adoption resistance** is real (Bergman, ZorgDomein, Plexus experience). Distribution through doctors is harder than modeled.
- **Target group reality**: heaviest users are older, less digitally fluent, lower health-literacy. Slow adopters; early positive feedback may be a curated early-adopter base.
- **AI commoditization**: summarization is easily replicated; low moat unless Ditto builds beyond "explain medical jargon." (This was Feb 2026, before the Jan-2026 lab launches made it more true.)

## Bupa UK (Sophia Guennoun, Group Head of Healthcare Innovation)

- **"So what?"** Insurers buy outcomes, not nice summaries. Drive better CX, cost reduction, or prevention.
- **Scribing is commoditizing fast.** Pitch the "what happens next" layer (personalization / workflows / outcomes).
- **The wedge: chronic + elderly** (high interaction frequency, multi-pathology, caregiver involvement).
- **Clearest near-term ROI**: reduce repeat visits + missed appointments. Evolve from scribe to agent.
- Commercial packaging: **white-label inside the insurer app, B2B2C, flat licensing fee**, not standalone DTC. Sales cycles 12-18 months.

## Novartis (Anurag Rai, ex-Digital Health Solutions)

- **Pharma pays for only two things**: adherence/post-prescription support, and advertising inside disease platforms. Everything else is "interesting" but not scalable.
- Belong.Life precedent: ~$200-300K upfront + $50K/mo for 15-20K users, lasted 2 years then stopped. **Contracts churn; not SaaS.**
- **Scale expectations are brutal**: pharma thinks in millions; 50-60K users = "irrelevant." Need ~20-30% penetration of a meaningful subsegment.
- **Don't underwrite pharma revenue** unless building a Belong-style ad model at real scale.

## Implications for the long-term vision

- The DD calls independently validate the vision's two design constraints: **build a habit layer (frequency)** and **don't depend on reimbursement / pharma (monetization)**.
- "Caregiver / sandwich generation / aging" as the wedge (SpringTide) and "chronic + elderly, high frequency" (Bupa) are direct external endorsements of **Route A** (caregiver) and **Route C** (chronic/treatment window).
- "Transaction pricing" and "channel acquisition" are concrete monetization/GTM ideas to carry into route design.
- RWD/pharma (Route C's second engine) is real but must be treated as **upside, slow and churny**, never the spine. Patient/caregiver revenue must stand alone.
- Insurer B2B2C is a white-label, outcomes-led, 12-18-month-cycle motion. Viable as a channel, not a fast spine.
