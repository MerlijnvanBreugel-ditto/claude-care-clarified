# 11 — Jobs To Be Done Across Critical Life Moments & Disease Trajectories

**Status:** evidence catalog, built 2026-06-04. Distills `research/healthcare-life-changing-moments/` (findings 02–08b, pain map 09) and `specs/ideas/health-agent/zeroclaw-jtbd-discovery.md` into one job map. No new research was done; every claim links back. Confidence labels carried over from the source files.

**The framing this catalog serves** (Merlijn, 2026-06-04): these diseases are severe enough that people live with them every single day. During active cancer treatment you are ill 24/7 and think about it constantly. Parkinson's and Alzheimer's reshape how a household experiences every day. So top-of-mind frequency is not the problem. The question is: **which concrete challenges fire at that frequency, for whom, and where can a product actually help?** This catalog answers the first two; the eigenquestions section at the end gives a read on the third, labeled as a read.

The three eigenquestions it feeds:
1. What are the core actions we want to anchor?
2. What problems do we actually want to solve?
3. Who is the main, primary user?

## How to read it

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Patient** | The person with the diagnosis |
| **Caregiver** | The one person doing most of the work (usually partner or adult child) |
| **Circle** | Everyone else who cares: family, friends, colleagues |
| **Fires** | How often the job actually demands an action, not how often it's on your mind |
| Confidence | High / Medium / Low, inherited from the research files. US-derived stats flagged. |

## 1. Jobs by trajectory

### 1.1 Oncology — patient-led, partner close behind

The patient is cognitively intact and runs their own care. Hospital contact during active treatment is frequent: chemo cycles, bloods, radiation can mean weekly or near-weekly visits. The frequency gap is not the treatment phase itself; it's the days between visits, the limbo before, and the years after.

| Phase (duration) | Jobs that fire | Whose job | Fires |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diagnostic limbo** (1–8 wks NL median; 10–25% wait far longer) | Hold the open question (when do I hear, from whom, what does this test mean); interpret ambiguous signals; survive the waiting (anxiety peaks *here*, driven by uncertainty) | Patient + partner | Daily worry, weekly events |
| **Diagnosis** (days) | Absorb the news (40–80% forgotten immediately, ~half of the rest recalled wrong); bring a second pair of ears; tell family, kids, work; first wave of frantic googling | Patient + partner | Concentrated burst |
| **Treatment decision** (days–wks) | Understand options and *intent* (69–81% of metastatic patients thought non-curative chemo could cure them, US data); formulate questions (prompt lists proven to work); decide together. 1 in 5 regret the decision later, more when less involved | Patient + partner | Per consult |
| **Active treatment** (months; hospital contact ~weekly) | Manage side effects at home (caregivers' #1 unmet need: side-effect info 78%, risk/benefit info 79%); meds incl. rescue meds; "is this normal or do I call?" (median ~25 distinct symptoms per chemo course); logistics + transport; update everyone after each cycle | Patient + caregiver | **Daily** |
| **Survivorship** (years; contact drops to ~quarterly) | Live with fear of recurrence (the most common unmet survivor need); re-enter normal life and the GP; "now what?" after the scaffolding of frequent contact disappears | Patient | Background-daily, few events |
| **Recurrence / palliative** | Align the family on goals (the unstructured "family meeting"); understand prognosis (~half of advanced patients unaware); manage frequent care-setting transitions in the last months | Patient + family | Weekly |

Evidence: [`findings/02-journey-cancer.md`](../../../research/healthcare-life-changing-moments/findings/02-journey-cancer.md). Confidence high throughout except EU magnitudes of US-flagged stats.

### 1.2 Neurodegenerative (dementia, Parkinson's, ALS) — caregiver-led

The defining fact: patient capacity declines, so a spouse or adult child progressively becomes historian, coordinator, decision-maker, and proxy. Dementia caregiving runs 8–12 years. The caregiver is the literature's "hidden patient": ~49% clinically significant burden, ~33% depression.

| Phase (duration) | Jobs that fire | Whose job | Fires |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"Is it just aging?" limbo** (years; dementia averages ~3.5y to diagnosis) | Notice and remember changes (the family's observations are the richest diagnostic signal, and nobody captures them); quietly compensate (driving, finances); get the person to a doctor | Family | Weekly observations |
| **Diagnosis** (days–wks) | Absorb the overload, then survive the drop (>50% of carers get no support referral); learn the trajectory; start planning while the patient can still decide | Family + patient | Burst |
| **Legal & planning window** (months, closes as capacity declines) | Arrange volmacht/POA; set up proxy access to records and portals (today: broken, ad hoc, or grey-zone login sharing); ACP, and in NL the advance euthanasia directive question | Caregiver | One-off but heavy |
| **Daily management** (8–12 years) | Give meds on time (~95% of carers manage meds in moderate-severe dementia; in Parkinson's a late dose triggers "off" periods within hours); handle behavior (BPSD hits up to 90% of dementia patients over the course; it's what actually breaks families); nights; coordinate helpers; fight bureaucracy (named top burden by the German Alzheimer society); stay alive yourself | Caregiver | **Daily, 24/7** |
| **Coordination** (continuous) | Be the integration layer between neurology, GP, home care, pharmacy, municipality; re-tell the story to every new face; keep the only complete record (it lives in the caregiver's head and a paper folder) | Caregiver | Weekly |
| **Placement & end of life** | Decide on the nursing home (nearly half report guilt, pressured from all sides); make proxy end-of-life decisions unprepared; grieve someone still alive (~73% of MND carers show grief reactions) | Caregiver | Episodic, heavy |

Evidence: [`findings/03-journey-neurodegenerative.md`](../../../research/healthcare-life-changing-moments/findings/03-journey-neurodegenerative.md). Confidence high; caregiver-mortality magnitudes are US-derived.

### 1.3 MS & autoimmune — patient-led, for decades

Hits people 20–40. Patient stays cognitively intact and self-manages for 40+ years. This is the highest-frequency *patient-side* journey in the whole evidence base.

| Phase (duration) | Jobs that fire | Whose job | Fires |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diagnostic odyssey** (MS ~18 mo; lupus median 47 mo, ~50% first misdiagnosed) | Log fluctuating, invisible episodes; get believed (young women's symptom reports are routinely psychologized); push for referral | Patient | Episodic, for years |
| **Diagnosis shock** (young adult) | Absorb an incurable lifelong diagnosis mid-career; tell partner and employer; watch your own mental health (28.5% report suicidal thoughts since diagnosis) | Patient | Burst |
| **The DMT thread** (recurring, decades) | Choose and re-choose disease-modifying therapy (~26% of switches driven by side effects); keep up blood/MRI monitoring; collide it with family planning | Patient | Quarterly + |
| **Daily life** (decades) | **"Is this a flare?"** self-triage against the 24-hour rule, every time something feels off (the single strongest daily burden found in any journey); pace invisible fatigue (>80% affected, 1/3 call it their worst symptom); pay the disclosure tax (explain the invisible illness, again); track symptoms (recommended daily; real tools are fragmented and abandoned); watch mood (~50% lifetime depression, ~2× suicide risk) | Patient | **Daily, multiple/day** |
| **Decades of overview** | Maintain your own 30-year history (relapses, DMTs, MRIs); re-tell it at every new clinician; 65% of EU MS patients aren't on any registry, so the system doesn't hold it either | Patient | Per transition |

Evidence: [`findings/04-journey-ms-autoimmune.md`](../../../research/healthcare-life-changing-moments/findings/04-journey-ms-autoimmune.md). Confidence high.

### 1.4 Sudden severe (stroke, cardiac, new T1D) — caregiver conscripted overnight

No diagnosis runway. Well one hour, lifelong condition the next, often with no memory of the event. The signature failure is the discharge cliff.

| Phase (duration) | Jobs that fire | Whose job | Fires |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute event** (days) | Hold the story (patient often can't encode it; ~half of cardiac-arrest survivors have memory loss at 1 year); make consent decisions at peak adrenaline | Family | Continuous |
| **Discharge cliff** (weeks 0–6) | Parse the leaflet folder (~90% *think* they understood; ~40% can't recall the plan; up to 62% can't name their new meds); learn a care regime overnight (only ~40% of stroke caregivers feel ready); set up home | Caregiver + patient | **Daily** |
| **Self-management ramp** (months) | Run the new med regime (up to 50% discontinue statins within a year; 12.5% have an adverse drug event within 30 days, US data); navigate referral-gated rehab; learn what "worse" looks like | Patient + caregiver | Daily |
| **Long term** (lifelong) | Keep secondary prevention going after motivation fades; live with invisible after-effects (neuro-fatigue, overstimulation); process "what just happened to me" (PTSD 15–50% in cardiac arrest survivors; family PTSD up to 49%) | Patient + family | Weekly → background |

Evidence: [`findings/05-journey-sudden-severe.md`](../../../research/healthcare-life-changing-moments/findings/05-journey-sudden-severe.md). UK/US magnitudes flagged in source.

## 2. The recurring jobs (master list, all trajectories)

Every job below appears in at least three of the four trajectories ([pain map](../../../research/healthcare-life-changing-moments/synthesis/09-cross-journey-pain-map.md)). Ranked by fire-rate × reach. ZC# = matching job in the [ZeroClaw 37-JTBD catalog](../../../specs/ideas/health-agent/zeroclaw-jtbd-discovery.md).

| # | Job, in the user's words | Fires | Who | Today's tool | ZC# | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Is this normal, or do I call someone?"** | Daily, often multiple/day | Patient (onc, MS); caregiver (neuro) | Notebook, Notes app, Google, or nothing | 28, 29 | High. Clinicians miss between-visit symptoms up to half the time; weekly e-symptom reporting added ~5 months median survival in the Basch trial (both US data) |
| 2 | **"Did I take it?" / "Did I give it?"** | Every dose, 3–10×/day | Both | Pill box, alarms, the partner who reminds | 14, 25, 27 | High. ~50% non-adherence; crowded reminder-app market is the caveat |
| 3 | **"Everyone wants to know how it's going."** Tell once, ask for help concretely | Daily–weekly | Caregiver-heavy | WhatsApp broadcast, retelling 20 times | 32, 34, 37, 40 | High (behavior). The only inherently multi-person job; ~73% of CaringBridge journals are caregiver-created |
| 4 | **"What does this mean for *me*?"** | Daily after diagnosis, spikes at every result | Both | Google at 2am, ChatGPT, forums | 2, 3, 8, 9 | High. 77% of Dutch people already search health info online; none of it grounded in their own record |
| 5 | **"How am I really doing?"** Mood, fear, exhaustion | Continuous background | Both (caregiver under-screened) | Bottling it up, journaling | 10 | High prevalence (1 in 4 cancer patients clinically depressed; caregiver depression 28–94% by setting) |
| 6 | **"Can I eat / do / plan this?"** | Every meal and plan; weekly salience | Patient | Leaflets, guessing, "ask your team" (then wait weeks) | 5, 24 | Medium-High |
| 7 | **"Who drives, who cooks, what's Tuesday?"** | Weekly clusters | Caregiver + circle | Shared calendar, WhatsApp, the one organized relative | 31, 38 | High burden, Medium EU magnitude |
| 8 | **"What was said, and what do I ask next time?"** | Per appointment: ~weekly on active treatment, monthly–quarterly otherwise | Both | **Ditto today** + paper notes | 1, 4, 15, 20, 21 | High. Our current home; episodic by nature |
| 9 | **"Keep the whole picture."** One record across providers and years | Continuous; fires hard at every transition and new clinician | Caregiver (neuro), patient (MS) | The caregiver's head, a paper folder | 17, 18 | High |
| 10 | **"Who looks after *me*?"** The caregiver's own health | Continuous, owned by nobody | Caregiver | Nothing | (none) | High burden; US-derived mortality magnitudes |

**Wearable enrichment** (hypothesis, Merlijn 2026-06-04, follows board input): wearables are not a route of their own. They slot into job #1: a daily note ("nauseous, skipped lunch, took the 2pm dose") auto-enriched with "heart rate above your normal, sleep score low, strain high." The note becomes richer without extra effort. Evidence for fit: MS fatigue is measured best by 6×/day momentary sampling, which nobody sustains manually; passive signals could carry part of that. Open: desirability untested, and the board flagged MDR risk the moment wearable data feeds interpretation. Status: ideation.

## 3. The circle side: follower jobs

The patient/caregiver side generates updates; the circle side consumes and responds. These jobs are real but mostly **reactive**: they fire when something comes from the patient side.

| Job | Fires when | Read on strength |
|---|---|---|
| Hear how it's going **without pestering the patient** | Every gap between updates | Strong demand; today's "solution" (pinging the patient) actively burdens them |
| **Send something thoughtful** at the right moment | After an update, a scan, a hard week | Merlijn's candidate core action. My read (hypothesis): real, but it's the *reply* in the loop, not the opening move; it can't fire without an update flow already existing. Matches his own "a bit less strong" |
| Offer or take on **concrete help** | Weekly | Converts diffuse goodwill ("let me know if I can do anything") into tasks; today scattered across apps |
| **Understand the condition** privately, without bothering anyone | Early + at every change | Strong; ZC11 (answer circle questions from the patient's context) |
| Be the **second pair of ears** at appointments | Per appointment | Already informal practice everywhere, never systematized |
| **Hand off** between caregivers | Shift changes (neuro, sudden severe) | Niche but acute; ZC38 |

## 4. Top-of-mind frequency vs. job fire-rate

Merlijn's reframe, operationalized. The disease occupies attention near-constantly; the product question is which jobs give that attention somewhere useful to go, and when.

| Trajectory phase | Disease top-of-mind | Supportable jobs firing | Care-system contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oncology, active treatment | Constant (ill 24/7) | Daily: #1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | **~Weekly** (cycles, bloods) |
| Oncology, limbo & survivorship | High (fear, waiting) | Background: #4, 5; few events | Weeks of silence / quarterly |
| Dementia/Parkinson's, mid–late | Constant for the caregiver | Daily: #1, 2, 3, 7, 9, 10 | Monthly-ish (GP, casemanager) |
| MS, relapsing-remitting | Daily (every odd sensation) | Daily: #1, 5; quarterly: DMT thread | 6–12 months neurology |
| Sudden severe, first 6 weeks | Constant | Daily: #1, 2, 8 | Front-loaded, then cliff |

Two honest caveats. The care-contact cadences are typical clinical patterns, not yet verified for NL; check them in the interviews. And the table shows the real shape of the opportunity: **the attention is always there; the system's presence is not.** Whoever fills the gap between contacts owns the daily relationship. That last sentence is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## 5. What this says about the three eigenquestions

A read, not a decision. The decision is the v2 of [source 10](./10-rethinking-the-core-action.md).

**Q1 — Core actions.** The evidence keeps pointing at a small set that fires daily and is badly served: interpret-today (#1), meds (#2), and tell-once (#3), with the appointment job (#8, ours today) as the recurring anchor that already fires near-weekly for on-treatment oncology. The follower-side "send something thoughtful" is the reward half of #3's loop rather than a standalone core action.

**Q2 — Problems.** Per the pain map: five universal clusters, of which daily interpretation and the circle layer are the most underserved high-frequency ones. Comprehension (our wedge) is the headwater problem but episodic.

**Q3 — Primary user.** It genuinely differs by trajectory: **patient** in oncology and MS (cognitively intact, self-managing), **caregiver** in neurodegenerative and the first months of sudden-severe. An oncology beachhead means patient-primary with the partner as the built-in second user; a later neuro expansion flips the account owner to the caregiver. The product has to survive that flip.

## 6. What this catalog cannot answer

| Gap | Why it matters | Cheapest next step |
|---|---|---|
| Medication purchasing & refill logistics (Merlijn's candidate list) | Not covered in any research file | Ask in interviews; check NL pharmacy delivery landscape |
| Explainer modality (podcast-level, infographic) | No evidence either way on format preference | Concept cards in interviews |
| NL magnitudes for US/UK-flagged stats | Several load-bearing numbers (Basch, discharge, adherence) | Spot-check during analytics pull + interviews |
| Wearable-enrichment desirability + MDR line | Elegant in theory, untested, regulatory edge | Concept card + one MDR consult (board: discuss with Łukasz) |
| Private journaling vs. broadcast | CaringBridge data is about broadcasting; private reflection demand is unmeasured | Interviews |
| Whether daily jobs *convert* to daily product use | The whole bet. Burden ≠ adoption (red-team's wounded hypothesis) | The 30-person wizard-of-oz cohort |
