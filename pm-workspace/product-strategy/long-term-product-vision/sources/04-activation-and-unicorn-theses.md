## The activation reframe (strategic-framing)

Two faces of the problem: **acquisition mismatch** (marketing pulls too broad a group, many too healthy to feel the pain) and **activation dependency** (the aha moment needs a doctor appointment Ditto doesn't control). Funnel drop-off is severe (~90% lost between start and a trackable appointment).

**Strategic decision: go deep on cancer first.** Highest appointment frequency, information overload, immediate care-circle activation, highest emotional stakes, longest journey (structural retention), and the largest current active group. The Flo analogy: own one life moment, nail it, then expand.

### Life moments (the segmentation lens)

| Life moment | Why it fits | Circle activation |
|---|---|---|
| Becoming parents | Most appointment-dense; both partners open to new habits | Full |
| Facing serious illness (cancer, cardiac) | Fear suppresses recall; high stakes | Immediate |
| Losing cognitive capacity (dementia, Parkinson) | Caregiver does the cognitive work | Gradual takeover by caregiver |

~95-98% of Dutch adults pass through one; ~20% are in a window at any time.

### The three pillars and four layers of need

- **Pillars**: Clarity (understand what's happening) → Convenience (navigate it) → Connection (inform others). Build bottom-up: clarity creates trust, convenience creates habit, connection creates the loop. Maps to Antonovsky's Sense of Coherence (comprehensibility / manageability / meaningfulness).
- **Four layers of patient need**: informational → psychological → relational → existential. Most health tech serves only layer 1. Ditto's opportunity is to go deeper ("face your care journey as yourself"). Compressed: **Understand. Prepare. Share.**

### The "no appointment" gap

The activation decision tree dead-ends for users without an upcoming appointment. The missing branch is the between-visit value path (voice profile, letter explanation, prep, content). This is the same frequency wound named in [02-growth](./02-growth-and-retention-diagnosis.md), seen from the activation side.

## The unicorn thesis (consumer-layer)

**The premise**: there is no consumer product for navigating healthcare (banking has Revolut, fitness has Strava, healthcare has a 2008 portal). Three structural barriers are weakening at once: data fragmentation (EHDS), episodic usage (AI makes between-episode value possible), and trust (earned at vulnerable moments).

**The core thesis: it's a graph, not a chatbot.** The defensible asset is the **care graph** (who cares about whom, what they need to know, how they coordinate), the social graph of healthcare that exists nowhere today. Product layers: Intelligence (input, not the moat) → Memory (journey, accumulates value) → Connection (care graph, the moat).

**AI as connective tissue, not interface.** The AI doesn't talk TO you (ChatGPT does that); it works BETWEEN you, translating the doctor's words into each circle member's version. The one exception: a contextual chatbot that operates only on the care graph (useless without Ditto's data, so not competing with Big Tech).

**The 50+ audience**: a constraint (techy interfaces create anxiety) and an advantage (every AI feature feels magical; lower bar to wow). Design = voice in, visual out. The bar to beat is "calling my daughter to explain what the doctor said."

**Data completeness reframe**: Ditto's job is **care understanding, not clinical completeness**. Recording + documents + voice captures ~80% of what the circle needs. EHDS resolves the rest by 2029.

**Business model at scale**: B2C (€5-15/mo) + B2B2C insurers (the EU growth accelerator: partner one insurer, reach millions) + EHDS data services + provider partnerships.

## Implications for the long-term vision

- The "consumer layer / care graph" thesis is the most ambitious framing and directly feeds **Route E**. Its honest open question (stated in the doc): can the graph grow organically, and is the insurer channel a growth engine or a dependency?
- The four-layers-of-need and Sense-of-Coherence framing is the emotional spine for whichever route wins; it argues for going beyond layer-1 comprehension.
- "Cancer first, then expand" and "care understanding not clinical completeness" are durable scoping principles.
- The "no appointment" gap is the activation-side restatement of the frequency problem the vision must solve.
