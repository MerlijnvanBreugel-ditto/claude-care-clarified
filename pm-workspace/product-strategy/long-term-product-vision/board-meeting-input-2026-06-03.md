## 1. Wearables × medical context
- Plain wearable data today is fitness- and health-focused. A big and growing trend, but generic.
- The real unlock: **wearable data placed in context with real medical data.** Example: you start a new medication and your resting heart rate rises. That correlation is only meaningful when the wearable stream meets the medical record.
- **Open Wearables** flagged as an interesting party to look into. _[MW: confirm the exact entity/standard.]_
- Action: discuss with **Lucas**. _[MW: possibly Łukasz — voice dictation garbles names.]_
- Caveat: contextualizing and interpreting wearable + medical data takes you "directly into the MDR side" (Medical Device Regulation). _[MW: same Class IIa / AI-Act threshold already flagged in the vision's reimbursement and Route-B notes.]_

## 2. The long-term vision, reframed
- Ditto's goal should not be only growth. It should be **"the most trusted digital solution for your health."**
- Concretely: when you have a medical question, you go to **Ditto instead of Google**, because it gives better, more trusted, context-aware answers.
- End-goal statements voiced in the room:
  - The **first digital solution that comes to mind** when you think about healthcare.
  - The **trusted place to manage your care**, and to see your loved ones and how they are doing.
  - A solution people **do not want to live without if they are seriously ill.**

## 4. North Star — beyond MRR / MAU
- Do not optimize only MRR / MAU.
- Aspirational example offered: **"every other oncology patient in the Netherlands uses Ditto within a week of being diagnosed."** (Roughly 50% of newly diagnosed NL oncology patients, within seven days of diagnosis.) Ties directly to the life-moments lens.

## 5. Monetization
- **B2B is the higher-upside, easier-than-reimbursement route — *if* you can unlock it.** Open question: which promise, and how do you create **lock-in**? What value do you provide the healthcare professional? Candidate value props raised:
  - Replace the patient portal.
  - Be the communication channel between provider and patient.
  - Reduce no-shows.
  - Demonstrate better medication adherence.
- **Pharmacies / pharma (RWD).** Trusted, anonymized data on how people navigate through care; symptom tracking linked to appointments and treatments. The real-world-evidence angle.

## 6. Merlijn's own reflection
- The unlock is probably **use cases with a frequency of daily to weekly at most.** Frequency is the binding constraint.
- A tension to resolve deliberately: **functional needs vs emotional needs.**
  - Functional information needs: "did I take my medication?", "what are the side effects of this treatment?", "is what I'm feeling normal?" These are valuable, but if Ditto serves *only* these, it does not tap the deeper **emotional / underlying human needs.**
- What Merlijn wants to research next (→ [aspirational-playbooks-and-human-needs.md](./sources/09-aspirational-playbooks-and-human-needs.md)):
  1. For people who are seriously ill (oncology, neurology / Parkinson's): **what are the underlying human needs** Ditto could serve, or offer a better alternative to?
  2. **Category-defining companies** (Airbnb, Uber, Spotify, Headspace, and others): did they start from a long-term vision, or build something that solved a problem now and expand from it? How clear was the long-term vision at the very beginning? Was it functional excellence, or unlocking an overlooked / unmet need? What are the parallels for Ditto?
