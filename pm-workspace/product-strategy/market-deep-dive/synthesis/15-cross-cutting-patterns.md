The eight briefs were researched independently and converge hard. Seven patterns hold across the whole field.

## The seven patterns

| # | Pattern | Evidence (briefs) | So what for Ditto |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The patient-pays wall.** Durable money in this entire space is B2B: hospital, insurer, employer, pharma, provider. The only species that sustains user payment is the consumer-habit app (Strava, Flo), and neither is severe-illness. | All 8. Sharpest in F1, F5, F6, F8. | Plan the business as B2B from the start. | 2 | **Everything is a multiplier; nothing here is the spine.** Every adjacent field, run standalone, gets acqui-hired, absorbed, or stays a feature. | Vinehealth→Sciensus (F1), Memora→Commure / Jasper staff cut (F5), PatientsLikeMe→UnitedHealth (F7), Vivy/HealthVault/Google Health (F3), Woebot (F6). | Do not bet the company on tracking, journaling, the record, coordination, social, or the front door as a standalone product. |
| 3 | **The recurring, understood medical moment is the unowned asset.** None of these players has a trusted, recurring, comprehended moment with the patient. That is precisely Ditto's wedge, and it is what every layer compounds on. | F1, F2, F3, F8 all conclude the layer needs an anchor moment Ditto already owns. | The conversation is the till. Everything else is the hook or the multiplier. Sequence outward from the moment. |
| 4 | **Frequency is engineered, never assumed.** The products that retain build a designed loop: self-monitoring in the active-treatment window, the audience pulling the next post (the guestbook), a circle-side one-tap acknowledgement, a human-in-the-loop responder. | F1 (alert loop), F2 (CaringBridge guestbook), F6 (human coach), F7 (Strava witnessing). | A frequency engine on the leaky bucket fails faster. | 5 | **The money-rung is the device-rung.** On the intelligence ladder, value and money concentrate at *individualised advising*. ## The central tension

v2 is patient-first. The patient is the contrarian unlock and the user Ditto is built to serve. But every monetization path this research surfaced is **B2B**: the patient is who you serve, and almost never who pays.

That is the real subject of the strategy session, not "which feature." The session has to resolve **who the payer is** (and accept it is an institution) **without losing the patient-first product soul.** The viable answer is the B2B2C shape used by the few who pulled it off: serve the patient and the circle for free or near-free, monetise the institution on the value that the served patient creates (better comprehension, fewer crisis contacts, adherence, real-world data, channel replacement). The product stays patient-first; the invoice goes to a hospital, insurer, or pharma partner.

This does not weaken v2. It sharpens "monetization as a hard filter" into a specific instruction: **design every layer so that a B2B buyer can see the value the patient is getting.** A layer that delights the patient but produces nothing an institution will pay for (pure social, pure journaling) stays a lean, not a spine.
