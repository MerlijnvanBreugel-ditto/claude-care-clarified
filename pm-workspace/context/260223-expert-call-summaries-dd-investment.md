## 1. SpringTide Ventures
**Date**: 16 February 2026 | **Rubio**: AS, NdR
**Reference**: Michael Levy, Venture Partner at SpringTide Ventures

### Key Takeaways

**1) Don't try to "boil the ocean"**
- Pick a tight persona / sub-segment with strong willingness to pay and repeat need (caregiver / "sandwich generation" / aging as the best wedge)
- Broad "app for everyone" in patient navigation gets crushed by fragmented care + crowded consumer market

**2) Paid acquisition in D2C patient tools is getting expensive**
- Similar consumer-facing clinical intelligence product: CAC started ~USD 17, improved to ~USD 12, struggling below USD 7-8
- Competition from big players (Amazon/One Medical; OpenAI/Anthropic) pushes CAC floor up
- Recommendation: shift to channel acquisition (revenue share with associations/partners/communities) vs pure ads

**3) Activation: measure "time-to-aha!"**
- Most important activation metric: time from download/registration to the first "aha!" moment
- Plausible "aha!" events for Ditto:
  - First high-quality visit summary produced
  - First record consolidation action (if/when introduced)
  - First share with caregiver/family that reduces anxiety/friction
- Emphasized pulling the aha! forward; if value only shows up at the next appointment weeks/months later, you lose them

**4) Retention: you likely need a "daily/weekly reason" even if the core use is episodic**
- Consumer retention is driven by habit. Episodic-only apps get forgotten/deleted
- Build passive or low-friction ongoing engagement (scores, insights, wearables, light check-ins)
- Track 30/60/90-day retention and segment by value prop (daily habit layer vs episodic care-event layer)

**5) What "good" looks like for engagement**
- ~50-60 meaningful engagements/year as a low-end bar (implies weekly-use app)
- If you have a weekly reason, you can probably create a daily reason; without this, long-term retention will be weak

**6) Churn guidance (subscription framing)**
- 30-40% churn over 6 months is "healthy"; beyond that stresses unit economics
- If you can't deliver frequent value, subscription will feel unjustified

**7) Pricing: consider transaction-based until you earn subscription behavior**
- Transaction pricing (pay per doctor-visit / summary / episode) could outperform EUR 7-15/month subscription in perceived fairness

**7A) Insurer monetization**
- Insurers care about: outcomes, integration/interoperability, provider-side advocacy
- "Critical mass" heuristic: 100-500K registered (preferably active) users
- Pilot contract size reference (US): USD 500K for an Aetna pilot

**7B) Pharma monetization**
- Bullish on pharma (being forced closer to patients): clinical trial matching, post-market outcome measurement
- Need data collection that maps to trial inclusion criteria
- Contract size heuristic (60-100K users): USD 50-150K/year

**8) 24-month "Series A readiness" checklist**
1. Pick the wedge persona (caregiver/aging)
2. Define 1-2 aha! moments and optimize time-to-aha as KPI
3. Build a habit layer (weekly/daily value)
4. Test transaction pricing vs. subscription
5. If insurer: plan for outcomes + integration
6. If pharma: design data model for trial matching readiness

### KPI Gap Assessment

| KPI Category | Series A Target | Ditto Today | Gap | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D30 Retention | 12-15% min; ideally ≥20% | Currently at 20% | 🟡 Early validation | Validate via cohorts (D30, D90 flattening) |

## 2. Jaap Maljers
**Date**: 17 February 2026 | **Rubio**: AS

### Summary
Jaap views Ditto as conceptually sympathetic but structurally very difficult to monetize and operationalize at scale. Three main concerns:

**Revenue Model: "Usefulness ≠ Business"**
- Pre-revenue with no proven monetization pathway
- Doubts insurers or pharma as structurally stable sources
- Pharma payments are typically temporary and tactical; risk misaligned incentives
- Pharma should be "means to an end," not core model

**Provider Adoption & Workflow Resistance**
- Strong resistance in clinical practice to additional patient-facing apps (experience: Bergman Clinics, ZorgDomein, Plexus)
- Physicians unlikely to spend time explaining, take responsibility for failures, or endorse tools increasing post-visit friction
- Distribution through doctors may be materially harder than modeled

**Target Group & Behavioral Reality**
- Most likely heavy users: older, less digitally fluent, lower health-literacy groups → slow adopters
- Early positive feedback may reflect curated early adopter base, not mainstream behavior

**AI Commoditization Risk**
- Summarization and explanation easily replicated by general-purpose AI
- Low technological moat unless Ditto builds beyond "explain medical jargon"

**Overall**: Would not invest. Waiting for proof on sustainable revenue model and real-world usage beyond early adopters.

## 3. Bupa UK HQ
**Date**: 19 February 2026 | **Rubio**: AS, NdR
**Reference**: Sophia Guennoun, Group Head of Healthcare Innovation at Bupa

### Key Takeaways

**1) Insurers buy outcomes, not "nice summaries"**
- First reaction: "so what?" — summaries aren't a complete solution unless they drive: better CX (NPS/retention), measurable cost reduction, or prevention/personalization

**2) Scribing is becoming a commodity fast**
- Easy for payers/providers to replicate in-house
- Pitch the "what happens next" layer (personalization engine / workflows / outcomes)

**3) The wedge: chronic + elderly (high interaction frequency)**
- Chronic disease patients (diabetes, cardio) with repeated visits + monitoring
- Elderly/aging with multi-pathology + fragmented care + caregiver involvement

**4) Positioning: "input layer" for digital twin / next-best-action**
- Value is using consolidated data to drive risk scoring, next-best actions, personalization + prevention
- Mentioned Integratome as example partner ("you are the input, they are the output")

**5) Regulatory: avoid medical-device territory via risk scoring**
- No diagnostic claims; use risk scoring + suggestions
- As long as app doesn't diagnose or predict disease, stays safe

**6) Insurer pain points (3 "must-haves")**
- Triage before expensive appointments (deflection)
- Virtual consult access (GP-in-pocket)
- Prevention engagement (step rewards / Vitality-style)

**7) Clearest near-term ROI hook: reduce repeat visits + missed appointments**
- If patients don't understand visit → insurer pays for second visit
- If patients don't follow instructions → churn back into more care
- Feature: appointment follow-up workflow (booking + reminders)
- Think: product evolution from scribe to agent

**8) Procurement friction: privacy + clinical governance gate**
- Insurers hypersensitive about data/premium perception
- Gatekeepers: legal, data privacy, clinical governance, security/IT

**9) Sales motion: long cycles**
- 6-8 months regulatory/security clearance
- Then pilot-first; ROI proof needs up to 12 months
- Assume 12-18 month path to real revenue

**10) Buy vs build risk**
- Summaries + visualization not hard to build in-house
- Need a hook: speed to deploy, governance, data consolidation, caregiver workflow, personalization engine

**11) Commercial packaging: white-label inside insurer app**
- Most plausible: white-labeled feature, flat subscription/licensing fee
- Model as B2B2C, not standalone DTC

**24-month "insurer readiness" checklist:**
- Pick one ICP: chronic or elderly/caregiver
- Add "so what" layer: risk scoring + next best actions and/or appointment workflows
- Make insurer ROI legible: reduce repeat visits, no-shows, improve NPS
- Design privacy separation and clinical governance from day one
- Package as white-label + integration-friendly

## 4. Novartis Global HQ
**Date**: 19 February 2026 | **Rubio**: AS, NdR
**Reference**: Anurag Rai, Former Executive Director, Digital Health Solutions at Novartis

### Key Takeaways

**1) Pharma pays only for things that hit the brand P&L**
- Two categories that work: adherence/post-prescription support (most reliable) and advertising + promotion inside disease-specific platforms (largest budget)
- Everything else usually "interesting" but not commercially scalable

**2) Without advertising, pharma monetization is close to zero**
- Need: compliant ad placements, contextual triggers, measurement
- If company won't build advertising capability, don't underwrite pharma revenue

**3) Belong Life as closest proof case (still not sticky)**
- Novartis → Belong MS: ~USD 200-300K upfront + USD 50K/month for 15-20K users, lasted 2 years then stopped
- Belong was doing ~USD 15M annual turnover
- Contracts churn and don't behave like SaaS

**4) Medisafe as adherence benchmark**
- ~USD 25-35M in sales from pharma engagements
- Adherence is one of few categories pharma repeatedly funds

**5) Mass market scale expectations are brutal**
- Pharma brands think in millions (Leqvio targets 40-50M patients)
- 50-60K users = "irrelevant"
- Need ~20-30% penetration in a meaningful subsegment

**6) Patient-level data is valuable but compliance blocks it**
- Aggregate insights = "nice to have"; real value = patient-level linkage
- Privacy/compliance typically prevents this

**7) Legal/compliance is the main deal-killer**
- ~95% of initiatives killed by: UGC risk, privacy, adverse event reporting, security + breach liability

**8) Sales motion**
- 12 months minimum sales cycle
- Pilots are the norm; contracts 2-3 years max
- Many never scale after pilot (engagement drops)

**9) Underwriting guidance**
- Don't include pharma revenue unless building Belong-style advertising model at real scale

**24-month checklist:**
- Decide: Belong-style ad platform or not?
- Pick a wedge (disease/community) vs "app for everyone"
- Build compliance/security/AEs as core product capability
- Track active engagement, not downloads
- Plan for 12+ month enterprise cycles and pilot churn
