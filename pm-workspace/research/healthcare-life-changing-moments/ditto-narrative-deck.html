The Living Health Companion

Which category can ditto shape? A research-grounded case that the biggest opportunity in serious illness is not documenting the appointment — it's showing up every day, for the patient and the people who love them.

The strategic crux

But the summary has a frequency problem

today's constraint

Low-frequency. Single-player. Externally triggered.

  • Fires only around appointments — often 1–2× / month
  • Depends on the patient remembering to use it
  • No accumulating data, no network → can't compound

A strong action and reward, but a rare trigger. The classic "vitamin utility that stalls" — hard to make habit-forming, hard to grow, hard to monetise.

the question

What do people on these journeys do every day?

You're ill all day — not just at the appointment. The opportunity is the high-frequency daily life of serious illness: symptoms, meds, fear, asking for help, "what can I still do?".

Like Uber or Airbnb, low individual frequency can still compound — if it's multi-player with a data moat.

Four journeys · one shape

Cancer

High volume, high information burden, high caregiver involvement.

The overlooked gaps

  • The patient is the unpaid care coordinator across oncologist, GP, radiology, pharmacy — no one holds the overview
  • Unverified understanding: 69%/81% of metastatic patients didn't grasp chemo wasn't curative
  • Diagnostic limbo — anxiety peaks while waiting, and the system goes silent

The loved one

Caregivers show measurably higher depression, anxiety and insomnia; their #1 unmet need is information — yet they have no formal access to records or the plan. The "update everyone" burden falls on the sickest person.

Sources: Weeks NEJM 2012 (US) · All.Can/ECO · Goren 2014. See findings/02.
Four journeys · one shape

Neurodegenerative — dementia, Parkinson's, ALS

Progressive. The caregiver becomes the primary user and decision-maker.

The overlooked gaps

  • The family is the integration layer — the only complete record lives in the caregiver's head for 8–12 years
  • Proxy/legal/digital access is silently broken as patient capacity declines
  • Flooded at diagnosis, then abandoned — >half get no support referral

The "hidden patient"

~49%
of dementia caregivers carry clinically significant burden; ~33% depression. No one is accountable for the caregiver's own health.

Four journeys · one shape

MS & autoimmune

Young, sudden, lifelong, dominated by uncertainty — the sharpest daily wedge in healthcare.

The overlooked gaps

  • "Is this a flare — should I call my team today?" — a high-anxiety judgement made daily, with no tool that knows the patient's baseline
  • Invisible symptoms (fatigue >80%, cognitive fog) — most disabling, least believed
  • Diagnostic gaslighting of young women; mental-health comorbidity under-detected (~2× suicide risk)

Why it's a strong beachhead

Onset at 20–40 (digital-native, highest lifetime value), tight online communities, near-zero direct competition, and a screaming need to "explain my invisible illness to the people around me."

Sources: MS Trust · MS fatigue meta-analysis · EMSP Barometer. See findings/04.
Four journeys · one shape

Sudden severe — stroke, cardiac, acute onset

Shock, zero preparation, and a sudden lifelong self-management regime.

The overlooked gaps

  • The discharge-comprehension illusion — ~90% say they understood; ~40% can't recall their diagnosis/plan; everyone signs off "patient educated"
  • The broken hand-off — the discharge summary reaches the GP in time only 12–34% of the time
  • The conscripted, untrained caregiver — only ~40% feel confident at discharge; the home transition is "sometimes traumatic"

The unnarrated event

~half of cardiac-arrest survivors have memory loss at 1 year — "what just happened to me?" is literal. Post-event PTSD and depression go largely unscreened.

Sources: Front Communication 2021 · BJGP 2018 · stroke-caregiver studies. See findings/05.

The tailwind — why now

Three unlocks that weren't here three years ago

LLMs

Plain-language comprehension and grounded Q&A are finally cheap and good — exactly what 48% limited health literacy always needed.

EHDS

In force since 26 Mar 2025; patient summaries EU-wide by 2029. It forces your data to you — but explicitly won't explain or organise it. The pipes are laid; nobody owns the tap.

Caregiving at breaking point

~80% of EU long-term care is informal, rising +50% by 2050 — and the "circle" already lives, ungoverned, on WhatsApp.

Plus the regulatory line that defines the safe lane: stay an information / comprehension / coordination tool (output stays with the patient) → not a medical device. Cross into clinical decision support → MDR Class IIa+. DiGA = the one clean reimbursement path, but it requires device status

Sources: EHDS Reg. (EU) 2025/327 (verified) · MDCG 2019-11 · AI Act Art. 50 · BfArM DiGA. See findings/06.
The whitespace

Everyone captures the visit. Nobody owns the life.

What exists

  • AI scribes (Abridge, Nabla, Corti) capture the richest signal — the consultation — but sell to clinicians; the patient is a data source
  • EU patient companions are oncology-only and consolidating (Outcomes4Me + Germany's Mika)
  • Patient portals (Epic MyChart) are the status quo — and being sued in 2025 for fragmenting records
  • Caregiver apps are US-centric and disconnected from care

The unowned space

No scaled, named EU player owns the patient's and family's understanding of their own care — over time, across every provider, every day.

That is the gap. And the summary alone won't hold it: a scribe could bolt one on. The defensible thing is the daily, multi-player relationship.

The contrarian insight

Everyone is building for the doctor's 15 minutes.
Nobody is building for the patient's 15 hours.

The whole industry — EHRs, scribes, even EHDS — optimises the clinical encounter and treats the patient as an episodic visitor and a data source. But serious illness is a life, lived every day, by a frightened person and a small circle who love them — and 99.9% of it happens outside the room, where there is no product.

Same reframe as Spotify (all music, always — not the album) and Airbnb (belonging anywhere — not the booking): change the unit from the transaction to the person's ongoing life.

The category to name & own

The Living Health Companion

A care OS for patients and the people who love them.

before

Healthcare happens to you in episodes you don't understand, can't remember, can't oversee, and carry alone — stitched across WhatsApp, a notes-app symptom list, pill alarms, Dr. Google, a binder of letters, and a portal you can't read.

after

You and your circle have one calm daily companion that helps you understand and manage your illness day to day, lets the people who love you actually help, and turns each clinical encounter into one you walk into prepared and walk out understanding.

The appointment summary isn't the category — it's the proof-of-trust artifact and the periodic crystallisation of the daily relationship. It's the "Wrapped," not the Spotify.

The growth physics

Why this compounds when the summary alone didn't

① Daily trigger → habit

Symptoms, mood and meds fire internally, every day — replacing the rare external trigger of an appointment.

② Personal data moat → retention

Each daily entry makes tomorrow's "is this normal for you?" better; the longitudinal record is switching cost. And it carries a clinical reward, not just convenience.

③ Network effect → CAC collapses

Each patient brings 5–20 loved ones who experience the value and become future seeds. Social rewards (replies, offers of help) are the most habit-forming class.

The Uber/Airbnb answer

They compound at low individual frequency because they're multi-player marketplaces with a data moat. ditto's "circle" is the same structure at human scale.

Where to start

The beachhead

A daily, compounding, multi-player product needs density: high frequency, an active circle, word-of-mouth, long duration, digital-native users.

recommended start

Newly-diagnosed cancer + primary caregiver

Largest, highest-intent inflow; the daily symptom job carries a survival signal; "update everyone" behaviour is most developed here; engaged caregiver; high word-of-mouth density. Exercises all three loops at once.

Risk: the one journey with EU competition; emotionally heavy.

strongest alternative

MS / young-onset autoimmune

Younger, digital, lifelong (highest LTV), the sharpest daily wedge, tight communities, near-zero competition. Smaller inflow, denser engagement, cleaner runway.

Pick this if you weight daily frequency + data moat + low competition over raw reach.

This is the #1 decision to settle with evidence — two concurrent 30-person concierge cohorts — not taste.

The 11-star experience

The day after a diagnosis

You open ditto. It already has yesterday's shattering appointment, rewritten so you actually understand it, with the three questions you forgot queued for next time. It asks one gentle question: "How are you feeling today?" You tap.

It notices your fatigue building across the week and tells you, in plain language, what your own care team said to watch for — and that this isn't yet the thing to call about. One tap, and your sister, partner and two closest friends get a calm update — not the gory details, just "tired today, treatment Thursday, could use a lift." Within an hour your sister has claimed the lift and a friend dropped off dinner. You made no calls and retold the story zero times.

Before Thursday, ditto hands you a one-page brief your oncologist actually thanks you for.

You never "used an app." A companion showed up — for you and the people carrying this with you. That is the category.

The build — ruthlessly sequenced

Vision = the OS. Build = one loop.

Layer onto the existing summary, in this order:

① Daily check-in

The habit engine. Low-effort, adaptive-cadence symptom/mood/med log anchored on "what did my team say to watch for?" — no triage verdict (stays non-device). Goes quiet on bad days; the caregiver can carry it.

② The circle

The growth engine. Permissioned, no-repeat updates + "here's how to help," built on existing proxy rails (DE ePA, NL DigiD Machtigen). Privacy-by-design — a P0.

③ The summary, reframed

Now grounded in a month of real daily data, shareable to the circle in one tap. The crystallisation, not the core.

Explicitly deferred: true clinical triage (until a deliberate CE-marked/DiGA module is justified), full record aggregation, lifestyle/peer-community layers, multi-journey expansion.

What must be true — next steps

Evidence-gated, cheapest first

  • Step 0 (this week, near-free): measure the actual problem — pull ditto's own usage/retention data to quantify the frequency problem before re-architecting around it.
  • Step 1 (concierge): two parallel 30-person cohorts (cancer+caregiver vs MS/autoimmune). Metrics: D30 daily-check-in retention (≥40% on ≥4 days/wk), circle size seeded, and whether any loved one starts their own circle — the network seed.
  • Step 2 (parallel spikes): price-probe who pays (patient / loved-one-as-payer / B2B2C-insurer-DiGA); get a regulatory read that the non-device design stays outside MDR.
  • The gate: don't scale until engagement, a credible payer, and the non-device design are all validated.
Open questions to hold

The question above all others

Are we building a daily companion that happens to make summaries — or a summary tool pretending to be daily?

Every roadmap decision gets tested against one thing: does it increase daily, multi-player engagement? Other open questions: which beachhead compounds fastest · can the non-device "watch-for" feature retain without a verdict · who is the payer · how much value can we deliver from patient-entered data before EHDS flows in 2029.

The vision is the operating system for living with serious illness. The discipline is to earn it one validated loop at a time.

The bet

Change the unit from the appointment to the day, and from the patient to the patient-and-circle.

Stop being "AI summaries of appointments." Become the Living Health Companion — the daily operating system for patients and the people who love them. That is the category-defining flip, and the answer to the frequency problem.
