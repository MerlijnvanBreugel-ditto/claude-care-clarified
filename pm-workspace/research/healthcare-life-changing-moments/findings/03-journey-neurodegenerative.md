# 03 — The Neurodegenerative Disease Journey (Dementia/Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, ALS/MND)

**Project:** Life-Changing Healthcare Moments — ditto.care
**Author:** Patient-experience research (Phase 1, agent C)
**Date / access date for all URLs:** 2026-05-31
**Geography:** Pan-EU, anchored on Netherlands + DACH. US-derived stats flagged inline.

> **Source tiers:** T1 = peer-reviewed / EU-national bodies · T2 = named patient orgs / guidelines · T3 = forums/news (texture, flagged).
> Every non-obvious claim carries an inline citation and a **Confidence** tag.

---

## Key takeaways

1. **The caregiver is the real user.** Across all three diseases, declining patient capacity means a spouse or adult child progressively becomes the primary historian, coordinator, decision-maker and proxy. Dementia caregiving uniquely runs for *years* (often 8-12) of escalating, 24/7 load. Roughly **49% of dementia caregivers meet criteria for clinically significant burden, ~33% for depression, ~35% for anxiety** ([Del-Pino-Casado / umbrella review, ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2950307825000785), T1, High). The caregiver is, in the literature's own phrase, the **"hidden patient."**

2. **Informal care is the actual care system, and it is invisible.** People with dementia in Europe receive **~11.9 billion hours of unpaid care/year**; informal care is **~68% of total dementia cost** (vs 26% institutional, 5% medical) ([Lancet Regional Health Europe 2022](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanepe/article/PIIS2666-7762(22)00139-9/fulltext), T1, High). The entire trajectory is run by an unpaid, untrained, unsupported family member.

3. **The diagnostic odyssey is long and silent.** Dementia averages **~3.5 years from first symptoms to diagnosis** (4.1 for early-onset) ([UCL News 2025](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2025/jul/dementia-takes-35-years-diagnose-after-symptoms-begin), T1, High); ALS **~12-16 months** with frequent alternate diagnoses ([NCBI Bookshelf NBK573423](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK573423/), T1, High); Parkinson's commonly takes multiple appointments over years, and roughly **1 in 6 PD diagnoses is later found inaccurate** ([healthcare-in-europe.com](https://healthcare-in-europe.com/en/news/parkinsons-disease-diagnosis-inaccurate.html), T3/T2, Medium).

4. **"Information overload at diagnosis" then "left to cope alone."** Families are flooded with information at the worst moment, then dropped: **over half of carers are not referred to any support service at diagnosis** ([Alzheimer's Society UK, "Left to Cope Alone"](https://www.alzheimers.org.uk/about-us/policy-and-influencing/left-cope-alone-unmet-support-needs-after-dementia-diagnosis), T2, Medium). The most-cited unmet need is information about *disease trajectory, post-diagnostic care, and practical home support* ([Post-diagnostic care pathways, PMC13002931](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13002931/), T1, Medium).

5. **The "keeping an overview" problem is structural, not personal.** Care is split across neurology, GP, home care, social services, pharmacy and multiple payers, with poor information transfer and no accountable owner. The family becomes the *only* continuous thread — the de-facto integration layer of a fragmented system ([Mind the gap systematic review, PMC12854086](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12854086/), T1, High).

6. **The most overlooked load is administrative + emotional, not clinical.** Bureaucracy is explicitly named by the German Alzheimer society as a top burden ([Deutsche Alzheimer Gesellschaft 2024](https://www.deutsche-alzheimer.de/artikel/zum-bundesweiten-tag-der-pflegenden-angehoerigen-angehoerige-von-menschen-mit-demenz-brauchen-entlastung-auch-von-buerokratie), T2, Medium); anticipatory grief ("mourning someone still alive") affects a majority of caregivers and predicts both burden and acute healthcare use ([JAMDA 2024](https://www.jamda.com/article/S1525-8610(24)00205-6/fulltext), T1, Medium).

## Stage 1 — Early symptoms & the diagnostic odyssey

**The pattern: symptoms normalized, then a long, frustrating hunt for a name.**

- **Dementia: ~3.5 years symptom-to-diagnosis** (4.1y early-onset), per a 2025 UCL-led meta-analysis ([UCL News 2025](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2025/jul/dementia-takes-35-years-diagnose-after-symptoms-begin), T1, High). In a 5-country European study of family carers (covering NL among others), **47% said an earlier diagnosis would have been preferable**; delays were driven by the person's own reluctance, low dementia awareness, professional response, and health-system delays ([Timely diagnosis of dementia, *Int J Geriatr Psychiatry* / PMC6586062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6586062/), T1, High). Adult-child carers and cases where another condition was diagnosed first reported the longest delays.
- **Parkinson's: no definitive test, clinical diagnosis, frequent misattribution.** Diagnosis often takes multiple appointments over several years; young-onset PD typically takes **>2 years** and is frequently misdiagnosed because of the patient's age ([Valasaki 2023, Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1467-9566.13622), T1, Medium). About **1 in 6 PD diagnoses later proves inaccurate** (commonly confused with PSP, MSA, essential tremor) ([healthcare-in-europe.com](https://healthcare-in-europe.com/en/news/parkinsons-disease-diagnosis-inaccurate.html), T3, Medium). Dissatisfaction with the diagnostic pathway rises with delay, lower education, and seeing multiple providers; diagnosis *by a specialist* markedly improves satisfaction, communication sensitivity and time for questions ([Diagnostic pathway survey, PMC5574149](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5574149/), T1, Medium).
- **ALS/MND: ~12-16 months delay, often with a wrong turn first.** Mean **~15.1 months (median ~11)** from first symptom to diagnosis; early referral to an ALS centre cuts this by months ([NCBI Bookshelf NBK573423](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK573423/) and [PMC7775542](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7775542/), T1, High). The delay is uniquely cruel given ALS's short median survival — months lost to the odyssey are a meaningful fraction of remaining life.

**Overlooked pain point — the pre-diagnosis "is it just aging?" limbo.** Families spend years quietly compensating (covering for memory lapses, taking over driving/finances) before anyone names the problem. There is no tool, owner, or record for this period; the family's observations — the single richest diagnostic signal — are never systematically captured. **Confidence: High** (consistent across the dementia and PD literature above).

## Stage 2 — Diagnosis & disclosure

**The pattern: information overload at the exact moment of least capacity to absorb it, followed by a support vacuum.**

- Information given at diagnosis routinely causes **"information overload"** — increasing confusion, misinterpretation, and impaired patient-provider communication ([geriatricians' views, BMC Geriatrics / PMC8858511](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8858511/), T1, Medium). This is the neurodegenerative analogue of ditto's core insight: the consultation is mostly forgotten or misremembered.
- Then the drop-off: **over half of carers are offered no referral** to counselling or community dementia support at diagnosis ([Alzheimer's Society UK, "Left to Cope Alone"](https://www.alzheimers.org.uk/about-us/policy-and-influencing/left-cope-alone-unmet-support-needs-after-dementia-diagnosis), T2, Medium). The dominant unmet needs are **trajectory information, post-diagnostic care navigation, and practical home support** ([Post-diagnostic care pathways, PMC13002931](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13002931/), T1, Medium).
- **NL bright spot to benchmark against:** the Dutch *casemanager dementie* is a named, continuous guide for patient + family and is, since April 2024, insured care from the early "niet pluis" (something's-not-right) phase ([Zorginstituut Nederland 2024](https://www.zorginstituutnederland.nl/actueel/nieuws/2024/04/23/casemanagement-dementie); [Alzheimer Nederland](https://www.alzheimer-nederland.nl/dementie/diagnose/de-casemanager-persoonlijke-begeleiding-bij-dementie), T2, High). But financing is unclear and responsibilities are fragmented, and access varies by region (waiting >6 weeks is reportable to the NZa, implying it happens) ([Regelhulp/VWS](https://www.regelhulp.nl/onderwerpen/ouderen/ondersteuning-bij-dementie), T2, Medium). DACH has no equivalent universal case-manager guarantee.

**Overlooked pain point — early informational support is a leverage point.** A 2025 preprint finds early informational support measurably reduces later family-caregiving burden ([medRxiv 2025.07.25.25332027](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.25.25332027.full.pdf), T1, Low — preprint). The window right after diagnosis is when structured, paced, caregiver-shareable information would have the highest return — and is precisely where the system fails. **Confidence: Medium.**

## Stage 3 — Care planning, legal & financial (advance directives, POA, proxy access)

**The pattern: capacity declines, so legal/financial/digital authority must be transferred — but the mechanics are arcane, time-pressured, and disease-specific (dementia closes the window).**

- **The capacity race.** In progressive dementia, advance care planning must happen *while the person can still consent* — but ACP uptake is low. A German intervention study (Project DECIDE) had to actively offer ACP to increase the number of *valid* advance directives in Alzheimer's patients ([PMC9733090](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9733090/), T1, Medium). German advance directives are described as "not yet common" and "often of questionable validity" ([new German advance-directive law](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/17989/1/oa_17989.pdf), T1, Medium).
- **NL specifics.** A durable *volmacht* (power of attorney) can take effect on incapacity, avoiding the slower/costlier *bewindvoering* (guardianship) route; NL is also distinctive in allowing an **advance euthanasia directive (AED)** to stand in for a verbal request in later-stage dementia — Alzheimer Nederland advises discussing it in ACP as early as possible post-diagnosis ([Frontiers Psychiatry 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.857131/full); [Law&More](https://lawandmore.eu/power-of-attorney/), T2, Medium). This adds an emotionally enormous, time-sensitive decision unique to the Dutch context.
- **AgeQualiDe (DE):** even among the oldest-old, advance directive and health-care POA completion is far from universal ([PMC5390475](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5390475/), T1, Medium).
- **Proxy access to records / portals is a recognized chokepoint.** Proxy enrolment is described as a barrier — downloading/completing forms, and *proving legal-representative status* when the patient lacks capacity (court guardianship or durable POA documentation) ([JMIR Aging 2024](https://aging.jmir.org/2024/1/e59584); [care-partner portal use, PMC10735919](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10735919/), T1, Medium). The EU baseline framework for representing adults who lack capacity rests on the legal representative's consent (e.g. Directive 2001/20/EC lineage) ([Italian legal-agency study, PMC2886844](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2886844/), T1, Low — for the specific portal-mechanics claim).

**Overlooked pain point — proxy/digital access is silently broken.** The caregiver doing all the work usually has *no legitimate, frictionless* access to the patient's records, portal, prescriptions or appointment systems. They either operate in a grey zone (sharing the patient's login) or face bureaucratic proof-of-authority hurdles. This is a concrete, product-shaped gap with real regulatory contours (capacity, consent, GDPR, national POA law). **Confidence: Medium** (mechanics well-evidenced; EU-portal specifics thinner, much portal-access evidence is US/UK).

## Stage 4 — Progressive decline & daily management

**The pattern: the family runs an increasingly medicalized home, mostly untrained, often sleep-deprived, with the hardest symptoms being the *behavioral* ones nobody warned them about.**

- **Burden prevalence (T1, High):** umbrella review of meta-analyses — **~49% burden, ~33% depression, ~35% anxiety** in informal caregivers; burnout and anticipatory grief prominent in those witnessing progressive decline ([ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2950307825000785)). NL Dementiemonitor (Nivel + Alzheimer Nederland, ~4,500 caregivers): roughly **1 in 7 caregivers feel heavily/over-burdened**, up from ~1 in 10 in 2011; **partners living at home are the most burdened** ([Alzheimer Nederland / Nivel Dementiemonitor 2022](https://www.alzheimer-nederland.nl/nieuws/dementiemonitor-mantelzorg-2022-mantelzorger-van-naaste-in-zorginstelling-steeds-meer-in-de); [Nivel Dementiemonitor 2024](https://www.nivel.nl/nl/nieuws/dementiemonitor-2024-zorgbelasting-van-mantelzorgers-van-mensen-met-dementie-blijft-reden), T1/T2, High). NL has ~**800,000 informal dementia caregivers, ~70% women** ([Alzheimer Nederland](https://www.alzheimer-nederland.nl/nieuws/overbelasting-mantelzorger-bij-dementie-neemt-toe), T2, High). Germany: ~**1.4 million** people care for relatives with dementia, burden falling mostly on one woman, near 24/7 ([Deutsche Alzheimer Gesellschaft 2024](https://www.deutsche-alzheimer.de/artikel/zum-bundesweiten-tag-der-pflegenden-angehoerigen-angehoerige-von-menschen-mit-demenz-brauchen-entlastung-auch-von-buerokratie), T2, Medium).
- **BPSD — the overlooked clinical reality.** Behavioral & psychological symptoms (agitation, aggression, nighttime disturbance, apathy, delusions) affect **up to 90% of dementia patients over the disease course**, drive caregiver stress and sleep loss, accelerate nursing-home placement, and raise caregiver psychiatric/cardiovascular illness ([BPSD review, PMC3345875](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3345875/); [Sci Rep 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-18470-8), T1, High). Families are coached on memory and medication but rarely prepared for the behavior — the thing that actually breaks them.
- **Medication management is a quiet, high-risk job.** Polypharmacy hits **~40-90% of dementia patients**; **>54% of carers manage medication, rising to ~95% in moderate-severe dementia**, and **~39% report burden specifically from medication assistance** ([Frontiers Public Health 2022, PMC9638151](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9638151/); [survey study, PMC11603863](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11603863/), T1, High). Refusal, swallowing difficulty, multiple daily doses and "forgot whether I gave it" are the daily failure modes; medication errors in home care are documented and under-supported ([Frontiers Neurology 2021 integrative review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2021.694572/full), T1, Medium).
- **Parkinson's daily reality.** PRISM (6 EU countries, 861 patients/256 carers): high non-motor symptom load (mean NMSQuest ~12.8), impaired QoL, and carers (65% female, 82% spouses) reporting **mild-moderate Zarit burden (mean ~26.6)** ([PRISM, PMC8461669](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8461669/), T1, High). Critically, **non-motor and neuropsychiatric symptoms (mood, apathy, cognition) predict caregiver burden more strongly than motor symptoms** ([scoping review PMC10802092](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10802092/); [neuropsychiatric/QoL study PMC8774188](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8774188/), T1, High) — yet motor symptoms get the clinical attention.
- **ALS daily reality.** As respiratory/bulbar function fails, caregivers manage cough assist, suctioning, ventilators, tracheostomy and feeding at home; supports are explicitly described as lacking, and care can exceed **100 hours/week** ([Living with ALS, NBK607593](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK607593/); [ALS caregiving burden, PMC5011853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5011853/), T1, Medium).

**Overlooked pain point — the caregiver is themselves at risk and unmonitored.** Spousal caregivers with strain show **~63% higher mortality**, rising to >4x with strain + their own disease ([Schulz & Beach lineage, "Till death do us part," PMC7187632](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7187632/), T1, Medium — *US data, EU applicability uncertain*). Caregiver mental stress is even associated with faster patient decline ([Berkeley News 2017](https://news.berkeley.edu/2017/06/26/caregiver-mentalstress/), T3 reporting on T1, Low/Medium — *US data*). The system has no view of, and no duty toward, the "hidden patient." **Confidence: Medium** (effect robust; magnitudes largely US-derived).

## Stage 5 — Coordination across neurology / GP / home care / social services

**The pattern: care is split across medical AND social systems with separate funding, records and logic; the family is the only continuous integrator — unpaid, untrained, and without access.**

- People with dementia "often experience care fragmentation… poor information transfer between providers, inadequate discharge planning, insufficient caregiver support, and lack of follow-up" ([ASPE research summary](https://aspe.hhs.gov/reports/research-care-coordination-people-dementia-family-caregivers-0), T2, Medium — *US source*). A 2025/26 systematic review of care transitions finds **fragmented governance, funding/policy inconsistency, and structural coordination failures** as the systemic drivers, partly mitigated only by proactive family planning ([Mind the gap, PMC12854086](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12854086/), T1, High).
- Caregivers explicitly act as the integration layer: **managing appointments, arranging transport, and relaying information between professionals to maintain continuity** ([Frontiers Aging scoping review 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging/articles/10.3389/fragi.2025.1578911/full), T1, Medium). They "must navigate multiple, evolving, increasingly complex systems, often without assistance."
- Case management *works* when it exists — reducing caregiver depression, burden and unmet needs ([primary-care case management, PMC4220234](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4220234/), T1, Medium) — but is inconsistently funded/available even in the NL (see Stage 2/Sources). Research nurses themselves found documentation burdensome and identified *fewer* unmet needs than researchers did — i.e., even professionals miss needs.

**Overlooked pain point — the family is doing unpaid systems-integration with no shared record.** Medical (neurology, GP, hospital, pharmacy) and social care (home care, day care, municipality/Wmo in NL, Pflegekasse in DE) do not share a record or a plan. The only place the full picture exists is in the caregiver's head and a folder of paper. This is the clearest "keeping an overview" gap. **Confidence: High.**

## Stage 6 — Institutionalization & end-of-life

**The pattern: the placement decision is drenched in guilt and made under-informed; end-of-life decisions land on a proxy who was never prepared.**

- Placement guilt is near-universal and socially reinforced: **nearly half of caregivers report guilt** from the care recipient, family, or facility staff; feelings of failure, shame and stigma dominate, often without decision support ("We moved her too soon") ([PMC9897423](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9897423/), T1, Medium). Pre-decision regret is documented *before* the move even happens ([PMC8958637](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8958637/), T1, Medium). NL Dementiemonitor specifically flags that caregivers of nursing-home residents are *increasingly* squeezed — placement does not end the burden ([Nivel Dementiemonitor 2022](https://www.nivel.nl/nl/nieuws/mantelzorgers-van-mensen-met-dementie-die-een-zorginstelling-wonen-steeds-meer-de-knel), T1, High).
- End-of-life decisions fall on unprepared proxies: **"insufficient information about disease course, combined with guilt and failure, leaves caregivers unprepared to make end-of-life decisions"**; they lack both informational and emotional support and need help understanding trajectory and comfort-care options ([end-of-life decisions for institutionalized dementia patients, ScienceDirect S0897189716000367](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0897189716000367), T1, Medium).
- In the NL, the advance euthanasia directive can make this even heavier — a proxy may have to act on (or interpret) a directive written years earlier, a genuinely contested ethical situation ([Frontiers Psychiatry 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.857131/full); [AED Dutch case, PMC9828198](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828198/), T1, Medium).

**Overlooked pain point — anticipatory grief is a years-long, unsupported state.** Caregivers grieve a living person across the entire decline. In MND, **~73% show grieving reactions (50.7% common, 22.6% intense)**, predicted by disease severity and behavioral change ([factors affecting anticipatory grief in MND, PMC11523914](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11523914/), T1, Medium); in severe dementia, caregiver burden + anticipatory grief **increase acute healthcare use** ([JAMDA 2024](https://www.jamda.com/article/S1525-8610(24)00205-6/fulltext), T1, Medium). This emotional load is real, measurable, has downstream system costs — and is essentially unaddressed by any product or pathway. **Confidence: Medium.**

## Most overlooked problems (ranked)

1. **Proxy / legal / digital access is silently broken (Stage 3).** The person doing 100% of the work has no clean, legitimate access to records, portals, prescriptions or appointments; capacity loss turns this into a bureaucratic and GDPR maze. Product-shaped, regulation-shaped, universal. *Confidence: Medium.* ([JMIR Aging 2024](https://aging.jmir.org/2024/1/e59584))

2. **The family IS the integration layer — with no shared record (Stage 5).** Medical and social care don't share a plan; the only complete picture lives in the caregiver's head. "Keeping an overview" across 8-12 years is the defining unmet need. *Confidence: High.* ([Mind the gap, PMC12854086](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12854086/))

3. **The caregiver is the "hidden patient" — unmonitored, at elevated mortality (Stage 4).** ~49% clinically significant burden; spousal strain linked to ~63% higher mortality. No one is accountable for the caregiver's own health. *Confidence: Medium (magnitudes US-derived).* ([ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2950307825000785); [PMC7187632](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7187632/) — *US*)

4. **Information overload at diagnosis → "left to cope alone" (Stage 2).** Flooded then abandoned; >half get no support referral; the highest-leverage window (early informational support) is exactly where the system fails. *Confidence: Medium.* ([Alzheimer's Society "Left to Cope Alone"](https://www.alzheimers.org.uk/about-us/policy-and-influencing/left-cope-alone-unmet-support-needs-after-dementia-diagnosis))

5. **Behavioral symptoms + medication management — the daily reality nobody prepares them for (Stage 4).** BPSD affects up to 90%; ~95% of carers manage meds in moderate-severe dementia with ~39% burdened by it. Families are trained on the wrong things. *Confidence: High.* ([BPSD review, PMC3345875](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3345875/); [Frontiers 2022, PMC9638151](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9638151/))

*Runners-up:* the pre-diagnosis "is it just aging?" limbo where the family's observations are never captured (Stage 1); anticipatory grief as a years-long unsupported state with measurable system cost (Stage 6); bureaucracy itself named as a top burden by the German Alzheimer society (Stage 4/5).

## Low-confidence and US-derived claims (handle with care)

- **Caregiver mortality magnitudes (+63%, >4x).** From US cohort work (Schulz & Beach lineage). Direction almost certainly holds in the EU; exact magnitudes uncertain. *US data — EU applicability uncertain.* ([PMC7187632](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7187632/))
- **Caregiver stress → faster patient decline.** Reported via Berkeley/US study; T3 reporting on a T1 study. *US data — EU applicability uncertain.* ([Berkeley News 2017](https://news.berkeley.edu/2017/06/26/caregiver-mentalstress/))
- **Care-coordination fragmentation specifics.** Several framing quotes are from US ASPE; the EU systematic-review evidence (PMC12854086) corroborates the *direction* strongly. ([ASPE](https://aspe.hhs.gov/reports/research-care-coordination-people-dementia-family-caregivers-0))
- **"1 in 6 Parkinson's diagnoses inaccurate."** T3 news framing of clinico-pathological accuracy studies; figure varies by study/era — treat as directional. ([healthcare-in-europe.com](https://healthcare-in-europe.com/en/news/parkinsons-disease-diagnosis-inaccurate.html))
- **"Early informational support reduces burden."** medRxiv **preprint**, not peer-reviewed. ([medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.25.25332027.full.pdf))
- **Patient-portal proxy-access mechanics.** Much of the granular evidence is US/UK; EU/NL/DACH portal specifics are thinner and should be confirmed against national infrastructure (e.g. NL MedMij/PGO, DE ePA) in the policy findings file. ([JMIR Aging 2024](https://aging.jmir.org/2024/1/e59584))
- **Burden % figures** carry instrument/threshold sensitivity (Zarit cut-offs vary); ranges, not point estimates, are the honest read.

## Context anchors (epidemiology, for the synthesis file)

- **Dementia, EU27:** ~7.85m (2020) → projected ~14.3m by 2050; women ~2:1 ([Alzheimer Europe 2020 report via EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/815689); [Alzheimer Europe prevalence page](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/dementia/prevalence-dementia-europe), T1/T2, High). Note a newer Alzheimer Europe report (Jan 2026) projects a **~64% increase by 2050** ([Alzheimer Europe 2026](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/new-alzheimer-europe-report-projects-64-increase-dementia-across-europe-2050), T2, High).
- **Dementia cost, Europe:** ~€392bn (2019, WHO via Lancet); informal care **~68% of total** ([Lancet Reg Health Eur 2022](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanepe/article/PIIS2666-7762(22)00139-9/fulltext), T1, High); **~11.9bn unpaid care hours/yr** ([same](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanepe/article/PIIS2666-7762(22)00139-9/fulltext)).
- **NL dementia:** ~280,000 (2020) → ~625,000 by 2050 (+121%) ([Alzheimer Nederland](https://www.alzheimer-nederland.nl/nieuws/overbelasting-mantelzorger-bij-dementie-neemt-toe), T2, High).
- **NL Parkinson's:** ~67,000 (2022), set to roughly double 2018→2040 (to ~82,600); ~1 in 22 lifetime risk; M:F ~60:40 ([Hersenstichting](https://www.hersenstichting.nl/hersenaandoeningen/parkinson/); [ParkinsonNederland](https://www.parkinsonnederland.nl/over-parkinson/wat-is-parkinson/); [RIVM/Hersenstichting via Kennisnetwerk CVA](https://kennisnetwerkcva.nl/news_cats/tot-2040-explosieve-stijging-aantal-nederlanders-met-dementie-parkinson-en-beroerte/), T1/T2, High). Parkinson's is the world's fastest-growing brain disease ([Radboudumc 2021](https://www.radboudumc.nl/nieuws/2021/er-zijn-zeven-miljoen-ziektes-van-parkinson), T2, High).
