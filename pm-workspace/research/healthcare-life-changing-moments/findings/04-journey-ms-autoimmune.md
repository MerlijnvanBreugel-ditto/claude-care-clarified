# 04 — The MS & Autoimmune Disease Journey (MS anchor; lupus/SLE, rheumatoid arthritis, IBD/Crohn's & colitis, type-1 diabetes)

**Project:** Life-Changing Healthcare Moments — ditto.care
**Author:** Patient-experience research (Phase 1, agent D)
**Date / access date for all URLs:** 2026-05-31
**Geography:** Pan-EU, anchored on Netherlands + DACH. US-derived stats flagged inline.

> **Source tiers:** T1 = peer-reviewed / EU-national bodies · T2 = named patient orgs / guidelines · T3 = forums/communities/news (texture, flagged — valuable for daily lived experience).
> Every non-obvious claim carries an inline citation and a **Confidence** tag.

> **Why this journey is different from cancer or neurodegeneration:** these diseases hit people **young (20-40)**, arrive as a **sudden, lifelong, identity-shattering diagnosis**, and are then governed by **uncertainty for decades** — relapsing-remitting unpredictability, invisible symptoms (fatigue, pain, cognitive "fog"), and the daily question *"is this a flare?"*. The patient is usually cognitively intact and self-managing — so unlike dementia, **the patient is the user, every day, for 40+ years.** This is a **high-frequency daily-life-management journey, not an episodic one.**

---

## Key takeaways

1. **The diagnostic odyssey is long, and it is disproportionately a story of women being disbelieved.** MS: mean ~18 months symptom-to-diagnosis, much longer in some EU cohorts (Italy mean 33.8 months; Spain ~19 months just to first physician visit) ([Diagnostic delay of MS, PMC10580682, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10580682/), T1, High). Lupus is worse: ~2 years on average, median **47 months** from first symptom, ~50% initially misdiagnosed, and women's "nonspecific" complaints are routinely dismissed ([Understanding impact of delayed/misdiagnosis of SLE, PMC11668484, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668484/), T1, High). MS is misdiagnosed *as a psychiatric disorder* often enough that documented cases span decades ([Three Decades of Drift, J Neuropsychiatry 2014](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.neuropsych.13030063), T1, Medium).

2. **The defining feature is uncertainty, not disability.** The everyday cognitive load is the question *"is this new symptom a real relapse, a pseudo-relapse, or nothing?"* — a judgement patients are expected to make themselves, against a "24-hour rule" and triggers like heat/stress/infection, before deciding whether to "bother" their MS team ([MS Trust: relapse vs pseudo-relapse](https://mstrust.org.uk/news/expert/it-ms-relapse-pseudo-relapse-or-flare), T2, High). This is a **daily, high-frequency self-surveillance burden** with no episodic equivalent in cancer.

3. **The dominant symptom is invisible.** Up to **90% of people with MS have fatigue**, and roughly **one-third name it their single most disabling symptom** — yet it, and cognitive "brain fog," are invisible to employers, colleagues and family ([Determinants of fatigue in MS, PMC10672347](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10672347/), T1, High; [Tired minds, normal scores, Front Neurol 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2025.1664734/full), T1, High). The burden of *explaining* an invisible illness is itself a recurring, exhausting task.

4. **DMT decisions are high-stakes, preference-sensitive, and never "done."** There is a growing menu of disease-modifying therapies differing in route, monitoring, safety and efficacy; ~1 in 4 switches is driven by side effects, ~1 in 5 by perceived lack of efficacy — so the decision recurs over the disease course ([Shared decision-making in MS, Front Neurol 2022 / PMC9691958](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9691958/), T1, High). For young women, DMT choice collides head-on with **family planning**.

5. **Mental-health comorbidity is the rule, not the exception.** Lifetime depression prevalence in MS is ~**50%**, point prevalence ~27%, and **suicide risk is roughly double** the general population — frequently underdetected because low mood is written off as "understandable" ([Prevalence/treatment of depression in MS, MSARD 2024](https://www.msard-journal.com/article/S2211-0348(24)00225-6/fulltext), T1, High; [Depression & suicidality in MS, PMC6713681](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6713681/), T1, High).

6. **The young loved-one is an invisible, under-supported workforce.** MS partners are typically young, building careers, often parents of small children; ~68% of MS caregivers report a perceived care burden, and both patient and partner describe "suffering in silence" ([Profile and burden of the family caregiver in MS, PMC10964650, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10964650/), T1, High).

7. **"Keeping an overview" runs for decades across a fragmented system.** Neurology, MS nurse, GP, rehab, ophthalmology, urology, psychology, pharmacy and DMT-monitoring labs are rarely integrated; **65% of people with MS in Europe are not on any national MS registry**, and only ~48% are employed ([EMSP MS Barometer](https://emsp.org/projects/ms-barometer-2025-2027/), T2, High). The patient is the only continuous record of their own 30-year history.

## Epidemiology & framing (EU-anchored)

- **MS scale:** ~2.8 million people worldwide; Western Europe has the highest regional prevalence (~127/100,000) ([Atlas of MS 3rd ed., MSIF](https://www.atlasofms.org/), via [MS News Today summary](https://multiplesclerosisnewstoday.com/news-posts/2020/09/21/ms-prevalence-2-8-million-worldwide-atlas-ms-third-edition/), T2, High). **Netherlands: ~36,500** people with MS (2025 GP+hospital linkage study — higher than previously assumed) ([Amsterdam UMC / Nivel / Rijnstate, via msinbeeld.nl 2025](https://www.msinbeeld.nl/2025/01/13/ms-prevalentie-nederland-36500-patienten/), T1/T2, High). **Germany: >280,000**, with **~15,000 new diagnoses/year** ([DMSG](https://www.dmsg.de/multiple-sklerose/was-ist-ms/), T2, High).
- **Young onset, female-predominant:** typical onset **20-40 years**; female:male ratio ~2-3:1 and rising ([Lancet Reg Health Eur 2024, emerging epidemiology](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanepe/article/PIIS2666-7762(24)00144-3/fulltext), T1, High; DMSG: women ~2× men, T2, High). MS is the **leading non-traumatic cause of neurological disability in young adults** ([Frontiers GBD analysis 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2025.1616245/full), T1, High).
- **Autoimmune clustering:** ~25% of people with one autoimmune disease develop at least one more ([CreakyJoints/EULAR 2019 summary](https://creakyjoints.org/eular-2019/type-1-diabetes-ibd-increase-rheumatoid-arthritis-risk/), T3, Medium) — so "keeping an overview" often means managing *several* lifelong conditions at once.

## Stage 1 — The diagnostic odyssey (long delays, dismissed symptoms — especially for women)

**The pattern: vague, fluctuating, invisible symptoms get normalized or psychologized; the patient is sent away repeatedly; a name arrives only after months-to-years.**

- **MS delay:** mean time from first medical visit to diagnosis ~**15 months**; symptom-onset to diagnosis ~**18 months**, with EU cohorts far longer (Italy mean **33.8 months**, median 19.5; Spain ~**19 months** *just to reach a physician*) ([Diagnostic delay of MS, PMC10580682, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10580682/), T1, High). **Sensory symptoms at onset** (numbness, tingling, "weird" sensations) — the ones easiest to dismiss — predict the *longest* delays ([same; also Factors driving delayed MS diagnosis, ScienceDirect 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2211034821006283), T1, High).
- **The psychiatric misattribution trap:** depression/anxiety are elevated *before* MS diagnosis (pre-diagnostic OR ~1.4 for depression/anxiety; higher antidepressant use) — meaning early MS is frequently labelled a mental-health problem, delaying the real diagnosis ([Medical News Today summary of prodrome research](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/depression-anxiety-bipolar-among-early-signs-of-multiple-sclerosis-ms), T3, Medium; [Three Decades of Drift, J Neuropsychiatry 2014](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.neuropsych.13030063), T1, Medium — documents a woman treated as "histrionic personality disorder + depression" for 32 years before MS).
- **Lupus — the starkest gaslighting story:** ~**50% report an initial misdiagnosis**; average delay ~**2 years**, median **47 months** from first symptom, up to 6+ years in some cohorts; women's complaints "often dismissed because of the nonspecific nature" ([Understanding impact of delayed/misdiagnosis of SLE, PMC11668484, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668484/), T1, High). The **German LuLa cohort** found delayed diagnosis adversely affects outcomes — DACH-relevant ([LuLa cohort, PMC7933718](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7933718/), T1, Medium — abstract-level; full text not retrievable). A US qualitative study describes the lived "lupus limbo" of seeking a diagnosis ([Lived experiences of women seeking SLE diagnosis, ScienceDirect 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S155541552200037X), T1, Medium) *(US — EU applicability uncertain)*.
- **IBD/Crohn's:** median diagnostic delay **~2-5.3 months** (longer for Crohn's than colitis), worse outcomes when delayed; symptoms misattributed to "mild/transient" causes, plus embarrassment and mistrust ([Diagnostic delay in adult IBD systematic review, PMC10038954](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10038954/); [Sources of diagnostic delay, qualitative, PMC11164383](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11164383/), T1, High). **Sex disparity:** women with IBD have longer diagnostic delay and different treatment trajectories ([Sex disparities in IBD treatment trajectories, PMC12260162](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12260162/), T1, Medium).
- **Even the diagnosis itself is uncertain.** MS has no single test; the McDonald criteria rely on MRI + clinical judgement, and **~1 in 5 people carrying an MS diagnosis don't meet criteria and have a more likely alternative** ([Incidence of MS misdiagnosis in referrals, PubMed 30738280, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30738280/), T1, High). The **2024 McDonald revision** allows diagnosis in *asymptomatic* people with suggestive MRI — improving early treatment but raising **overdiagnosis** risk ([2024 McDonald criteria overdiagnosis risk, Nature Rev Neurol 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41582-025-01140-4), T1, High). So patients can be left in diagnostic *limbo* (CIS / "radiologically isolated syndrome") for years.

**Overlooked pain point — there is no owner or record for the multi-year "something is wrong but no name" phase.** The patient's own log of fluctuating, invisible episodes is the single richest diagnostic signal, yet it is never systematically captured, and is actively *discounted* when it comes from a young woman. **Confidence: High** (consistent across MS, lupus, IBD literature above).

## Stage 2 — Diagnosis & shock at a young age

**The pattern: a 28-year-old in the middle of building a career/family is told they have an incurable, unpredictable, lifelong brain/immune disease — then largely sent home to figure it out.**

- MS is "most often diagnosed between 20 and 40," precisely when people are forming careers, partnerships and families ([Lancet Reg Health Eur 2024](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanepe/article/PIIS2666-7762(24)00144-3/fulltext), T1, High). The diagnosis lands as identity rupture: a future imagined as healthy is suddenly governed by "what if I end up in a wheelchair?" — a fear amplified by outdated public images of MS.
- The diagnostic conversation is information-dense at the moment of least absorption (the same consultation-recall problem ditto targets), and crucially is immediately followed by a **treatment decision** (see Stage 3) the newly-diagnosed person is unequipped to make.
- **Mental-health onset is immediate:** a structured-interview study found **28.5% had suicidal thoughts since their MS diagnosis** ([Suicidal ideation/anxiety/depression in MS, PubMed 29898075](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29898075/), T1, Medium). The early period is high-risk and under-monitored.

**Overlooked pain point — the "young diagnosis" peer-isolation gap.** Patients are often the only person their age they know with a serious chronic illness; their friends are not dealing with anything comparable, and family struggles to grasp an invisible disease. Structured, paced, *shareable* information for the patient and their close circle in the weeks after diagnosis is exactly where the system goes quiet. **Confidence: Medium** (inferred from young-onset demographics + caregiver "suffering in silence" findings; direct EU-quantified evidence thinner).

## Stage 3 — Disease-modifying therapy (DMT) decisions

**The pattern: immediately after a shock diagnosis, the patient must choose among many complex, lifelong drugs — a recurring, preference-sensitive decision with real trade-offs.**

- DMTs "do not cure"; they reduce relapse frequency and slow progression, and **differ in route, timing, administration, safety and tolerability** — from injectables to infusions to oral high-efficacy agents with serious monitoring needs ([Shared decision-making in MS, PMC9691958, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9691958/), T1, High; [Sequence of DMTs, PMC5688209](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5688209/), T1, High).
- The decision **recurs**: reasons for switching include **adverse effects (~26%), perceived lack of efficacy (~19%), new options (~14%), financial (~7%)** ([MyMSTeam: switching DMTs, summarising registry data](https://www.mymsteam.com/resources/switching-ms-treatments-when-and-why-it-might-be-time-to-switch), T3, Medium). So "the DMT conversation" is not one event but a thread across decades.
- Patients want a say: in the NARCOMS registry, ~48% preferred patient-centred decision-making and ~43% shared decision-making — i.e. the overwhelming majority want active involvement, which requires understanding complex trade-offs ([SDM & autonomy in NARCOMS, PMC5734714](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5734714/), T1, Medium) *(US registry — preference patterns likely transferable, exact numbers EU-uncertain)*.
- **The family-planning collision:** most people with MS are of child-bearing/-fathering age, and several DMTs are contraindicated or must be paused around pregnancy — so a 30-year-old woman must weave "do I want children, and when?" into "which lifelong drug do I start?" ([Family planning decision-making in MS, PMC8113643](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8113643/); [Patient-centred pregnancy planning in MS, PMC11500293](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11500293/), T1, High).

**Overlooked pain point — DMT side-effect / monitoring tracking is a silent ongoing chore.** Many DMTs require ongoing blood/MRI monitoring, injection-site rotation, and vigilance for specific red-flag side effects — a structured, recurring task the patient owns largely alone, with no good shared overview tool. **Confidence: Medium-High.**

## Stage 4 — Daily symptom & flare management; relapse/remission uncertainty

**The pattern: every day brings the question "is this normal-for-me, or is this a flare?" — a self-triage decision with no clear threshold, plus a constant, invisible symptom load.**

- **The "is this a relapse?" decision is offloaded to the patient.** Guidance asks them to apply a **24-hour rule** (true relapse = new/worsening symptoms persisting >24h, from inflammation) and to distinguish it from a **pseudo-relapse / pseudoexacerbation** (temporary worsening from heat, stress, infection, or fatigue, resolving when the trigger clears) — and to first rule out a UTI before "bothering" the team ([MS Trust: relapse, pseudo-relapse or flare-up](https://mstrust.org.uk/news/expert/it-ms-relapse-pseudo-relapse-or-flare); [MS Society: managing relapses](https://www.mssociety.org.uk/about-ms/types-of-ms/relapsing-remitting-ms/managing-relapses), T2, High). This is genuinely hard: the same symptom can mean "do nothing," "call the nurse," or "go to A&E."
- **Invisible symptoms dominate daily life.** Fatigue affects up to **90%**; ~1/3 call it their worst symptom; it independently reduces work capacity and quality of life ([Determinants of fatigue, PMC10672347](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10672347/); [Work capacity & QoL reduced by fatigue, PMC3606119](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3606119/), T1, High). **Cognitive fatigue / "cog fog"** is "one of the most common and disabling symptoms, yet difficult to measure" — patients can score normal on tests while thinking itself is exhausting ([Tired minds, normal scores, Front Neurol 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2025.1664734/full), T1, High; [MS Australia: cognition](https://www.msaustralia.org.au/symptom/cognition/), T2, High).
- **Heat sensitivity (Uhthoff's phenomenon)** means a hot day, a fever, or exercise can transiently resurface old symptoms — feeding the daily "is this real?" ambiguity ([covered in MS Trust pseudo-relapse guidance], T2, High).
- **Self-tracking exists but is fragmented and low-adherence.** A symptom diary is the recommended self-management tool to "spot patterns and possible relapses," but pen-and-paper diaries suffer "low adherence, misplacement, time-consuming analysis," and the app landscape (MyTherapy, My MS Manager, MS-UK diary) is splintered and not integrated with the care team's records ([MS-UK symptom diary](https://ms-uk.org/ms-symptoms-diary/); [MS Trust: diary of symptoms](https://mstrust.org.uk/a-z/diary-symptoms); [MSAA My MS Manager](https://mymsaa.org/news/ms-manager-app-features/), T2, High).

**Overlooked pain point — the daily self-surveillance burden is the journey's defining, highest-frequency load, and it is almost entirely unsupported.** No tool reliably helps the patient answer "is this a flare, and should I call my team today?" with their own longitudinal baseline. This is the single strongest daily, recurring problem in the whole journey. **Confidence: High.**

## Stage 5 — Work, relationships & family-planning impact

**The pattern: a young person must repeatedly decide whether to disclose an invisible illness, while quietly losing earning power, and renegotiate relationships and parenthood under uncertainty.**

- **Employment loss is large and early:** only ~**48% of people with MS in Europe are employed** ([EMSP MS Barometer](https://emsp.org/projects/ms-barometer-2025-2027/), T2, High); ~**50% of workers with MS experience reduced work ability from disease onset** and many eventually lose or quit jobs, with female sex and higher disability predicting unemployment ([Determinants of early working impairments in MS, PMC9784221](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9784221/); [Predictors of unemployment in RRMS, PMC9213301](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9213301/), T1, High). Employment loss is prospectively linked to **worse mental-health quality of life** ([Predictors of change in employment & QoL, PMC7031412](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7031412/), T1, High).
- **Workplace disclosure is a recurring dilemma, not a one-off.** A Swedish population-based survey found ~**85% disclose** at work, but the dominant reason *not* to disclose is **fear of discrimination**; drivers span work, social, personal and circumstantial factors ([Disclosing or concealing MS at work, Sweden, PMC10925750](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10925750/), T1, High). Because symptoms are invisible, the patient must actively *perform* explanation and proof of an illness others can't see ([Stigma/discrimination/disclosure systematic review, PMC9367867](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9367867/), T1, Medium).
- **Family planning:** fertility and pregnancy course are generally not harmed by MS itself, but DMT timing, relapse-risk perception, and uncertainty create real unmet need around contraception, conception timing and pregnancy planning ([Fertility preferences & unmet need for family planning in women with MS, PMC9682175](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9682175/), T1, High).
- **Loved ones, young:** MS care-partners are disproportionately young husbands/partners, often parents of small children and early in their careers; spouses report losing "their partner and their sense of self, companionship, support, income," and ~**68% report perceived care burden** — yet this is "invisible" labour ([Care partners and MS: differential effect on men and women, PMC4673917](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4673917/); [Profile and burden of family caregiver in MS, PMC10964650, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10964650/), T1, High).

**Overlooked pain point — "explaining the invisible illness" is itself a chronic, energy-draining task.** Patients spend finite energy (which fatigue has already depleted) justifying their condition to employers, colleagues, friends and even family who "can't see anything wrong." A shareable, credible way to convey current status to a chosen circle is a concrete, product-shaped gap. **Confidence: High.**

## Stage 6 — Long-term progression & "keeping an overview" over decades

**The pattern: a 40-year disease threading through many specialists, drug switches, life events and slow accumulation — with the patient as the only continuous record.**

- **Fragmentation is structural.** Care spans neurology, MS nurse, GP, rehab/physio, ophthalmology, urology/continence, psychology, pharmacy and DMT-monitoring labs, rarely integrated; **65% of people with MS in Europe are not on any national MS registry**, so even the *system's* longitudinal record is incomplete ([EMSP MS Barometer](https://emsp.org/projects/ms-barometer-2025-2027/), T2, High). "Huge and unacceptable inequalities" in timely access to care/HCPs/social support across Europe are explicitly documented ([EMSP, via YouMove Europe petition](https://action.wemove.eu/sign/2023-12-quality-access-to-care-for-people-with-multiple-sclerosis-and-ne-EN), T2, Medium).
- The patient must remember and re-tell their own 10-, 20-, 30-year history (relapse dates, prior DMTs and why each was stopped, MRI changes, EDSS scores) at every new appointment and to every new clinician — the classic ditto consultation-recall + overview problem, but stretched across decades and many disciplines.
- **Transition to progressive MS** is itself an uncertain, poorly-signposted shift (relapsing-remitting → secondary progressive), often recognised only in hindsight — there is no clear "you have crossed a line" moment, compounding the long-run uncertainty.

**Overlooked pain point — there is no durable, patient-owned, shareable "MS passport."** The lifelong, multi-specialty, multi-DMT history lives in the patient's head and scattered portals. Reconstructing it is high-effort and error-prone, and no single owner holds it. **Confidence: High.**

## Daily / high-frequency self-management burdens

Ranked by frequency and how *normalized-but-high-impact* (and unsupported) they are:

1. **The daily "is this a flare?" self-triage.** Highest-frequency cognitive load in the whole journey. Patient must apply the 24-hour rule, weigh triggers (heat, stress, infection, fatigue, sleep), rule out a UTI, and decide whether to log it, call the MS nurse, or escalate — *every time something feels off* ([MS Trust](https://mstrust.org.uk/news/expert/it-ms-relapse-pseudo-relapse-or-flare); [MS Society](https://www.mssociety.org.uk/about-ms/types-of-ms/relapsing-remitting-ms/managing-relapses), T2, High). **This is the strongest daily burden found.**
2. **Living with / pacing around invisible fatigue & cognitive fog** — up to 90% affected; shapes every day's decisions about energy budgeting, work and social life ([PMC10672347](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10672347/); [Front Neurol 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2025.1664734/full), T1, High).
3. **Symptom & well-being tracking** — recommended daily, but tools are fragmented, low-adherence and disconnected from the care team ([MS-UK diary](https://ms-uk.org/ms-symptoms-diary/); [MSAA](https://mymsaa.org/news/ms-manager-app-features/), T2, High).
4. **DMT adherence + side-effect/monitoring vigilance** — injections/orals/infusions on schedule, injection-site rotation, watching for drug-specific red flags, keeping up with blood/MRI monitoring ([PMC9691958](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9691958/), T1, High).
5. **The disclosure/explanation tax** — repeatedly deciding whether and how to explain an invisible illness to employers, colleagues, friends and family, spending scarce energy on it ([PMC10925750](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10925750/), T1, High).
6. **Mood self-monitoring** — given ~50% lifetime depression and doubled suicide risk, daily mental state is a clinically meaningful, under-tracked signal ([MSARD 2024](https://www.msard-journal.com/article/S2211-0348(24)00225-6/fulltext), T1, High).
7. **Appointment prep & overview maintenance** — assembling "what changed since last time" for infrequent, time-pressured neurology visits, with no running record ([structural; see Stage 6], T2, High).

## Most overlooked problems (ranked)

1. **The "is this a flare, and should I call my team today?" decision is fully offloaded to the patient with no tool.** Highest-frequency, highest-anxiety, clinically consequential, and unsupported by anything that knows the patient's own baseline. *(Confidence: High)*
2. **Invisible symptoms (fatigue, cog-fog) are simultaneously the most disabling and the least believed** — by employers, family, and even clinicians ("you look fine"), and don't show up on tests ([Front Neurol 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2025.1664734/full), T1, High). *(Confidence: High)*
3. **Diagnostic gaslighting of young women.** Months-to-years of dismissal/psychologising before diagnosis (MS ~18mo; lupus median 47mo, ~50% misdiagnosed) — and the patient's own symptom log, the richest signal, is discounted ([PMC10580682](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10580682/); [PMC11668484](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668484/), T1, High). *(Confidence: High)*
4. **Under-detected mental-health comorbidity.** ~50% lifetime depression, ~28% suicidal thoughts since diagnosis, ~2× suicide risk — low mood normalised as "understandable" and not systematically screened ([PMC6713681](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6713681/); [PubMed 29898075](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29898075/), T1, High). *(Confidence: High)*
5. **No durable, patient-owned, shareable record across a decades-long, multi-specialty journey.** 65% not even on a registry; the patient is the only continuous historian of relapses, DMT switches, MRIs and EDSS over 30+ years ([EMSP](https://emsp.org/projects/ms-barometer-2025-2027/), T2, High). *(Confidence: High)*
6. **The young loved-one is an invisible, unsupported care-partner** — early-career, often parenting small children, "suffering in silence," ~68% perceived burden, with no role-specific support ([PMC10964650](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10964650/), T1, High). *(Confidence: High)*
7. **DMT decisions recur and collide with family planning** — not a one-off choice but a thread, entangled with whether/when to have children for a 30-year-old woman ([PMC9691958](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9691958/); [PMC9682175](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9682175/), T1, High). *(Confidence: Medium-High)*

## Low-confidence / US-derived / caveated claims

- **DMT switch-reason percentages** (~26% side effects, ~19% efficacy, etc.) come via a patient-community summary of registry data (T3) — directionally reliable, exact figures EU-uncertain.
- **Shared-decision-making preference splits** (~48%/43%) are from the **US NARCOMS** registry — preference *pattern* likely transfers; exact numbers EU-uncertain *(US data — EU applicability uncertain)*.
- **Lupus "8 years" / racial-disparity figures** and the "lupus limbo" qualitative texture are largely **US** sources *(US data — EU applicability uncertain)*; the German **LuLa cohort** corroborates that delay worsens outcomes but full text was not retrievable (abstract-level, Medium).
- **MS prodrome psychiatric OR ~1.4** reached via a secondary news summary (T3) of primary research — Medium confidence pending primary-source confirmation.
- **"~68% perceived caregiver burden"** and **"~25% develop a second autoimmune disease"** are single-study / conference-summary figures — directionally solid, treat as Medium.
- Several primary PMC articles (PMC10580682, PMC7933718, PMC11668484, PMC9691958, etc.) returned **HTTP 403 to automated fetch**; figures were captured via search-engine extraction of those same sources and cross-checked across multiple results. URLs are provided for human verification.
