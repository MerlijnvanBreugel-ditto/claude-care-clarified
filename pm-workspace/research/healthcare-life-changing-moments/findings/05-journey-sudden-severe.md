# 05 — The Sudden Severe Diagnosis Journey

**Journey:** Acute, life-changing events that arrive overnight — stroke, major cardiac events (heart
attack, heart failure, sudden cardiac arrest), new-onset type-1 diabetes, and acute severe diagnoses
generally (sudden organ failure, sepsis/ICU survivorship).
**Owner:** Patient-experience research, ditto.care
**Date / access date for all URLs:** 2026-05-31
**Geography:** Pan-EU framing, anchored on Netherlands + DACH; US-derived stats flagged inline.

> **The defining feature of this journey.** Unlike cancer or neurodegenerative disease, there is no
> "diagnosis runway." The patient is well one hour and, the next, is a person with a lifelong
> condition, a daily medication regime, and a rehab calendar — typically while still in shock and
> often with no memory of the event itself. The signature trauma is the **discharge cliff**: a fast,
> chaotic hospital stay ends with an information dump, and patient + a thrown-in caregiver are sent
> home to run a self-management regime nobody taught them, with no overview of what just happened.

---

## Key takeaways

1. **Comprehension collapses at exactly the moment stakes peak.** ~40% of discharged patients cannot
   recall their diagnosis or care plan, up to 62% of older patients cannot name their new medications,
   and >50% cannot recall their planned follow-up — yet ~90% *believe* they understood. Cardiac
   diagnosis is an independent predictor of *poorer* understanding. (High)
2. **The "discharge cliff" is a named, lived phenomenon**, not a metaphor. Stroke survivors describe
   discharge as "falling off a cliff"; community rehab support frequently collapses at ~6 weeks; large
   shares of survivors get no rehab assessment at all. (High for UK; Medium EU-wide)
3. **The caregiver is conscripted, not prepared.** Only ~40% of stroke caregivers feel confident to
   care at discharge; 12/14 spousal dyads in one study called the transition "very difficult, sometimes
   traumatic." Caregiver anxiety/depression runs 20–50%. (High)
4. **Medication self-management fails fast and silently.** Up to ~50% of post-MI patients discontinue
   statins within a year; ~12.5% of discharged patients suffer an adverse drug event within 30 days,
   ~60% preventable. The hand-off itself is broken: a discharge summary is available at the first GP
   visit only ~12–34% of the time. (High)
5. **Psychological aftermath is large, under-recognized, and shared by loved ones.** Post-stroke
   depression ~30% (cumulative up to ~52% at 5y); cardiac-arrest survivors 15–50% PTSD/anxiety/cognitive
   sequelae with ~half having memory loss at 1y; ~50% of sepsis survivors get post-sepsis syndrome;
   ICU family PTSD up to ~49%. "What just happened to me?" is a literal, unmet need. (High)
6. **There is no overview after the chaos.** The patient leaves with a folder of leaflets, a med list,
   and several appointment slips across hospital/GP/rehab/specialists — and no single, comprehensible
   picture of their event, their plan, or who is responsible for what. (Medium; strong qualitative + structural support)

## Scale of the journey (EU / NL / DACH)

- **Stroke, Netherlands:** ~41,200 people present to GPs with a stroke per year; ~543,200 people are
  known to GPs as having had a stroke (2023). The population living with stroke consequences is
  projected to rise ~54% to ~672,600 by 2040 — and the Hersenstichting stresses the most disabling
  effects are **invisible** (neurofatigue, memory, planning, overstimulation), so loved ones and
  systems under-recognize them. [Hersenstichting, 2023/2024](https://www.hersenstichting.nl/hersenaandoeningen/beroerte/);
  [Kennisnetwerk CVA NL](https://kennisnetwerkcva.nl/news_cats/tot-2040-explosieve-stijging-aantal-nederlanders-met-dementie-parkinson-en-beroerte/) — Tier 2. (High)
- **Stroke, Germany:** ~270,000 strokes/year (~200,000 first-ever, ~70,000 recurrent); ~84% ischemic.
  Stroke is the 3rd most common cause of death. [Schlaganfallbegleitung / Schlaganfall-Hilfe factsheets](https://schlaganfallbegleitung.de/wissen/schlaganfall-fakten);
  [Stiftung Deutsche Schlaganfall-Hilfe factsheets](https://www.schlaganfall-hilfe.de/de/presse/factsheets-fuer-journalisten) — Tier 2. (High)
- **Heart failure, Europe:** >1 million HF hospitalizations; 20–25% readmitted within 30 days, and
  >50% readmitted within 6–12 months of first diagnosis. [Wideqvist et al., ESC Heart Failure, 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ehf2.13221) — Tier 1. (High)
- **Type-1 diabetes, Netherlands:** ~108,100 people with T1D known to GPs (2022); ~2,700 new
  diagnoses/year, projected ~3,600–3,800/year to 2040; **mean age at T1D diagnosis ~32** — i.e. a large
  share are *adults* whose established lives (work, relationships) are upended overnight.
  [Nivel / Diabetesfonds, 2022](https://www.diabetesfonds.nl/uploads/2025-02/prevalentie-en-incidentie-diabetes-2022.pdf) — Tier 1/2. (High)

## Stage 1 — Acute event & hospitalization: shock, zero preparation, lost memory

The acute phase is defined by the patient's *absence from their own story*. Cardiac-arrest survivors
"typically have difficulty remembering these events"; **about half have memory loss one year later**,
and 40% of long-term (~21-month) survivors report moderate-to-severe memory/concentration change.
[Sudden Cardiac Arrest UK — after-effects](https://suddencardiacarrestuk.org/information/sequelae/);
[SCA Foundation — memory loss](https://www.sca-aware.org/blog/scafoundation/memory-loss-among-sca-survivors-you-are-not-alone) — Tier 2/3. (High)

For ICU/sepsis events, sedation, delirium and invasive treatment produce **fragmented or absent
illness narratives**; the literature explicitly frames the unmet need as "filling memory gaps and
completing the fragmented illness narrative," which is the rationale for ICU diaries (below).
[F1000Research ICU-diary protocol, 2022](https://f1000research.com/articles/11-707) — Tier 1. (High)

**Overlooked pain point:** the patient and family are making (or being asked to consent to)
life-altering decisions during peak adrenaline and (for the patient) often no encoding of events at
all. The family becomes the *de facto* memory and decision-maker with no briefing.

## Stage 2 — The discharge cliff (hospital-to-home transition)

This is the journey's defining failure and the single highest-leverage zone.

**Comprehension collapses.** In an emergency/observation discharge study, **~90% said they understood
the information, yet ~40% could not recall their diagnosis or the care plan** afterward. Across
discharge literature, **up to 62% of older patients could not name their new medications** and **>50%
could not recall planned follow-up appointments**. Independent predictors of poor understanding include
older age, poorer cognition — and **cardiac diagnosis specifically**.
[Recall & Understanding of Discharge Information, Front. Commun., 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2021.736095/full);
[Hospital Discharge Instructions: Comprehension and Compliance Among Older Adults, 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4238191/) — Tier 1. (High)

**The patient feels overwhelmed and unsatisfied.** Patients report frustration at the *volume* of
information at discharge, "little time to clarify," and reduced satisfaction; in stroke specifically,
**own-medication information is the area people are least satisfied with**.
[PLOS One — hospital discharge processes, 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0308042);
[Teach-Back at discharge for stroke secondary-prevention meds, 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9914903/) — Tier 1. (High)

**"Falling off a cliff."** UK stroke survivors literally describe discharge that way; community rehab
support **frequently collapses ~6 weeks post-discharge**, **68% of stroke survivors do not get the
rehab assessment that should follow discharge**, and survivors/caregivers report feeling **abandoned**
by services and frustrated by the lack of proactive follow-up. Support provision varies wildly by
region (e.g. only 41% in NI vs 60% in East Midlands feeling adequately supported).
[Stroke Association — 10-Year-Plan response, 2025](https://www.stroke.org.uk/10-year-plan-response);
[University of Cambridge — survivors/caregivers feel abandoned](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/stroke-survivors-and-caregivers-feel-abandoned-by-health-services-study-finds) — Tier 2. (High for UK; **EU-wide applicability Medium** — system-dependent, but corroborated by German findings below.)

**The hand-off itself is broken.** A discharge summary is available at the first post-discharge primary-care
visit only **12–34% of the time**, and even at 4 weeks only **51–77%**, affecting care quality in ~25%
of follow-up visits. So the GP — meant to catch the falling patient — often has no information when the
patient arrives. [Discharge summary availability/timeliness QI, 2014/2018 (UK + Sweden)](https://bjgp.org/content/68/673/e576) — Tier 1. (High; EU-relevant, NL/DE systems differ in EHR maturity.)

> NL anchor: Dutch cardiac discharge guidance (Hartstichting, Thuisarts, hospital folders) is good
> *content* — but it is delivered as a stack of leaflets/PDFs ("leefregels", driving/flying rules,
> walk-twice-a-day advice) with no personalization or single overview, illustrating the gap between
> *information existing* and *information landing*. [Hartstichting — hartrevalidatie](https://www.hartstichting.nl/hart-en-vaatziekten/behandelingen/hartrevalidatie);
> [Thuisarts — hart-revalidatie](https://www.thuisarts.nl/hart-revalidatie/ik-krijg-hart-revalidatie) — Tier 2. (High)

## Stage 3 — Sudden self-management ramp (meds, monitoring, rehab)

Overnight, the patient must run a regime they have never run before.

**Medication adherence fails fast and silently.** **Up to ~50% of post-MI patients discontinue statins
within 1 year**; sufficient adherence (≥80%) is seen in only ~41% over follow-up; low first-year statin
adherence is associated with **higher 2nd-year mortality** in a 54,872-patient study.
[Low statin adherence & mortality, Eur Heart J CV Pharmacotherapy, 2021](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcvp/article/7/2/141/5736133);
[Medication adherence after MI & determinants, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7374107/) — Tier 1. (High)

**The new regime is itself dangerous in transition.** ~**12.5% of patients suffer an adverse drug event
within 30 days of discharge, ~60% preventable/ameliorable**; medication-list discrepancies between what
the patient reports and the record are found in the *vast majority* of reconciliations.
[Medication reconciliation accuracy & ADEs after discharge, 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3475816/);
[Medication errors/harm after discharge — systematic review, Drug Safety, 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40264-020-00918-3) — Tier 1. (High)

**New-onset type-1 diabetes = a self-management cliff with no taper.** Adult-onset T1D "can be very
disruptive, demanding the acquisition of challenging self-management skills" (carb counting, dosing,
hypo fear, device learning) at a life stage with established commitments. **20–30% experience elevated
diabetes-related distress**, a barrier to the very self-management they must master immediately.
[Prevalence/predictors of diabetes distress in T1D, 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9492762/);
[Psychosocial support for adult-onset T1D — cross-national feasibility, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/clinical-diabetes-and-healthcare/articles/10.3389/fcdhc.2026.1749766/full) — Tier 1. (High)

**Rehab is real but hard to navigate.** Dutch hartrevalidatie is a 3–6 month program, insurance-covered
*but referral-gated*, with home/tele options — i.e. value exists but the patient must understand
eligibility, get a referral, and self-organize attendance while exhausted and low.
[Hartstichting — hartrevalidatie](https://www.hartstichting.nl/hart-en-vaatziekten/behandelingen/hartrevalidatie) — Tier 2. (High)

## Stage 4 — Coordination across hospital / GP / rehab / specialists

The post-event patient is instantly a multi-provider patient: hospital cardiology/neurology, GP, rehab
team, physiotherapy, possibly endocrinology, pharmacy, and (for ICU/sepsis) follow-up clinics.

- Fragmented care = "services delivered across multiple providers and settings **without shared
  information, clear accountability, or consistent follow-up**." Booking across specialties is a
  "cumbersome, manual process involving phone calls, faxes, and long waits"; patients receive
  **conflicting advice** and struggle to coordinate appointments, lowering adherence and outcomes.
  [Care coordination challenges — multimorbidity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11011960/) — Tier 1;
  [Solace — managing multiple specialists](https://www.solace.health/articles/managing-multiple-specialists-for-chronic-illness-a-comprehensive-guide) — Tier 3 (texture). (High for the structural problem; Medium that it's distinctively acute for this cohort.)
- German stroke orgs name the coordination load directly: supporting a stroke survivor "**can take a lot
  of time just through appointment-organizing and filing applications (Anträge)**," and families say
  they **lack information about the disease and onward care**, driving overload (*Überforderung*).
  [Stiftung Deutsche Schlaganfall-Hilfe — Nachsorge / Schlaganfall-Helfer](https://www.schlaganfall-hilfe.de/de/aktuelles/2025/in-der-sprechzeit-herausforderung-schlaganfall-nachsorge) — Tier 2. (High)

**Overlooked pain point — no overview owner.** No single artifact or actor holds "what happened, what's
the plan, what meds, which appointments, who's responsible." The hospital owns the stay; the GP owns
nothing until the summary arrives (often it hasn't); the patient owns a folder they can't parse. This
is the structural root of most downstream failures.

## Stage 5 — Secondary prevention & long-term adjustment

The condition is lifelong, so the regime never ends — but engagement does.

- Secondary prevention depends on sustained meds + lifestyle change, yet adherence decays within the
  first year (Stage 3). Stroke "Life After Stroke" support that would reduce recurrence is "**too often
  not commissioned as part of the pathway**." [Stroke Association, 2025](https://www.stroke.org.uk/10-year-plan-response) — Tier 2. (High for UK)
- **Invisible disability** drives a chronic mismatch with loved ones' and employers' expectations:
  fatigue, planning difficulty, overstimulation and memory problems "are invisible to others but can
  have significant impact on daily life." [Hersenstichting — beroerte](https://www.hersenstichting.nl/hersenaandoeningen/beroerte/) — Tier 2. (High)
- T1D long-term load: complications within ~10 years are common; ~13-year lower life expectancy at age
  45 — a permanent vigilance burden layered on the initial shock. [Diabetesfonds/Nivel, 2022](https://www.diabetesfonds.nl/uploads/2025-02/prevalentie-en-incidentie-diabetes-2022.pdf) — Tier 1/2. (High)

## Stage 6 — Psychological aftermath (PTSD, anxiety, "what just happened to me")

This is the most *normalized-but-high-impact* zone — routinely under-screened and rarely owned by any
service, and it hits the loved ones too.

- **Stroke:** post-stroke depression ~30% in first 24 months (cumulative up to ~52% at 5y); anxiety
  20–25% of survivors. [Post-stroke depression review, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-025-07580-x) — Tier 1. (High)
- **Cardiac arrest:** 15–50% of survivors report PTSD, cognitive difficulty, anxiety or depression; fear
  of recurrence ("my body betrayed me without warning"), hypervigilance, and **ICD-shock anxiety** are
  common. The psychological after-effects "can be just as debilitating as the physical ones, yet are far
  less likely to be acknowledged or treated." [Psychological dimensions in cardiac-arrest survivors & families — state-of-the-art review, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11840352/);
  [Sudden Cardiac Arrest UK — anxiety](https://suddencardiacarrestuk.org/information/sequelae/anxiety/) — Tier 1/2. (High)
- **Heart attack / heart failure (NL):** "about one-third of people experience severe depression or
  anxiety" after a cardiac event; fatigue, apathy and uncertainty are framed as normal recovery — useful,
  but it also normalizes distress that may need active support. [Hartstichting/hospital aftercare guidance](https://www.hartstichting.nl/hart-en-vaatziekten/behandelingen/hartrevalidatie) — Tier 2. (Medium-High)
- **Sepsis / ICU survivorship:** post-sepsis syndrome affects **~50%** of survivors (fatigue,
  dementia-like cognitive decline, PTSD, anxiety); **>1 in 5 critical-illness survivors** show clinical
  depression and/or PTSD. [Post-sepsis syndrome — review, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6938630/);
  [PTSD trajectories in sepsis survivors 2y post-ICU, Critical Care, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13054-024-04815-4) — Tier 1. (High)
- **Loved ones (PICS-Family):** depressive symptoms 6–48.5%, anxiety 15–49.3%, **PTSD 33–49%**,
  complicated grief 5–46% among ICU families. ICU diaries cut family PTSD scores ~26%. The "what just
  happened" memory-repair need is real and intervenable. [PICS-F narrative review, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11478118/);
  [F1000Research ICU-diary protocol, 2022](https://f1000research.com/articles/11-707) — Tier 1. (High)
- **Caregiver mental health generally:** stroke-caregiver anxiety ~21–45% and depression ~20–50%; caregiver
  burden estimated 25–54%. [Relationship between caregiver anxiety/depression & burden, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6200450/) — Tier 1. (High)

## Involving loved ones & preparing for appointments (cross-cutting)

- **The caregiver is conscripted with no training.** Only ~**40% of stroke caregivers feel confident**
  to care at discharge; **12 of 14 spousal dyads** described the home transition as "very difficult,
  sometimes traumatic," felt "pressured to take patients home despite feeling unprepared," and found
  rehab-taught skills "did not transfer well to the home setting." A 32-study meta-synthesis confirms
  training need, uncertainty, and poor individual discharge planning as core themes.
  [Improving stroke caregiver readiness for transition, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5881730/);
  [Training/support needs in hospital-to-home triad — meta-synthesis, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39668451/) — Tier 1. (High)
- **Preparing for the follow-up flood is its own unsupported task.** Patients leave with multiple
  appointments they can't recall (>50% can't recall follow-up — Stage 2), and German orgs flag appointment-
  organizing/Anträge as a major time sink for families. There is no tool that turns the chaotic stay into
  a prepared, prioritized question + appointment plan. (Medium; inferred from cited recall + coordination data.)

## Most overlooked problems (ranked)

1. **The discharge-comprehension illusion.** ~90% *think* they understood; ~40% can't recall the
   diagnosis/plan, up to 62% can't name new meds, >50% can't recall follow-up — and cardiac diagnosis
   *worsens* understanding. Everyone signs off as "patient educated," so the failure is invisible and
   unowned. *Highest reach, highest severity, fully normalized.*
   [Front. Commun., 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2021.736095/full);
   [Older-adult discharge comprehension, 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4238191/). (High)
2. **No single overview after the chaos.** Hospital, GP, rehab and specialists each hold a fragment;
   the summary often hasn't even reached the GP (available at first visit only 12–34% of the time). The
   patient holds an unparseable folder. Nobody owns "the whole picture."
   [BJGP discharge-summary processing, 2018](https://bjgp.org/content/68/673/e576);
   [Schlaganfall-Hilfe — Nachsorge](https://www.schlaganfall-hilfe.de/de/aktuelles/2025/in-der-sprechzeit-herausforderung-schlaganfall-nachsorge). (High / Medium)
3. **The conscripted, untrained caregiver.** ~60% don't feel ready at discharge; the transition is
   "traumatic"; their own PTSD/anxiety/depression runs 20–50%. They are the de facto care manager and
   memory-keeper, yet are not assessed, briefed, or supported.
   [Caregiver readiness, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5881730/);
   [PICS-F review, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11478118/). (High)
4. **Silent, fast medication failure.** Up to 50% statin discontinuation within a year (with mortality
   signal), 12.5% ADEs within 30 days (~60% preventable), reconciliation discrepancies in the majority —
   all while the patient believes they're following the plan.
   [Statin adherence & mortality, 2021](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcvp/article/7/2/141/5736133);
   [Med reconciliation/ADEs, 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3475816/). (High)
5. **The unnarrated event + unscreened psychological aftermath.** ~Half of cardiac-arrest survivors have
   memory loss at 1y; ~50% of sepsis survivors get post-sepsis syndrome; post-stroke depression ~30%;
   one-third of cardiac patients have severe anxiety/depression — and loved ones carry PTSD up to ~49%.
   "What just happened to me?" is literal, common, intervenable (ICU diaries cut family PTSD ~26%), and
   almost never systematically addressed.
   [Cardiac-arrest psych review, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11840352/);
   [Post-sepsis syndrome, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6938630/);
   [ICU diaries, 2022](https://f1000research.com/articles/11-707). (High)

## Confidence & caveats

- **US-derived / non-EU stats (EU applicability uncertain, flagged):** the 12.5%-ADE-within-30-days and
  medication-reconciliation-discrepancy figures derive largely from US cohorts
  [PMC3475816](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3475816/); statin-adherence figures span US,
  Swedish and Australian cohorts (the 54,872-patient mortality study is Swedish — strongly EU-relevant)
  [EHJ-CVP 2021](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcvp/article/7/2/141/5736133); cardiac-arrest psychological
  prevalence draws on mixed UK/US sources. Direction is robust; exact magnitudes may shift in NL/DACH.
- **"Discharge cliff" / abandonment evidence is strongest for the UK** (Stroke Association, Cambridge);
  treated as **Medium** for direct EU-wide magnitude but **corroborated** by German Schlaganfall-Hilfe
  *Überforderung* findings and Dutch leaflet-overload structure. (Confidence: Medium-High that the
  phenomenon generalizes; Low that UK percentages transfer numerically.)
- **WebFetch limitation:** several PMC/Frontiers PDFs returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool; figures above
  are taken from the search engine's extracted text of those same peer-reviewed sources. Treated as
  Tier 1 but flagged for re-verification against full text in the verification phase (Phase 1.5).
