# 11 — The Contrarian Founder Lens

**Project:** Life-Changing Healthcare Moments — ditto.care
**Date:** 2026-05-31
**Lens:** Daniel Ek (unbundle the broken thing, rebundle it as always-on access; win the relationship, not the transaction) × Brian Chesky (design trust into a category that "shouldn't" work; do things that don't scale; build the 11-star experience) × category design (name the new game so you're the only one playing it).
**Inputs:** synthesis/09–10 + all findings.

> This file is deliberately opinionated. It takes a stance on the single biggest opportunity, where to start, and what must be true — rather than hedging across options. The hedges live in file 12 (red-team).

---

## 1. The contrarian insight

**Consensus view:** healthcare software's job is to capture the clinical encounter accurately — better notes, better records, better portals. The whole industry (EHRs, AI scribes, even EHDS) is optimizing the **15 minutes the patient is in the room** and the data exhaust that comes from it. The patient is treated as a *data source* and an *episodic visitor*.

**The contrarian truth:** serious illness is not a sequence of appointments. It is a **life, lived every day, by a frightened person and a small circle of people who love them** — and 99.9% of it happens *outside* the room, where there is no product. The most valuable software in healthcare won't be the one that documents the visit best. It will be the one that **shows up every day for the person living the illness and the people carrying it with them** — and treats the clinical system as just one input into that daily life, not the center of gravity.

Said plainly: **everyone is building for the doctor's 15 minutes; nobody is building for the patient's 15 hours.**

This is the same shape as the insights that made Spotify and Airbnb. The incumbents were optimizing the *transaction* (selling an album; booking a hotel). The winners reframed the unit around the *person's ongoing life* (all music, always, for one fee; belonging anywhere). ditto's reframe: not "a record of your care" but "a companion for your life with illness."

---

## 2. Why now (the unlock that wasn't there 3 years ago)

A contrarian bet still needs a tailwind. Three converged in 2025–26:
1. **LLMs make plain-language comprehension and grounded Q&A cheap and good** — the thing health literacy (47.6% limited, verified) and Dr. Google always needed.
2. **EHDS (in force 26 Mar 2025; patient summaries EU-wide by 2029)** legally forces the patient's data *to* the patient — but explicitly **does not explain or organize it**. The pipes are being laid; nobody owns the tap.
3. **Caregiving is at a breaking point** (~80% of EU long-term care informal, burden +50% by 2050) and the "circle" already lives on WhatsApp/CaringBridge — the behavior exists, ungoverned, waiting to be productized.

---

## 3. The category ditto can name and own

Don't compete in "patient portal" or "symptom tracker" or "AI scribe." Name a new game:

> ### The Living Health Companion — *care OS for patients and the people who love them.*

**Before (the status quo):** healthcare is something that *happens to you* in episodes you don't understand, can't remember, can't oversee, and carry alone — stitched together across WhatsApp, a Notes-app symptom list, a pill alarm, Dr. Google at 2am, a binder of letters, and a portal you can't read.

**After (the category ditto defines):** you and your circle have **one calm daily companion** that knows your situation, helps you understand and manage it day to day, lets the people who love you actually help, and turns each clinical encounter into something you walk in prepared for and walk out understanding.

The appointment summary — ditto's current product — is not the category. It is the **proof-of-trust artifact** and the **periodic crystallization** of the daily relationship. It's the Spotify "Wrapped," not the Spotify.

---

## 4. The unbundle → rebundle (the Ek move)

**Unbundle** what serious illness is hacked together from today, and **rebundle** it as one always-on companion:

| Today's fragmented hack | Rebundled into the companion |
|---|---|
| WhatsApp family broadcast | **The circle** — permissioned updates + "here's how to help" |
| Notes app / notebook symptom list | **Daily check-in** — symptom/mood/med log that compounds |
| Pill alarms | Meds, in context, with side-effect guidance |
| Dr. Google at 2am | **Grounded Q&A** — answers tied to *your* situation, not generic |
| Binder of letters / unreadable portal | **Your living overview** — understandable, longitudinal, EHDS-fed |
| The appointment summary (ditto today) | The periodic crystallization, shareable to the circle in one tap |

The rebundle is the moat: each piece alone is a commodity utility; together, governed by one daily relationship and one accumulating record, they become something no single-point competitor can copy.

## 5. Where to start — the beachhead

A daily, compounding, multi-player product needs a beachhead with: **(a) high daily frequency, (b) an active, engaged circle, (c) word-of-mouth density, (d) long duration (LTV), (e) digital-native users.** Two beachheads dominate; pick one to *start*, not to limit.

**Recommended primary beachhead: the newly-diagnosed cancer patient in active treatment + their primary caregiver.**
- **Why:** the largest, highest-intent inflow (~2.8M new EU diagnoses/yr); the daily symptom job is real and even carries a *survival* signal during chemo (Basch); the "keep everyone updated" behavior is already most developed here (CaringBridge is cancer-heavy); the caregiver is highly engaged and present; word-of-mouth density is high (oncology centres, patient orgs, tight diagnosis cohorts). It exercises **all three loops at once** — daily self-management, the circle, and the comprehension/summary crystallization.
- **The risk it carries:** it's the *one* journey with EU competition (Outcomes4Me + Germany's Mika), and it is emotionally heavy. You must win on the daily+circle experience, not on "another cancer app."

**Strongest alternative: MS / young-onset autoimmune (incl. IBD, T1D).**
- **Why it could be better:** younger, more digital, lifelong (highest LTV), the sharpest daily wedge in all of healthcare ("is this a flare — should I call?"), tight online communities, near-zero direct competition, and "explain my invisible illness to the people around me" is a screaming unmet need. Smaller inflow, but denser engagement and a cleaner runway.
- **When to pick this instead:** if you weight *daily frequency + data moat + low competition* over *raw reach* — i.e. if you believe the data-moat loop compounds faster than the network loop.

**This beachhead choice is the #1 load-bearing decision (see §8). It should be settled with evidence, not taste.**

## 6. The 11-star experience (what "magical" looks like)

*The day after a cancer diagnosis.* You open ditto. It already has your diagnosis and the summary of yesterday's shattering appointment, rewritten so you actually understand it, with the three questions you forgot to ask queued for next time. It asks one gentle question: *"How are you feeling today?"* You tap. It quietly notices your fatigue is building across the week and tells you, in plain language, what your own oncology team said to watch for — and that this isn't yet the thing to call about. With one tap, your sister, your partner and your two closest friends get a calm update — *not* the gory details, just "tired today, treatment #2 Thursday, could use a lift" — and within an hour your sister has claimed the lift and a friend has dropped off dinner, without you making a single phone call or retelling the story once. Before Thursday's appointment, ditto hands you a one-page brief — your week of symptoms, your questions, your meds — that your oncologist actually thanks you for. You walk out understanding what happened, and it's already shared with your circle.

You never "used an app." A companion showed up, every day, for you and the people carrying this with you. *That* is the category.

## 7. The growth physics (why this compounds when the summary alone didn't)

- **Daily trigger** (symptoms/mood/meds fire internally, every day) replaces the rare external trigger (the appointment). → habit.
- **Personal data moat** (each daily entry makes tomorrow's answer better; the longitudinal record is switching cost). → retention.
- **Network effect** (each patient brings 5–20 loved ones who *experience the value* and become future seeds; social rewards — replies, offers of help — are the most habit-forming class). → CAC collapses past critical mass, like a marketplace.
- **The summary as investment artifact** ties it together and gives ditto's existing strength a daily reason to exist.

Uber/Airbnb compound at low *individual* frequency because they're **multi-player marketplaces with a data moat**; ditto's "circle" is the same structure at human scale. That is the answer to the founder's frequency problem.

## 8. The first load-bearing questions (what would have to be true) — answer these before scaling

Ordered by how much they'd change the plan. Each with a cheap test.

1. **Beachhead: cancer vs MS/autoimmune?** *What's true if right:* one journey shows daily retention + organic circle growth in weeks. *Cheap test:* run two 30-person concierge cohorts (one each), measure D7/D30 daily-check-in retention and average circle size seeded per patient. Pick the loop that compounds.
2. **Will patients actually do a daily check-in?** *Test:* 4-week wizard-of-oz with manual nudges; target ≥40% of cohort checking in ≥4 days/week by week 4.
3. **Will the circle activate — and do loved ones convert into users?** *Test:* measure invites sent, % of invited loved ones who open/engage, and whether any invited caregiver later starts their *own* patient/circle (the network seed). This is the single most important growth signal.
4. **Can we deliver the "should I call?" job WITHOUT becoming an MDR medical device?** *Test:* design the non-device version (pattern-surfacing + "what your team said to watch for" + question-prompting, no triage verdict) and get a regulatory read; confirm patients still find it valuable without a clinical verdict.
5. **Who pays, and is it a painkiller they'll pay for?** *Test:* before building, price-probe three models — patient/family subscription, the loved-one as payer (the daughter pays for the parent), and B2B2C via insurer/provider/patient-org (incl. German DiGA if a CE-marked module is on the roadmap). Measure willingness-to-pay by *whom*.
6. **Is daily symptom data clinically/again-and-again valuable enough to retain — and would a care team ever consume it?** *Test:* show 10 clinicians a week of patient check-in data; would they want it before a visit? (informs the eventual provider/payer wedge.)
7. **Does the EHDS data actually flow to us in NL/DACH on a timeline we can build on?** *Test:* technical spike on MedMij/PGO (NL) and ePA (DE) access today, vs how much value we can deliver from patient-entered + summary data *without* waiting for 2029.

**The meta-question (the one to hold above all):** *Are we building a daily companion that happens to make summaries — or a summary tool that's pretending to be daily?* Every roadmap decision should be tested against whether it increases **daily, multi-player** engagement, not just feature surface.

---

## 9. The opinionated bottom line

Stop describing ditto as "AI summaries of appointments." That is a feature, low-frequency, and ultimately ownable by an AI scribe extending downstream. **Become the Living Health Companion: the daily operating system for patients and the people who love them**, beachhead through one serious-illness journey, win the daily check-in + the circle, and let the summary be the artifact that proves the relationship. Solve the founder's frequency problem not by adding features but by **changing the unit from the appointment to the day, and from the patient to the patient-and-circle.** That is the category-defining flip.
