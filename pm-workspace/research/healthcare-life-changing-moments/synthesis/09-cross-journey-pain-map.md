# 09 — Cross-Journey Pain Map

**Project:** Life-Changing Healthcare Moments — ditto.care
**Date:** 2026-05-31
**Inputs:** findings/01–08b (epidemiology, four journeys, EU policy, competitive, overlooked, daily-life)
**Purpose:** Collapse ~50 journey-specific pain points into the *universal* unmet-need clusters that recur across cancer, neurodegenerative, MS/autoimmune and sudden-severe journeys — then rank them by reach × severity × how-overlooked, with frequency called out separately (per the founder's habit/growth lens).

---

## Method

Each journey file surfaced 5–8 ranked pain points. I tagged every pain point against (a) which journeys it appears in, (b) which of the founder's five problem areas it serves (understand care / involve loved ones / manage health / prepare for appointments / keep an overview), (c) whether it is **episodic** (appointment- or event-bound) or **continuous** (daily/weekly), and (d) confidence. Pains that recur across ≥3 journeys are "universal." The whole point: universal + continuous + overlooked = the category-defining zone.

## The five universal unmet-need clusters

### Cluster A — Comprehension & memory ("I don't understand / can't remember what's happening to me")
The headwater problem. Patients forget **40–80% of medical information immediately**, and ~half of what's retained is wrong ([Kessels 2003](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/014107680309600504) — *verified*). It compounds across the journey: 69%/81% of metastatic cancer patients didn't grasp chemo wasn't curative ([Weeks 2012](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1204410), US); ~40% of discharged patients can't recall their diagnosis/plan ([discharge comprehension](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4238191/)); **~48% of EU adults have limited health literacy** ([HLS19](https://m-pohl.net/HLS19) — *verified, 47.6%*) which multiplies all of it. **Serves:** understand care, prepare for appointments. **ditto already attacks this** with the appointment summary — but only episodically.

### Cluster B — Continuity & overview ("nobody, including me, can see the whole picture")
Care is disease-siloed; **~50M Europeans live with multimorbidity** ([WHO/Euro Observatory](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK464548/)) and see many specialists who don't share a plan. The discharge summary reaches the GP in time only **12–34%** of the time ([BJGP](https://bjgp.org/content/68/673/e576)). In dementia the only complete record lives in the caregiver's head for 8–12 years; **65% of EU MS patients aren't even on a registry** ([EMSP](https://emsp.org/projects/ms-barometer-2025-2027/)). The patient/family is silently conscripted as the integration layer. **Serves:** keep an overview, manage health. **Continuous.**

### Cluster C — The loved-one layer ("the people who care for me can't actually help")
**~80% of EU long-term care is delivered by ~100M informal carers** ([Eurocarers](https://eurocarers.org/about-carers/)), yet they are legally/practically locked out of records and portals, untrained, and unsupported — the "hidden patient" (dementia caregiver burden ~49%; ICU-family PTSD up to ~49%). Two sub-jobs: (1) **access & act** on the patient's behalf (proxy), (2) **stay informed & coordinate** without the patient retelling the story 20 times (the update burden — ~73% of CaringBridge sites are caregiver broadcasts). **Serves:** involve loved ones. **Continuous — and the only inherently multi-player cluster (network-effect seed).**

### Cluster D — Daily self-management & interpretation ("is this normal, and what do I do today?")
The highest-frequency cluster and the one ditto does *not* yet touch. The dominant daily question is **"is this a normal symptom/flare or should I call someone?"** — clinicians miss between-visit symptoms up to half the time ([Basch 2017](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2630810), US); MS fatigue >80%; chemo patients ~25 distinct symptoms; ~50% medication non-adherence (~200k deaths, €125bn/yr EU [OECD/WHO](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08870446.2022.2144923)). Today's tools: notebooks, Notes app, pill alarms, Dr. Google, or nothing. **Serves:** manage health. **Daily.** Symptom self-monitoring even carries a **clinical/survival signal**, not just convenience.

### Cluster E — Emotional & existential load ("I am scared, exhausted, and alone all day")
A continuous background state, under-screened across every journey: ~1 in 4 cancer patients clinically depressed; ~50% lifetime depression and ~2× suicide risk in MS; post-stroke depression ~30%; anticipatory grief and caregiver depression 28–94%. The "limbo" phases (waiting for diagnosis/results) and the "discharge cliff" are ownerless, high-anxiety windows where the system goes silent. **Serves:** all five, emotionally. **Daily.**

## The synthesis insight (where this is heading)

The five clusters are not five products. They are **one continuous loop**:

> Every day you manage symptoms, meds and mood (**D, E**); you try to understand what's happening (**A**) and keep an overview (**B**); you lean on and update the people around you (**C**); and periodically all of it crystallizes into an appointment, where comprehension fails and the cycle resets.

ditto today owns the *thinnest, most episodic slice* (the appointment summary, Cluster A at the moment of the visit). The unowned, high-frequency, high-reach territory is **D + C + E running every day**, with **A + B** as the connective tissue — and the appointment summary repositioned as the periodic crystallization of a daily habit, not the product itself.
