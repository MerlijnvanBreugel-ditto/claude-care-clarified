# Cancer Patient Needs & Solution Landscape — Master Catalogue

*A single map of every need, problem, and challenge people with cancer face — before, during, and after treatment — across medical, social, emotional, practical, and functional life, spanning both their healthcare interactions and the long stretches between appointments. For each need-domain: what is still **unmet** vs. what already has a **solution**, how successful each solution is, and an **EU / US / Asia** read.*

**Prepared for:** Ditto Care (Rotterdam) — oncology beachhead. **Compiled:** 2026-06-16.
**Geography:** EU-first (anchored NL / DACH), with US and Asia as inspiration.
**Method:** synthesis of Ditto's audited patient-experience + market research corpus (`healthcare-life-changing-moments/`, `market-research/`, `kanker-nl-competitive-analysis/`, `core-action-evidence-sweep/`), plus a targeted web sweep for Asia exemplars and per-need solution traction.
**Sourcing:** Tier 1 (T1) = peer-reviewed / EU & national bodies / company-financial · Tier 2 (T2) = reputable press / named patient orgs · Tier 3 (T3) = vendor / app-store / forum (texture, flagged). **US-derived statistics flagged.** Confidence noted where it matters. Full source list at the end.

---

## How to read this

The catalogue is organised into **14 need-domain categories** (the top level). Each need-domain cuts across the journey, so every need carries two tags: a **dimension** (medical / social / emotional / practical / functional) and a **phase** (before / during / after treatment). Inside each category, needs are split into:

- **Unmet needs** — real, evidenced problems with no adequate product home.
- **Needs with a solution** — where a product exists, named, with its **full traction line** (users · revenue · funding · retention · geography) and a one-line judgment of *how well it actually solves the need in this sphere*.

A solution appearing here does **not** mean the need is closed. Many "solved" needs are only nominally served — e.g. symptom trackers exist but ~71% are abandoned inside 90 days. The **index matrix** and the **verdict** per category capture that gap between "a product exists" and "the need is met".

## The stakes (EU sizing)

- **22.3M** people in the EU-27 are alive with a cancer diagnosis (EUROCARE-6, 2020); **~2.8M** newly diagnosed each year; prevalence grew **+41%** across 2010–2020. ([corpus: `01-epidemiology-market-eu.md`])
- **14.85M** are alive >5 years post-diagnosis; **9.1M** >10 years — survivorship is now the largest, fastest-growing segment.
- **Caregiver multiplier:** ~1.4–2.0 involved loved ones per patient → a patient+caregiver pool of **~29–45M** in the EU.
- Highest-intent acquisition window: the **~2.8M/year newly diagnosed** and their circles.

Ditto's wedge sits at the intersection of 1 → 2 → 6 → 8 (understand → answer → overview → share), grounded in the patient's own record.

## Phase-coverage view — before / during / after

The top level is need-domains, but the journey runs in phases. Here is where each bites hardest.

**BEFORE treatment (pre-diagnosis, diagnostic limbo, diagnosis delivery, decision-making)**
Signature needs: the **ownerless waiting phase** (anxiety/depression peak *while waiting*, driven by uncertainty); **comprehension collapse at diagnosis** (40–80% forgotten immediately); **misunderstanding treatment intent** (most metastatic patients believe non-curative chemo is curative); **not knowing what to ask**. → Categories **1, 2, 3, 9**.

**DURING treatment (active therapy + coordination across specialists)**
Signature needs: **daily "is this normal / should I call?"** (chemo patients ~25 distinct symptoms; clinicians miss between-visit symptoms up to half the time); **medication timing & side-effect handling**; **no one holds the overview** across oncologist/GP/radiology/pharmacy; **caregiver locked out**; **the "update everyone" burden**; **logistics & transport**; **income loss**. → Categories **4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13**.

**AFTER treatment (survivorship, recurrence, palliative, end-of-life)**
Signature needs: the **"now-what" cliff** when frequent contact ends; **fear of cancer recurrence** (a top unmet need that *predicts* its own severity); **late-effect surveillance**; **return to work**; **goals-of-care alignment & advance-care planning**; **palliative care referred too late** (~75% of EU lung specialists refer only at end of options). → Categories **14, 9, 6, 13, 11**.

## Category 1 — Understanding the diagnosis & what was said
**Dimension:** medical / cognitive · **Phase:** all (peaks at diagnosis delivery) · **Frequency:** episodic at the visit, spills daily · **Verdict:** 🟡 Partially served — *Ditto's current beachhead*

**Reach & evidence.** This is the headwater problem of the whole journey. Patients forget **40–80%** of medical information immediately, and **~half of what is retained is recalled incorrectly** — and recall *worsens* as information volume and anxiety rise, exactly the conditions of a cancer consult ([Kessels 2003, T1](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/014107680309600504)). **~48% of EU adults** have limited health literacy ([HLS19, T1](https://m-pohl.net/HLS19)), multiplying the gap. Misunderstanding compounds into the most consequential errors: **69% of metastatic lung and 81% of metastatic colorectal patients did not grasp that chemo was not curative** ([Weeks, NEJM 2012, T1 — US data, EU magnitude uncertain](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1204410)).

### Unmet needs
- **Verifying that the patient (and family) actually understood** — no routine teach-back / comprehension check exists; understanding is *assumed*. The single most overlooked problem in the journey. (patient + caregiver; all phases)
- **Plain-language translation of jargon and pathology reports** *between* appointments, on demand — the consult ends, the questions start. (patient; during)
- **Comprehension as a longitudinal thread**, not a one-time artifact — understanding decays and re-breaks at every new scan, result, and specialist. (patient + caregiver; continuous)
- **Closing the prognosis/intent gap** — curative vs. palliative intent is the highest-stakes thing to get right and the most frequently misunderstood. (patient + caregiver; decision points)

### Needs with a solution
- **A plain-language summary of the appointment itself** → **Ditto Care** — 4.61★ (264 iOS ratings), 50K+ Play installs, "Getoetst door IKNL en GGD" trust badge, the most-rated app in the kanker.nl catalogue (NL) ([corpus: kanker.nl analysis]). **Kin Health** (US) — records visits, plain-English summaries + action items, care-circle roles, free; $9M seed (Maveron, May 2026); US-only, English-only, not HIPAA-certified ([corpus: M5]). *How well it's solved:* the **appointment-moment** comprehension artifact is real and loved — but it is episodic, single-player, and externally triggered; daily and longitudinal comprehension is not addressed.
- **Capturing the consult (ambient/scribe) so nothing is lost** → **Abridge** (~$5.3B val, ~$100M ARR, 150+ US systems), **Nabla** (FR/EU, $120M+ raised, 85k clinicians), **Heidi** (~$86.6M raised), **Corti** (EU/DK), **Dragon Copilot** (Microsoft) ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* these own the *clinician* note; the patient-facing summary is an afterthought — the consumer comprehension layer is unowned by the people who capture the raw material.
- **Static plain-language medical content** → **Thuisarts.nl** (NL, GP-authored, national), **Apotheek.nl** (NL), **Mayo Clinic**, **NHS.uk**, **Healthline** ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* trustworthy but generic — it explains the disease, not *your* conversation or *your* result.

### Geography read
- **EU:** Ditto (NL), Nabla/Corti (scribes), Thuisarts.nl — strong trust rails, plain-language tradition, but no one owns longitudinal patient comprehension.
- **US inspiration:** Kin Health (the closest direct analog); Abridge proving the scribe→patient-summary supply chain.
- **Asia inspiration:** **LinkDoc** (China) — structures messy oncology records into a continuous-care layer (~$259M raised; US IPO withdrawn 2022); the closest Asian analog to "understood conversation → continuous record." **Alodokter** (Indonesia; 28M+ MAU) — free, trustworthy health content as the comprehension top-of-funnel.

## Category 2 — Getting trustworthy answers ("what does this mean for me?")
**Dimension:** informational · **Phase:** all, intensifies before & during · **Frequency:** daily · **Verdict:** 🟢 Wide open (patient-grade, grounded, EU-available, personalised)

**Reach & evidence.** Information-seeking is a near-daily reflex: **55% of EU citizens (77% NL, 80% Finland; 45% DE) searched health info online** in the prior 3 months ([Eurostat tin00101, T1](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/product/view/tin00101)); after a serious diagnosis it becomes compulsive. The failure modes are (a) misinformation/anxiety — search ranks by keywords not prevalence — and (b) inability to map generic info onto *my* case ([Dr. Google study, T1/3](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2021-03-dr-google-patients-diagnosis.html)). ~1 in 4 of ChatGPT's 800M+ weekly users now asks health questions (~40M/week) ([corpus: M6/M11]).

> *"Scrolling at 2am… none of it is grounded in the patient's own record, diagnosis specifics, or medication list, so the patient becomes their own (badly-equipped) information integrator."* (corpus: 08b)

### Unmet needs
- **A cited, trustworthy answer grounded in the patient's *own* record** — no open-web engine knows your diagnosis, stage, meds, and last visit. This is the single biggest white space in the catalogue. (patient; daily)
- **Answers that refuse-when-unsure and route to care** instead of catastrophising or hallucinating. (patient; daily)
- **EU-available** consumer-grade grounded answers — the leading US engine (ChatGPT Health) is **explicitly blocked in the EU/UK/Switzerland** on GDPR special-category + MDR grounds ([corpus: M13]). (patient; daily)
- **Answers in the patient's language** at EU-necessary multilingual breadth. (patient + migrant caregivers; all)

### Needs with a solution
- **General-purpose AI health Q&A** → **ChatGPT / ChatGPT Health** (~40M/wk health users; EU-blocked for Health), **Gemini**, **Perplexity**, **Claude** ([corpus: M11/M16]). *How well it's solved:* huge demand proven, but not grounded in the user's record, not EU-cleared for the health product, variable safety.
- **Clinician-grade grounded answers** → **OpenEvidence** — >40% of US physicians, ~$12B valuation (2026), 18–20M monthly consultations, licensed NEJM/JAMA ([corpus: M6]). *How well it's solved:* the gold standard for *cited* answers — but **clinician-only**; the patient-grade equivalent does not exist. **Consensus / UpToDate** similar (clinician/research).
- **Symptom-triage answers** → **Ada Health** (DE; 13M+ users, 30M+ assessments), **Infermedica/Symptomate** (PL; 100M+ checks), **K Health** (US; AI triage into telehealth, ~$900M val) ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* answers the acute "what might be wrong" question episodically; not personalised to an existing cancer record, and triage tools churn.

### Geography read
- **EU:** Ada (DE), Infermedica (PL), Mediktor (ES) for triage; **no EU consumer grounded-answer engine grounded in the patient's record** — the wedge.
- **US inspiration:** OpenEvidence (cited-answer model to port to patient-grade); K Health (answer→care transaction).
- **Asia inspiration:** **Ubie** (Japan; 13M+ monthly users, $125M+ raised, Google-backed) — AI symptom-checker as a free disease-awareness funnel that converts to care routing; **Haodf** (China; 280k verified hospital doctors, acquired by Ant Group 2025) — verified-clinician grounding fused with AI. Signal: Asia is pairing **AI answers with verified-clinician grounding**.

## Category 3 — Preparing for appointments & making treatment decisions
**Dimension:** functional / medical · **Phase:** before & at decision points · **Frequency:** episodic · **Verdict:** 🟡 Partially served

**Reach & evidence.** Shared decision-making is patchy and prognosis is under-communicated; clinicians are often untrained in breaking bad news and eliciting values ([prognostic disclosure review, T1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9119949/)). **~1 in 5 patients regret their treatment decision**, with regret higher among the less-involved and those who get side effects ([decision-regret meta-analysis, T1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2588931123000378)). **Question prompt lists (QPLs)** given before consults measurably increase prognostic/EOL questions and satisfaction ([RCT, T1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2375236/)) — a proven, under-productised mechanic.

### Unmet needs
- **Personalised, dynamic "what to ask at this appointment"** generated from the patient's actual situation — QPLs work in trials but are static paper lists, not productised at scale. (patient; before each visit)
- **Decision support for values-based choices** (e.g. prostate, early breast: similar efficacy, different side-effect profiles) that the patient can hold onto and revisit. (patient + caregiver; decision points)
- **Affordable, accessible second opinion** outside the US employer-benefit model and the Indian expert-network model. (patient; decision points)

### Needs with a solution
- **Second opinion / expert navigation** → **2nd.MD**, **Included Health**, **Transcarent** (~$3B val, 20M+ members), **Grand Rounds** (all US, B2B2C employer-funded) ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* high-trust and effective where an employer pays; **no D2C / EU-reimbursed equivalent for cancer patients**.
- **Structured appointment prep** → mostly **static QPL leaflets** from patient orgs (Macmillan, KWF); the defunct **kanker.nl "Gesprekshulp"** (question lists + record, no AI, last updated Oct 2020) ([corpus: kanker.nl analysis]). *How well it's solved:* barely — the proven mechanic has no living product.
- **Oncology decision-info platforms** → **Outcomes4Me** (US; oncology; 280k patients, $21M round May 2025) ([corpus: M4]). *How well it's solved:* surfaces guideline-based options + trial matching; valued ("discovered a component of my pathology I was never told about"), but US-centric.

### Geography read
- **EU:** weakest layer — QPLs proven in EU trials but unproductised; second-opinion is ad-hoc/private.
- **US inspiration:** employer-funded navigation & second-opinion (2nd.MD, Included Health); Outcomes4Me for guideline-grounded options.
- **Asia inspiration:** **Navya** (India; 120k+ patients across 75 countries; virtual multidisciplinary tumour board with **Tata Memorial / National Cancer Grid**, ~24h expert consensus, free for low-income) and **Onco.com** (India; $13M raised, ~10k registrations/mo, 18+ countries) — both built cancer companies on **virtual-tumour-board-as-a-service**. The panel anchored to a trusted cancer centre is the transferable mechanic for EU second opinions.

## Category 4 — Daily symptom self-management & "should I call?"
**Dimension:** medical / emotional · **Phase:** during treatment (+ survivorship) · **Frequency:** daily, often multiple times/day · **Verdict:** 🟡 Partially served (and high churn)

**Reach & evidence.** The dominant daily question of serious illness is **"is this normal, or should I call someone?"** Clinicians **miss/underestimate between-visit symptoms up to half the time** ([Basch, JAMA 2017, T1 — US](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2630810)). Chemo patients report a **median ~25 distinct symptoms**; cancer-related fatigue affects **~60–62% during treatment** ([T1](https://www.jpsmjournal.com/article/S0885-3924%2820%2930649-7/fulltext)). Crucially, the job has a **clinical, not just convenience, reward**: electronic symptom monitoring during chemo produced **median overall survival 31.2 vs 26.0 months** vs usual care ([Basch, JAMA 2017, T1 — US](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2630810)).

> *"It is worth keeping your chemo diary up to date… for me each cycle followed the same pattern."* — patient (corpus: evidence sweep)
> *"The fatigue is indescribable… If I didn't sit, I would have fallen."* — patient

### Unmet needs
- **Interpretation, not logging** — the unmet job is a trustworthy "you're OK / call now" answer, not another tracker. An anxious layperson has no reference frame; patient and clinician interpret the same self-report differently ([Frontiers Oncol 2025, T1](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1595322/full)). (patient; daily)
- **A daily cadence that doesn't feel like work** — symptom self-tracking has **~43% dropout** and ~71% of health-trackers are abandoned <90 days ([corpus: M7]); manual logging is the #1 churn trap. (patient; daily)
- **Consumer access to clinically-validated ePRO** — the survival-signal monitoring exists mostly inside hospital/B2B systems, not in the patient's pocket between visits. (patient; during)

### Needs with a solution
- **Manual symptom/side-effect trackers** → **Bearable** (~900k users), **MyTherapy** (DE; 10M+ DLs, diary+meds), generic "My Cancer Tracker" apps ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* poorly — single-player, churny, no interpretation; flagged **Avoid(churn)** in the corpus.
- **Clinical ePRO / remote symptom monitoring (EU oncology)** → **Kaiku Health** (Finland; ~€4.4M Series A, **acquired by Elekta 2021**, CE Class I, 25+ cancer-type modules, **40+ European cancer clinics**); **Resilience** (France; **~$65M+ raised** incl. €40M Series A, **90+ centres, 10,000+ patients**, French national reimbursement, live FR/BE → DE); **Moovcare/Sivan** (France; lung-cancer telemonitoring — **landmark RCT: median OS 19 vs 12 months, HR 0.33**, the strongest survival evidence in this catalogue; French reimbursement 2019, but scale/status stale); **OncoKompas** (NL; Amsterdam UMC; RCT of 625 survivors across 14 NL hospitals); **Cankado** (DE; trial/pharma ePRO, PreCycle breast-cancer RCT); **Noona** (Varian/Siemens Healthineers; EMR-integrated, 12 languages) ([gap-fill]). *How well it's solved:* clinically validated and EU-rooted, but **B2B / clinic-gated and reimbursement-prescribed** — not a daily, patient-owned companion. Active-user counts are largely undisclosed.
- **Symptom checkers (acute triage)** → **Ada**, **K Health**, **Infermedica** ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* episodic triage, not continuous cancer-specific self-management.

### Geography read
- **EU:** the clinical ePRO platforms (Kaiku FI, Resilience FR, OncoKompas NL, Cankado) are an EU strength — but locked behind clinics. Consumer daily interpretation is open.
- **US inspiration:** Basch/PRO survival evidence; Noona (Varian) ePRO.
- **Asia inspiration:** **Welby "MyKarte ONC"** (Japan) — a neutral PHR running **pharma-funded, cancer-specific side-effect (irAE) self-monitoring modules** (with Chugai, AstraZeneca); the cleanest B2B2C path for patient-facing "should I call?" monitoring. **Kakao PASTA** (Korea) — daily-data + AI-interpretation + family-sharing loop (diabetes; the mechanic transfers).

## Category 5 — Medication management
**Dimension:** medical / functional · **Phase:** during (+ chronic supportive meds after) · **Frequency:** daily, every dose · **Verdict:** 🔵 Crowded

**Reach & evidence.** ~**50%** of medicines for long-term conditions are not taken as prescribed; the OECD attributes **~200,000 deaths and €125bn avoidable spend/year in Europe** to non-adherence ([T1](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08870446.2022.2144923)). Oncology adds oral oncolytics, supportive meds, and side-effect rescue meds — 3–10+ discrete events/day. Reminder apps show a real effect (**OR 2.34** across 29 studies) ([corpus: M17]).

### Unmet needs
- **The link to "why"** — reminders are utilitarian and easy to ignore; tying the med to *its appointment context* ("this is the anti-nausea your oncologist added on the 3rd") is unmet. (patient; daily)
- **Side-effect management at the moment it happens** ("the drug makes me nauseous — what now?") — handled by googling or waiting for the next visit. (patient + caregiver; daily)
- **Caregiver-shared medication oversight** with proper access (links to Category 7). (caregiver; daily)

### Needs with a solution
- **Reminders + adherence + caregiver loop** → **Medisafe** (~10M+ registered, 650M+ doses, "Medfriend" caregiver loop), **MyTherapy** (DE; 10M+ DLs), **Round Health**, **Hero** (hardware dispenser, $120M+ raised) ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* the reminder loop is genuinely **crowded and mature** — but low emotional reward, plateaus, and disconnected from the patient's clinical context.

### Geography read
- **EU:** MyTherapy (DE) is a strong EU-built pill+symptom combo; reminders are commoditised everywhere.
- **US inspiration:** Medisafe (caregiver "Medfriend" loop), Hero (dispenser).
- **Asia inspiration:** **JD Health / Alibaba Health** (China) — adherence loops attached to e-pharmacy fulfilment at scale (JD Health 183M+ annual active users, ~$8.1bn FY24 revenue); shows scale when medication delivery is the wedge, though hard to replicate under EU regulation. **Welby** (Japan) — pharma-funded per-drug adherence modules.

## Category 6 — The overview: care coordination & continuity across providers
**Dimension:** functional / systemic · **Phase:** all · **Frequency:** continuous · **Verdict:** 🟡 Partially served (structurally unowned)

**Reach & evidence.** Care is fragmented across oncologist, GP, radiology, and pharmacy with broken hand-offs; the **discharge summary reaches the GP in time only 12–34%** of the time ([BJGP, T1](https://bjgp.org/content/68/673/e576)). In the All.Can pan-European survey (~4,000 patients), **diagnosis was the single most-cited inefficiency (26%)** and information-sharing a top improvement area ([All.Can / ECO, T2](https://www.europeancancer.org/resources/news/news-patient-insights-on-cancer-care-opportunities-for-improving-efficiency.html)). **No one entity holds the overview, so the patient/family does** — under maximum cognitive and emotional load.

### Unmet needs
- **A single, patient-held overview across all providers and over time** — the integrating record lives in a notebook or the caregiver's head, not in a shared system. (patient + caregiver; continuous)
- **Insight on top of aggregation** — even where EU rails aggregate data (EHDS, MedMij, ePA), a record people open only when sick is **inert**; the daily-use insight layer is missing. (patient; continuous)
- **GP–oncologist continuity** that the patient can actually see and carry between settings. (patient; transitions)

### Needs with a solution
- **PHR / aggregation rails** → **MedMij PGOs** (NL: Quli, Ivido, Medicalla), **ePA "für alle"** (DE, rolling out to all insured 2025), **NHS App** (UK, ~30M+ registered), **Apple Health**, **Epic MyChart** (190M+ records), **Doctolib** (~80M+ EU patients) ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* the **rails exist** (an EU advantage) but they aggregate, they don't explain or interpret — "a record alone is inert."
- **Care-navigation overview** → **Included Health**, **Transcarent** (US, B2B2C) ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* good where employers pay; no EU consumer equivalent.

### Geography read
- **EU:** regulated exchange rails (EHDS, MedMij, ePA, Doctolib) are a structural advantage and a partner surface — "EHDS won't *explain* it" is the gap.
- **US inspiration:** MyChart ubiquity; b.well aggregation.
- **Asia inspiration:** **Ping An Health / Ali Health / JD Health** (China) — super-app PHR + pharmacy + telemedicine aggregation, monetised B2B2C via insurers/employers (Ping An ~5.8M B-end paying users). The aggregation-inside-a-trusted-channel model; the *insight* layer on top remains the gap everywhere.

## Category 7 — Caregiver information rights & proxy access
**Dimension:** social / legal · **Phase:** all · **Frequency:** continuous · **Verdict:** 🟢 Wide open

**Reach & evidence.** **~80% of EU long-term care is delivered by ~100M informal carers** ([Eurocarers, T1](https://eurocarers.org/about-carers/)), yet they are legally/practically locked out of records and portals. European cancer caregivers carry measurable clinical morbidity — higher depression (**OR≈1.46**), anxiety (**OR≈1.97**), insomnia (**OR≈1.95**), and far more lost work ([Goren 2014, T1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24496758/)) — and **information is their #1 unmet need**, with ≥33% still needing coping help **two years** post-diagnosis ([Informal Caregiving review, T1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3816360/)). The "hidden patient."

### Unmet needs
- **GDPR-grade, consented proxy access** that lets the person doing home care actually see the data they need, without cumbersome ad-hoc setup. (caregiver; continuous)
- **Information *for* the caregiver** — how to manage side effects at home (the top unmet need at ~78%), what to watch for, when to call. (caregiver; during)
- **Role-appropriate views** (full proxy vs. informed follower) under EU law. (caregiver; continuous)

### Needs with a solution
- **Consumer proxy features** → essentially none adequate; US patient-portal proxy is HIPAA-framed and ad-hoc; EU mechanisms differ under GDPR and are largely **unbuilt for consumers** ([proxy-access review, T1 — US framing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35437282/)). **Ditto Care Circle** (follow requests, approval, per-event privacy, EU residency, e2e encryption) is one of the few consumer attempts ([corpus: care-circle context]). *How well it's solved:* barely — this is one of the clearest **wide-open** zones in the catalogue.

### Geography read
- **EU:** GDPR makes this *harder to fake* and *more valuable to do right* — a defensible position; almost no one serves it well.
- **US inspiration:** caregiver-benefit platforms (ianacare) point at demand, but the access mechanics don't transfer.
- **Asia inspiration:** family / "F-end" account models in Asian super-apps (Ping An's family-finance channel; **Kakao PASTA**'s family/friends data-sharing community) show consented multi-user health accounts working at scale — the mechanic to adapt under GDPR.

## Category 8 — Keeping loved ones updated & mobilising help
**Dimension:** social / practical · **Phase:** all · **Frequency:** daily–weekly · **Verdict:** 🟡 Partially served — *the only inherently multi-player need (network-effect seed)*

**Reach & evidence.** Wanting to let people know how you are — *and* ask for concrete help — *without retelling the story 20 times* is a daily emotional+logistics load. **~73% of CaringBridge sites are caregiver-created** to broadcast updates; the WhatsApp group is the most common workaround ([T3](https://brainsforthecure.org/caringbridge-bridging-the-gap-between-patients-and-caregivers/)). Pre-Care-Circle, **30% of all Ditto summaries were already being shared with family** via WhatsApp/Signal ([corpus: care-circle context]) — demand proven by behaviour.

> *"I had so many people wanting to know how I was and I would lose track of what I had told to whom!"* — patient (corpus: evidence sweep)
> *"Group texts: great until messages get lost. Emails: nobody reads them on time. Phone calls: no time…"* — caregiver

### Unmet needs
- **A no-repeat update channel** that distinguishes "I'm OK" from "I need a meal Tuesday," captures mood over time, and doesn't force the patient to *perform reassurance*. (patient + caregiver; daily–weekly)
- **Turning diffuse goodwill into specific tasks** (meals, lifts, school runs). (caregiver; weekly)
- **Auto-generated updates from the clinical record** so the patient doesn't have to compose them. (patient; per event)

### Needs with a solution
- **Health-journey broadcast** → **CaringBridge** (millions of users, non-profit, US-centric), **Carely**, **Stamps** (NL family-update journal; 4.71★, 63 ratings, loved but manual) ([corpus: M16 + kanker.nl]). *How well it's solved:* the broadcast exists but is **manual** (the sick person must write every update) and disconnected from the clinical record.
- **Help coordination** → **Lotsa Helping Hands**, **MealTrain**, **Jointly (Carers UK)** ([corpus: M16 + gap-fill]). *How well it's solved:* practical-help calendars work but are US/UK-centric and siloed from the rest of the journey.
- **Share-with-circle from a summary** → **Ditto Care Circle**, **Kin** care roles ([corpus]). *How well it's solved:* the most promising bridge (summary → family artifact in one tap), still early.

### Geography read
- **EU:** WhatsApp/Signal is the incumbent; Stamps (NL) shows appetite; **no one owns it well in the EU** — the highest-leverage network-effect flip.
- **US inspiration:** CaringBridge (the canonical broadcast); Lotsa (help calendars).
- **Asia inspiration:** **LINE Doctor** (Japan, on 83M+ LINE users) and **Kakao PASTA**'s sharing community — health delivered inside the messenger people already live in, with built-in family groups; the "meet them where they already are" distribution play.

## Category 9 — Emotional & existential load
**Dimension:** emotional · **Phase:** all; peaks in diagnostic limbo & after treatment · **Frequency:** daily / continuous · **Verdict:** 🟡 Crowded-but-mismatched (huge general market; cancer-specific daily distress under-served)

**Reach & evidence.** ~**1 in 4** cancer patients experience clinical depression; anxiety runs ~2.7× general population ([ESMO + prevalence, T1](https://www.esmoopen.com/article/S2059-7029(23)00375-7/fulltext)). Distress **peaks during the diagnostic "limbo,"** driven by uncertainty, not severity ([HRQoL diagnostic-phase, T1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4873991/)) — and the system "books the patient and goes quiet." Caregiver depression/anxiety runs **28%–94%** ([T1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10086361/)).

### Unmet needs
- **Ownership of the "limbo" and "discharge-cliff" windows** — the highest-anxiety, lowest-support phases, with no owner of the open question. (patient + caregiver; before & after)
- **Cancer-specific, daily emotional support** layered onto symptoms and the care circle — not a generic meditation library. (patient; daily)
- **Routine distress screening that leads to actual support** — built into German certified centres but uptake lags ([DKFZ, T2](https://www.krebsinformationsdienst.de/krebs-und-psyche/psychoonkologie)). (patient; periodic)

### Needs with a solution
- **General mental-health apps** → **Calm** (~3.5M subs, declining), **Headspace**, **Wysa** (~6.5M+ users, NHS deals), **Youper**, **Clare&Me** (EU/DE voice), **Finch** (~10M MAU, 54%/37% D1/D7) ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* a **crowded** general market — but almost none are cancer-aware or tied to the patient's situation; meditation content decays.
- **Cancer-specific psycho-oncology** → **Untire** (fatigue/wellbeing, RCT evidence, 4.74★/95 ratings, but price anger and **only 31% trial retention**), **OncoKompas** (NL) ([corpus: kanker.nl + gap-fill]). *How well it's solved:* evidence-backed but niche, churny, and not a daily companion.

### Geography read
- **EU:** Untire (NL), OncoKompas (NL), Clare&Me (DE) — cancer/EU-specific but small; the daily emotional layer tied to the journey is open.
- **US inspiration:** Finch's emotional-reward loop (the mechanic, not the content).
- **Asia inspiration:** **Belong.Life** (Israel/global; cancer community → "Dave" conversational AI oncology mentor; $14M Series B) — emotional support fused with peer community and AI; **Naluri** (Malaysia; human+AI coaching incl. cancer, B2B2C to insurers, $5M Series A, peer-reviewed outcomes) — the bundled mental-health + chronic-disease coaching model.

## Category 10 — Peer connection & community ("people like me")
**Dimension:** social · **Phase:** during & after · **Frequency:** daily for actives, episodic otherwise · **Verdict:** 🟡 Partially served

**Reach & evidence.** Online health communities have grown sharply; in a Danish survey **72% looked to peers** for advice and **68% felt recognised by peers** in a way the formal system didn't provide ([T1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9594495/)). Peer communities function as "familial communities" and meaningfully improve wellbeing ([qualitative review, T1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27956659/)).

### Unmet needs
- **Diagnosis/stage-matched peers** ("people like me") connected to the patient's own data and journey, not a generic forum. (patient; during & after)
- **Misinformation-safe community** — current peer spaces are valuable but unstructured and unmoderated for clinical accuracy. (patient; ongoing)

### Needs with a solution
- **Cancer social networks / communities** → **Belong.Life** (Israel/global; hundreds of thousands of cancer patients/caregivers, $14M Series B, "Dave" conversational AI oncology mentor), **kanker.nl community** (NL; **~780k visits/month, 40k+ participants**), **Macmillan Online Community** (UK; **80k+ members, ~286k reached in 2024**), **PatientsLikeMe** (850k+ members, 2,800+ conditions, Optum-owned), **Inspire** (US; monetises by selling condition-specific audiences to pharma), **HealthUnlocked**, **Reddit/Facebook groups** ([corpus + gap-fill]). *How well it's solved:* loved and trusted but forum-era, disconnected from the patient's record, prone to misinformation, engagement concentrated in a minority — and increasingly **B2B-monetised** (Inspire) or **consolidated** (PatientsLikeMe → Optum, Wisdo → Talkspace).

### Geography read
- **EU:** kanker.nl community (NL), Macmillan (UK) — strong patient-org anchors.
- **US inspiration:** Inspire, Belong.Life scale and matching.
- **Asia inspiration:** **MiJian 觅健** (China; ~2.3M members) — a cancer-*only* community with per-cancer sub-forums (lung, breast), validating community-as-growth-engine and the narrow-path-first structure at national scale.

## Category 11 — Lifestyle adaptation ("is it safe for me?")
**Dimension:** functional / medical · **Phase:** during & after · **Frequency:** weekly–daily · **Verdict:** 🟡 Partially served

**Reach & evidence.** Survivors "have few clear answers to basic questions such as 'Should I change what I eat?' and 'Should I exercise more?'" ([ACS guideline, T1](https://acsjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3322/caac.21142)); only **~11%** of survivors follow a "healthy lifestyle" pattern ([T1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11092290/)). The job is daily ("can I have a glass of wine / go to work / walk today?") but the answer is gated behind a low-frequency appointment.

### Unmet needs
- **Personalised "is it safe *for me* today?"** — diet, exercise, work, travel, sex, sleep — grounded in the patient's diagnosis and current treatment. (patient; daily–weekly)
- **Daily support for prescribed rehab/physio homework** (post-surgery, post-stroke). (patient; daily)

### Needs with a solution
- **Generic lifestyle guidance** → patient-org leaflets (**Macmillan**, **ACS**, **KWF**) ([corpus: 08b]). *How well it's solved:* good *static* guidance exists; the **personalised, daily "is this ok for me"** answer does not.
- **Nutrition / fitness apps** → **MyFitnessPal**, **Yuka** (~70–80M users, instant scan-grade), **Noom**, **Oviva** (EU; dietitian + app, reimbursed DE/UK, 800k+ patients) ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* general-population tools; **Oviva** shows the EU-reimbursed model but isn't cancer-specific; none grade "safe for *your* cancer."

### Geography read
- **EU:** Oviva (reimbursed nutrition therapy) is the model to study; cancer-specific personalisation is open.
- **US inspiration:** Yuka's instant-verdict UX; ACS survivor guidance.
- **Asia inspiration:** **Kakao PASTA** (Korea) — AI food-recognition + real-time dietary guidance grounded in a 170k-person cohort (diabetes); the personalised "is this ok to eat?" loop is the transferable mechanic (cancer-specific safety still unmet).

## Category 12 — Practical logistics & administration
**Dimension:** practical · **Phase:** during · **Frequency:** weekly clusters, daily coordination · **Verdict:** 🟡 Partially served (fragmented)

**Reach & evidence.** Patients navigate "complex treatment pathways… coordinating and scheduling appointments at various specialty services and locations" ([PLOS One 2024, T1](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0300852)); transport is a burden for "almost every participant," and **74% of cancer caregivers** raised transportation ([T1 — US](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10501274/)). The patient/family is the de-facto project manager across 4–5 generic apps.

### Unmet needs
- **A single coordination layer** for the calendar, lifts, paperwork, and dispatching helpers — currently split across paper calendar, WhatsApp, and a binder of letters. (patient + caregiver; weekly)
- **Appointment sequencing across chemo/radiation/surgery** held in one place. (patient; during)

### Needs with a solution
- **Booking/access rails** → **Doctolib** (~80M+ EU patients), **NHS App**, **MedMij** ([corpus: M16]). *How well it's solved:* booking is solved; *coordinating the whole cluster + helpers* is not.
- **Help/logistics tools** → **Lotsa Helping Hands** (US; free care-coordination calendar), **MealTrain** (US; meal rotas), **Jointly / Carers UK** (UK; council-distributed "circle of care"), **Kaizen Health** (US; payer-reimbursed non-emergency medical transport, ~$3.8M raised) ([corpus + gap-fill]). *How well it's solved:* fragmented, mostly free/nonprofit or US payer-plumbing, disconnected from the journey; **no oncology-specific, patient-owned coordination product — and almost nothing in the EU beyond Jointly.**

### Geography read
- **EU:** Doctolib dominates booking; the coordination layer above it is open and best delivered as a feature of Category 8 (the help circle).
- **US inspiration:** Lotsa/MealTrain help-rotas.
- **Asia inspiration:** **Halodoc / Doctor Anywhere / LINE Doctor** — super-apps bundling search → book → consult → pay → delivery in one flow; logistics solved by embedding in an existing super-app rather than building a standalone tool.

## Category 13 — Financial toxicity, work & insurance
**Dimension:** practical / financial · **Phase:** during & after · **Frequency:** weekly–monthly · **Verdict:** 🟢 Wide open (EU)

**Reach & evidence.** Financial toxicity exists **even in strong-welfare EU systems and is driven by income loss, not co-pays**: in Germany **81% reported out-of-pocket costs and 37% income loss** (24% of those lost >€1,200/month) ([BMC Cancer 2020, T1](https://bmccancer.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12885-020-07028-4)); across **25 European countries**, patients reported income loss, out-of-pocket costs, and care avoidance for cost reasons ([ESMO Open 2025 / NKI, T1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12180988/)).

### Unmet needs
- **Return-to-work / re-integration support** that is digital and patient-facing (in the EU this is bureaucratic and offline). (patient; after & during)
- **Navigating sick leave, income protection, and entitlements** during treatment. (patient + caregiver; during)
- **Cost/insurance navigation** localised to EU national systems. (patient; ongoing)

### Needs with a solution
- **Work & financial navigation** → **Working With Cancer Pledge** (Publicis-led; **2,500+ companies / 35M+ employees** — but a *movement*, not a patient tool), **Cancer and Careers** (US nonprofit; RTW content + coaching), **Family Reach** (US; financial-toxicity grants across **1,000+ cancer centres**); in NL only statutory **UWV** scaffolding + kanker.nl/werk content ([gap-fill]). *How well it's solved:* thin, geographically narrow, mostly pledge / nonprofit / grant; **no consumer-grade digital navigator ties medical → work → income.** A clear EU white space, with historically low willingness-to-pay (→ payer/employer monetisation).

### Geography read
- **EU:** one of the clearest **wide-open** zones — strong-welfare systems reduce but don't eliminate the problem, and nothing digital serves it well.
- **US inspiration:** Family Reach (financial), Cancer and Careers (work).
- **Asia inspiration:** **Ping An** (China) — health woven into an insurance/finance group, where the insurer funds the health benefit; the insurance-linked funding model is instructive for EU income-protection navigation, though the integrated-insurer structure doesn't transfer cleanly.

## Category 14 — Survivorship, recurrence, palliative & end-of-life
**Dimension:** medical / emotional · **Phase:** after & advanced disease · **Frequency:** episodic → continuous · **Verdict:** 🟡 Partially served (fragmented)

**Reach & evidence.** **Fear of cancer recurrence (FCR)** is one of survivors' most common unmet needs, and unmet needs *predict* FCR severity ([FCR review, T1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23475398/)); distress persists for years. The **"now-what" cliff** removes the scaffolding of frequent contact. At the other end, **palliative care is referred too late** — ~75% of EU lung specialists refer only when options are exhausted; only ~22% earlier ([EU specialist survey, T1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4279692/)) — and **~half of advanced patients are unaware of their prognosis** ([T1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7181418/)).

### Unmet needs
- **The survivorship transition** — a structured "now what" after active treatment ends (late-effect surveillance, re-entry to the GP, follow-up scheduling). (patient; after)
- **FCR management** as an ongoing, supported job, not a leaflet. (patient; after)
- **Goals-of-care alignment & advance-care planning** that is structured, documented, and shared across the family ("the family meeting" rarely happens well). (patient + caregiver; advanced)
- **Earlier, de-stigmatised palliative integration.** (patient; advanced)

### Needs with a solution
- **Survivorship / FCR tools** → **OncoKompas** (NL; Amsterdam UMC; RCT of 625 survivors across 14 NL hospitals, reduced symptom burden — but an academic tool, commercial traction undisclosed), **Untire** (NL/intl; fatigue-specific, RCT N=799, ~30k downloads), **iConquerFear** (AU; the most evidence-anchored FCR-specific digital CBT, but research-stage), **OWise** (NL→UK/US; NHS-approved, breast/prostate), **Outcomes4Me** (survivorship features) ([gap-fill]). *How well it's solved:* fragmented, niche, low retention. **Cautionary:** **Blue Note Therapeutics "Attune"** — the best-funded cancer-distress DTx (~$31M, FDA Breakthrough) — **shut down in 2024 after its pivotal trial failed.** A scaled survivorship/late-effects product is a genuine white space, but the regulated-DTx route has burned capital.
- **Advance-care planning** → **Koda Health** (US; **700k+ patients** via Cigna/Privia/Houston Methodist, $7M Series A Oct 2025), **MyDirectives / ADVault** (US; interoperable digital advance directives, Humana-distributed, users in 40+ countries), **Cake** (US; consumer EOL, **acquired into a funeral-services group 2024** — weak standalone monetisation), **Thuisarts.nl "Verken uw wensen"** (NL; free public ACP choice-aid, ZonMw-funded) ([gap-fill]). *How well it's solved:* real US momentum (Koda), but EU/NL is a free public choice-aid + offline *wilsverklaring* — unproductised and not cancer-specific.

### Geography read
- **EU:** OncoKompas (NL) and national palliative frameworks; ACP is offline. Survivorship at 14.85M people >5 years is a huge, under-productised segment.
- **US inspiration:** Koda Health / MyDirectives (ACP), survivorship care-plan tools.
- **Asia inspiration:** limited — survivorship/ACP is thin across Asia too. **CancerAid** (Australia) is the cautionary arc: a D2C cancer app (incl. survivorship/EOL content) **delisted in 2024**, surviving only by becoming an insurer-funded "Cancer Coach" program — reinforcing that the payer channel, not D2C, sustains these tools.

# MULTI-PERSPECTIVE READ

*One sharp take per lens on the whole needs+solution map — where the biggest opportunity is, what's overhyped, and what they'd bet on. These same lenses were used as a completeness check while building the catalogue.*

**🚀 The visionary founder.** The category isn't "appointment summaries" — it's **the operating system of being seriously ill**. Today the patient is the unpaid integrator across 14 broken jobs. The winning move is to change the unit from *appointment* to *day* and from *patient* to *patient-and-circle*: a daily, grounded companion (Categories 1+2+4) that crystallises into the appointment artifact and shares into the care circle in one tap (Category 8). The wedge is comprehension (where trust is already earned); the moat is the **compounding longitudinal record** that makes every future answer better. Bet: own 1→2→6→8 grounded in the patient's own data, and let the rest become features.

**💰 The VC.** Most of this map is a graveyard of churn (symptom trackers ~71% abandoned <90 days; meditation decaying; Babylon bankrupt). The venture-scale gaps are the ones with **structural retention or network effects**: (8) the only multi-player, network-effect job — every patient brings 5–20 contacts, lowering CAC; (2) patient-grade grounded answers, an unowned white space with a data moat; (7/13) wide-open EU zones protected by GDPR and national-system complexity (a moat, not a bug). Overhyped: standalone AI therapy and another tracker. The B2B2C / EU-reimbursement path (Oviva, DiGA, Kaiku) is the credible monetisation, not US D2C ad spend.

**🩺 The healthcare leader (oncology).** The clinically load-bearing needs are (4) between-visit symptom monitoring — it has an **overall-survival signal**, not just satisfaction — and (1/3) comprehension and prognostic understanding, where the errors are most dangerous (most metastatic patients misunderstand curative intent). What I fear: an unsupervised consumer tool that gives wrong "should I call?" answers, or medical-device positioning that triggers MDR/AI-Act high-risk obligations. Bet: a human-in-the-loop, clinic-connected model (ePRO done consumer-grade) that *feeds* the MDT rather than competing with it; integrate, don't replace.

**🧑‍🦲 The cancer patient.** What actually ruins my days isn't the disease alone — it's not understanding what was said, lying awake Googling, not knowing if today's symptom means I should call, repeating bad news to 20 people, and watching my partner get locked out of my records. Give me one place that *remembers everything for me*, tells me in plain language what it means for me, tells me when to worry, and lets the people who love me help without me having to manage them. I don't want another tracker to fill in. I want the weight carried.

**🤝 The caregiver / loved one.** I'm the hidden patient — depressed, anxious, sleepless, and I can't even see the records I'm making decisions about. My #1 need is **information**: how to manage side effects at home, what to watch for, when to call. And I'm exhausted fielding everyone's questions. Build for me explicitly: consented proxy access (Category 7), caregiver-facing guidance, and a no-repeat update+help channel (Category 8). The caregiver is not a secondary user — they are the growth engine and the second customer.

**Honest opposing view.** The strongest argument *against* this whole expansion: Ditto's proven, trusted asset is the **episodic appointment summary**, and every attempt to bolt on daily engagement risks inheriting the 71% tracker churn curve without the clinical scaffolding that makes ePRO work. The disciplined counter-bet would be to stay narrow — be the best comprehension+sharing artifact in the EU, partner for everything else (scribes upstream, ePRO/clinics for monitoring, EHDS rails for the record) — rather than trying to own 14 jobs. Whether to expand or concentrate is the real strategic fork this catalogue exposes.

**Cross-geography signal.** Every *scaled* solution in this space is **B2B-monetised** — insurer, employer, pharma, or health-system. The EU oncology ePRO leaders (Resilience, Kaiku/Elekta) are clinician-channel and reimbursement-prescribed; the cleanest patient-facing model (Welby, Japan) is pharma-funded per-drug modules; the cancer-community → AI-mentor model (Belong.Life) monetises via pharma/trials. Meanwhile, **consumer-direct cancer DTx (Blue Note) and D2C cancer apps (CancerAid) have failed or been absorbed.** The defensible EU consumer wedge is **the understood conversation + the care circle, grounded in the patient's own record**, monetised *through* the rails (scribes upstream, ePRO/clinics, EHDS/PGO/ePA, insurers) — not D2C ad spend.
