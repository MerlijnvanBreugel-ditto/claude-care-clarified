## Verifications

### (a) DiGA classification logic — does it REQUIRE the app to be at least a Class I medical device (and never a non-device)?

**Verified: YES.** A DiGA must *be* a CE-marked medical device, and it must be of **risk class I or IIa**. It cannot be a non-device. The exact rule, from the official BfArM page:

> "A DiGA is a CE-marked medical device... Medical device of the risk class I or IIa (according to the Medical Device Regulation (MDR) or the transitional regulation Medical Device Directive (MDD))."
> — BfArM, *Digital Health Applications (DiGA) — Interesting facts*: https://www.bfarm.de/EN/Medical-devices/Tasks/DiGA-and-DiPA/Digital-Health-Applications/Interesting-facts/_artikel.html

The "main function based on digital technologies," "medical purpose mainly achieved by the digital function," and "supports the recognition, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of diseases / injuries / disabilities" criteria mean a **pure wellness or lifestyle app (a non-device) does not qualify** — it must clear MDR medical-device qualification first. (Confirmed on the same BfArM page and by infoteam Software AG: "Digital health applications (DiGA) are class I or IIa medical devices that are subject to approval" — https://infoteam.de/en/our-know-how/digital-health-applications-diga.)

**Answer to the explicit question — "if DiGA doesn't require Class IIa, does it always require Class I?"**
Effectively yes. The eligible set is **{Class I, Class IIa}**. DiGA does **not** require Class IIa (per the Dutch VWS Dec-2025 report, 54 of 57 German DiGA — 95% — are in fact Class I, assessed without a notified body — https://www.zorgakkoorden.nl/documenten/publicaties/2025/251210-vws-financing-and-making-available-of-digital-health-applications-in-europe.pdf). But it always requires the app to be **at least a registered Class I medical device**. There is no "non-device" route into DiGA. So the floor is Class I and the ceiling is IIa; anything below Class I (i.e. not a medical device at all) is excluded, and anything above IIa (IIb/III) is also excluded from the DiGA fast-track.

**Implication for Ditto:** the "stay light / Class I" reading in phase2-A is correct *for DiGA eligibility* — Ditto could pursue DiGA at Class I without a notified body. But "stay light" cannot mean "stay a non-device": to be reimbursable as a DiGA, Ditto must still cross the MDR medical-device line (intended purpose with a medical benefit) and register as at least Class I. A summary tool with no claimed medical purpose is a non-device and is **not** DiGA-eligible as-is. This is the real one-way door — not Class IIa, but the device-vs-non-device line.

**Assessment — is 2027-28 defensible?**
Directionally yes, but it should be presented as a **reasoned analyst estimate with a wide band, not a fact.** The logic is sound: (1) the artifact is already live in production (ZAS, Apr 2026); (2) the mechanism for generalization exists (ChipSoft building it as a platform feature in the dominant EHR); (3) the patient-direct Epic layer is 18-24 months out, not live. Stacking those, "broadly normal for hospital encounters in NL/BE within ~2 years" is a fair read.

**Caveats that argue for reframing the slide language:**
- It is a single-analyst extrapolation; no published roadmap, market forecast, or regulator commits to a date.
- "Standard-of-care" is doing a lot of work. Today's deployments are narrow (ZAS = 2 specialties; pilots only) and **hospital-gated and single-provider** — that is different from "standard-of-care across all NL/BE encounters." GP-side portals (Pharmeon/Uw Zorg Online) are not yet in the visit-summary game at all.
- Rollout pace in EU healthcare IT routinely slips (procurement cycles, EHR upgrade timelines).

**Recommended slide framing:** "On current rollout pace, in-portal patient-friendly summaries are likely to become a *normal expectation* for hospital encounters in NL/BE around **2027-2028** — an analyst estimate, not a published figure, anchored on ZAS going live (Apr 2026), ChipSoft building it as a platform-level HiX feature, and Epic's patient-direct layer being ~18-24 months out." Avoid stating "standard-of-care by 2027" as a hard fact; keep the "estimate" qualifier and the single-provider/portal-gated scope visible.
