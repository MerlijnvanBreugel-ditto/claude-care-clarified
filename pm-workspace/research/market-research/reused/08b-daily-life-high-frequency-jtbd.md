# The Daily Lived Experience of Serious Illness — High-Frequency Jobs-To-Be-Done (Patient + Loved Ones)

*Research file for ditto.care patient-experience research. Geography: pan-EU, anchored on the Netherlands + DACH. Compiled 2026-05-31.*

**Strategic frame:** ditto's current product (AI summaries of appointments) is structurally **low-frequency** — it fires ~1–2x/month, only around appointments, and depends on the patient *remembering* to use it. This file maps the **daily and weekly** jobs-to-be-done of living with a serious illness — the things patients and their loved ones do, worry about, and hack together *every day* — because daily relevance is what compounds into a habit, a data moat, and a category.

**Source tiers:** Tier 1 = peer-reviewed / meta-analyses / EU & national bodies (esp. HRQoL, self-management, symptom-burden literature) · Tier 2 = named patient orgs' "living with" guidance · Tier 3 = forums/communities/vendor (lived-experience texture, flagged — but uniquely valuable for *real daily behavior*). **US-derived statistics are flagged "(US data — EU applicability uncertain)."** Each finding carries a Confidence rating (High / Medium / Low).

---

## Key takeaways

1. **The daily question that dominates serious illness is "is this normal, or should I call someone?"** Symptoms fluctuate within a single week and severe symptoms emerge without warning, yet clinicians miss/underestimate patient symptoms **up to half the time** between visits ([Basch et al., JAMA 2017, Tier 1 — US data, EU applicability uncertain](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2630810)). The patient is left as the real-time interpreter of their own body, daily. **Confidence: High.**

2. **Symptom burden is genuinely daily and high-volume.** Over 4–6 months of chemotherapy patients report a median of ~25 distinct symptoms each ([symptom-monitoring RCT, Tier 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4290846/)). Cancer-related fatigue alone affects ~49–52% of patients overall and ~60–62% during treatment ([Ma et al. meta-analysis, JPSM 2020, Tier 1](https://www.jpsmjournal.com/article/S0885-3924%2820%2930649-7/fulltext)); MS fatigue affects **>80%** of people with MS and is the single most disabling symptom ([global MS fatigue meta-analysis, Tier 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11479926/)). **Confidence: High.**

3. **Medication is a daily failure point.** ~50% of medicines for long-term conditions are not taken as prescribed; the OECD attributes ~200,000 deaths and €125bn in avoidable spend per year in Europe to non-adherence ([medication non-adherence review, Tier 1](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08870446.2022.2144923)). For Parkinson's, *timing* errors (late/missed doses) directly trigger "off" periods within hours ([Parkinson's Foundation, Tier 2](https://www.parkinson.org/library/fact-sheets/managing-off-time)). The micro-job "did I take it?" recurs every single dose. **Confidence: High.**

4. **Information-seeking is a near-daily reflex.** **55% of EU citizens** sought health information online in the prior 3 months (2021), rising to **77% in the Netherlands** ([Eurostat, Tier 1](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/product/view/tin00101)). After a serious diagnosis this intensifies into compulsive "Dr. Google" checking — which improves diagnostic accuracy modestly but carries real misinformation/anxiety risk ([Dr. Google study, Tier 1/3](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2021-03-dr-google-patients-diagnosis.html)). **Confidence: High.**

5. **The "keep everyone updated" job is the single most inherently multi-player JTBD — and the only one with marketplace-style network effects.** Caregivers describe being "exhausted trying to respond to everyone," forgetting "who they told what" ([CaringBridge, Tier 3](https://api.caringbridge.org/resources/caregiving-burden-cancer-journey/)). ~73% of CaringBridge sites are created by a family caregiver to broadcast updates ([CaringBridge, Tier 3](https://brainsforthecure.org/caringbridge-bridging-the-gap-between-patients-and-caregivers/)). Every patient has many loved ones → each patient seeds a small network. **Confidence: High (behavior); Medium (exact magnitude).**

6. **Loved ones carry a parallel daily load that today has no product home.** Family caregiver depression/anxiety ranges from 28% to as high as 52–94% in cancer ([family-caregiver review, Tier 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10086361/)); their top unmet need is *information*, and in MND/ALS care families progress to managing feeding, communication and life-sustaining treatment at home within months ([MND caregiver studies, Tier 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9315024/)). **Confidence: High.**

7. **The strategic punchline:** the appointment-summary job is a low-frequency *single-player utility*. The daily jobs above are higher-frequency, and two of them — **"keep my loved ones updated"** (multi-player/network) and **"track my symptoms & meds"** (accumulating personal-data moat) — are the candidates that can flip a low-frequency tool into a daily, compounding, category-defining product.

## JTBD 1 — Symptom tracking & interpretation ("is this normal, or do I call?")

**Frequency:** Daily, often multiple times/day. Symptoms fluctuate over the course of a week and severe symptoms emerge without prior signs, so a *daily* cadence is needed to catch them; less-frequent check-ins miss rapidly developing problems ([remote symptom-monitoring analysis, Tier 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8897754/)). MS fatigue is measured most accurately by *ecological momentary assessment* — six self-reports per day — because it peaks in late afternoon and swings with heat/exertion ([daily-fatigue EMA study, Tier 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5613039/)). **Confidence: High.**

**Reach:** Cancer-related fatigue ~49–52% overall, ~60–62% during treatment ([JPSM meta-analysis, Tier 1](https://www.jpsmjournal.com/article/S0885-3924%2820%2930649-7/fulltext)); MS fatigue **>80%** ([Tier 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11479926/)); chemo patients ~25 distinct symptoms each over a treatment course ([Tier 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4290846/)). This is effectively every patient on an active treatment journey. **Confidence: High.**

**The core unmet job is interpretation, not logging.** Patients' own perception of symptom severity differs significantly from clinicians' interpretation of the same self-reported data ([Frontiers in Oncology 2025, Tier 1](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1595322/full)), and clinicians miss symptoms up to half the time between visits ([Basch, JAMA 2017, Tier 1 — US data](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2630810)). The daily decision "is this a normal side effect or an emergency?" is made by an anxious layperson with no reference frame. Patient-org guidance is a static list of "call your doctor if you have a fever / bruising / blood" ([oncology side-effect guidance, Tier 2](https://www.cdc.gov/cancer-survivors/patients/side-effects-of-treatment.html)) — it can't tell you whether *your* symptom today is the one that matters. **Confidence: High.**

**Current hacky solution:** Paper notebooks, the phone Notes app, ad-hoc spreadsheets, a Fitbit/Apple Watch for steps/sleep, or — most commonly — *nothing*, with the patient trying to remember the arc of symptoms to recount at the next appointment. Dedicated apps (My Cancer Tracker etc.) exist but are niche, single-player, and abandoned. **Brokenness: High.** The thing patients most need (real-time interpretation + a trustworthy "should I call?" answer) is exactly what notebooks and step-counters cannot provide.

**Why it matters strategically:** symptom self-report isn't just a vitamin. In the landmark Basch trial, electronic symptom monitoring during chemo produced a median **overall survival of 31.2 vs 26.0 months** vs usual care (~5 months / +HR benefit; the survival result was reported in [JAMA 2017, Tier 1 — US data, EU applicability uncertain](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2630810); systematic-review confirmation in lung cancer, [npj Digital Medicine 2025, Tier 1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01812-x)). A daily symptom job has a *clinical* reward, not just an emotional one. **Confidence: High (effect); Medium (EU magnitude).**

## JTBD 2 — Medication & treatment management ("did I take it?")

**Frequency:** Every dose, every day — for many serious-illness patients that is 3–10+ discrete medication events/day across primary drugs, supportive meds, and side-effect rescue meds. **Confidence: High.**

**Reach:** ~50% of medicines for chronic conditions are not taken as prescribed; non-adherence is linked to ~200,000 deaths and €125bn avoidable spend/year in Europe ([Tier 1 review citing WHO/OECD](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08870446.2022.2144923)). In older populations adherence averages **<45%** ([three-cohort European study, Tier 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6179242/)). Suboptimal adherence is "the most frequent reason" chronic diseases stay poorly controlled ([EJIM 2023, Tier 1](https://www.ejinme.com/article/S0953-6205(23)00233-9/fulltext)). **Confidence: High.**

**The timing sub-job is acute in neuro disease.** In Parkinson's, late/missed/extra doses directly cause unpredictable "off" periods and worsen quality of life for patient and caregiver; getting medication *on time, every time* is a daily, clock-driven task that caregivers often own ([Parkinson's Foundation, Tier 2](https://www.parkinson.org/library/fact-sheets/managing-off-time); [medication-timing-error study, Tier 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2957227/)). **Confidence: High.**

**Current hacky solution:** Pill organizers (dosette boxes), phone alarms, the partner who "reminds them," sticky notes, or memory. The recurring failure mode — *"did I already take the 2pm one?"* — has no reliable consumer answer; smart pill bottles exist but adoption is marginal. Side-effect management (what to do when the drug makes you nauseous) is handled by googling or waiting for the next appointment. **Brokenness: High.**

## JTBD 3 — Emotional / psychological daily load ("how am I feeling today" + the update burden)

**Frequency:** Daily and continuous — anxiety and low mood are a background state, not an event. **Confidence: High.**

**Reach:** ~1 in 4 cancer patients experience clinical depression; depressive disorders are 2–3x general-population rates; in one cross-sectional study ~25% had symptomatic anxiety and ~21% symptomatic depression ([ESMO guideline + prevalence studies, Tier 1](https://www.esmoopen.com/article/S2059-7029(23)00375-7/fulltext); [prevalence study, Tier 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4143739/)). Among family caregivers, anxiety/depression runs 28% up to 52–94% depending on setting ([caregiver review, Tier 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10086361/)). **Confidence: High.**

**The hidden daily job here is the "update everyone" burden** — wanting to let loved ones know how you are, *and* ask for help, *without retelling the story 20 times*. Caregivers describe it directly: "exhausted trying to respond to everyone," "forgetting who they told what," wanting to spend time caregiving rather than fielding "phone calls, emails, texts" ([CaringBridge, Tier 3](https://api.caringbridge.org/resources/caregiving-burden-cancer-journey/)). For the patient, repeating bad news is re-traumatizing; for both, well-wishers' incoming concern is a second job. This is simultaneously an *emotional* load and a *logistics* load — and it is inherently multi-player. **Confidence: High (behavior); Medium (magnitude).**

**Current hacky solution:** A WhatsApp broadcast/family group, a mass email, occasionally a CaringBridge/Caring Bridge-style journal (~73% of which are caregiver-created precisely to broadcast updates — [Tier 3](https://brainsforthecure.org/caringbridge-bridging-the-gap-between-patients-and-caregivers/)), private journaling, or bottling it up. WhatsApp groups don't distinguish "I'm OK" from "I need a meal Tuesday," don't capture mood over time, and force the patient to perform reassurance. **Brokenness: High** — and uniquely, this is the job whose current "solution" already drags in a *network of people*, which is the seed of network effects.

## JTBD 4 — Information seeking / researching the disease (daily Dr. Google)

**Frequency:** Daily, especially in the weeks after diagnosis and around each symptom or scan. **Confidence: High.**

**Reach:** **55% of EU citizens** searched health info online in the prior 3 months (2021), up 17 points since 2011; **NL 77%, Denmark 75%, Finland 80%** at the top; **Germany 45%** notably *lower* ([Eurostat tin00101, Tier 1](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/product/view/tin00101); [Eurostat 2022 news, Tier 1](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/-/edn-20220406-1)). Among the seriously ill the rate is effectively universal — "many people are inclined to turn to the internet for more information after hearing they have cancer" ([Tier 2/3](https://www.hopeandhealingcare.com/blog/paging-dr-google-searching-your-cancer-diagnosis-online)). **Confidence: High** (general); the DACH gap (DE lower than NL) is a real, flag-worthy regional difference.

**The job has two failure modes:** (a) misinformation/anxiety — search engines rank by keywords not clinical prevalence, so "chest pain" surfaces "tumor" regardless of the searcher's actual risk ([Tier 3](https://oncolifehospitals.com/blog/why-does-every-symptom-on-google-search-lead-to-cancer/)); and (b) inability to map generic information onto *my* case. Dr. Google modestly improves diagnostic accuracy but does not reduce anxiety ([Tier 1/3](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2021-03-dr-google-patients-diagnosis.html)). **Confidence: High.**

**Current hacky solution:** Google, YouTube, patient forums, ChatGPT, scrolling at 2am. None of it is grounded in the patient's own record, diagnosis specifics, or medication list, so the patient becomes their own (badly-equipped) information integrator. **Brokenness: High.**

## JTBD 5 — Lifestyle adaptation (what food/exercise/work/travel/sex/sleep is safe?)

**Frequency:** Weekly→daily decisions (every meal, every "should I go for a walk / go to work / have a glass of wine"). **Confidence: Medium.**

**Reach:** Universal among survivors and people in treatment, but acted on by few: only ~11% of cancer survivors fall into a "healthy lifestyle" pattern across activity/diet/sleep ([latent-class study, Tier 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11092290/)). Sleep problems are markedly more common in cancer patients/survivors ([ACS, Tier 2](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/supportive-care/nutrition-activity-with-cancer/acs-nutrition-and-physical-activity-guideline-for-survivors.html)). **Confidence: High (reach); Medium (frequency of the decision).**

**The job is "is it safe for *me*?"** Survivors "have few clear answers to basic questions such as 'Should I change what I eat?' and 'Should I exercise more?'" ([ACS guideline, Rock et al., Tier 1](https://acsjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3322/caac.21142)). Exercise is safe for most but the patient is told to "ask your team" — i.e. wait for a low-frequency appointment to answer a daily question. Rehab/physio exercises (post-stroke, post-surgery) are prescribed as daily homework with little daily support. **Confidence: High.**

**Current hacky solution:** Generic patient-org leaflets (Macmillan "healthy lifestyle after treatment," [Tier 2](https://www.macmillan.org.uk/cancer-information-and-support/after-treatment/lifestyle-and-wellbeing-after-treatment)), googling, forum anecdotes, or guessing. **Brokenness: Medium–High** (good static guidance exists; daily personalized "is this ok today" does not).

## JTBD 6 — Practical daily logistics (appointments, transport, paperwork, coordinating helpers)

**Frequency:** Weekly clusters of appointments, daily coordination of who-drives-whom and who-does-what. **Confidence: High.**

**Reach:** Patients face "complex treatment pathways… coordinating and scheduling appointments at various specialty services and locations" ([logistic-burden qualitative study, PLOS One 2024, Tier 1](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0300852)). Transport was a burden for "almost every participant" because most couldn't drive themselves at some point; **74% of cancer caregivers** raised transportation across a 6-month study ([transportation-burden study, Tier 1 — US data, EU applicability uncertain](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10501274/)). **Confidence: High (burden exists); Medium (EU magnitude).**

**The job:** holding the calendar, arranging lifts, managing insurance/sick-leave paperwork, and dispatching the people who keep asking "what can I do?" toward concrete tasks (meals, school runs, lifts). The patient/family is the de-facto project manager. **Confidence: High.**

**Current hacky solution:** Shared paper calendar, phone calendar, WhatsApp, a binder of letters, the one organized relative. Meal-train / lift-rota tools exist (US-centric) but are disconnected from the rest of the journey. **Brokenness: High** — the coordination layer is fragmented across 4–5 generic apps.

## JTBD 7 — Connection & support (peers + telling friends/family how to help)

**Frequency:** Daily for active community members; episodic for others. **Confidence: Medium.**

**Reach:** Use of online health social networks has "increased dramatically"; in one Danish peer-community survey, **72% looked to peers** for advice/experience because of discontinuity with the formal system, and **68% felt recognized by peers** in a way the health system didn't provide ([Danish online-community survey, Tier 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9594495/)). Online peer communities function as "familial communities" for people in daily pain and meaningfully improve wellbeing ([qualitative systematic review, Tier 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27956659/)). **Confidence: High.**

**The job:** finding "people like me" (same diagnosis/stage) for normalization and practical tips, *and* converting diffuse goodwill from friends/family into specific help. Note the overlap with JTBD 3 (the update burden) and JTBD 6 (coordinating helpers) — these three converge on a single multi-player surface. **Confidence: Medium.**

**Current hacky solution:** Facebook groups, Reddit, condition-specific forums, charity helplines, WhatsApp. Valuable but unstructured, prone to misinformation, and disconnected from the patient's own data. **Brokenness: Medium.**

## Frequency & habit-formation analysis

### Why the current product stalls (single-player, low-frequency utility)
The appointment summary is a **single-player utility with an external, infrequent trigger** (the appointment). A single-player tool's value to the user doesn't grow with the user base, and an infrequent trigger can't build a habit. By the Hook Model ([Eyal, summarized Tier 3](https://amplitude.com/blog/the-hook-model)), a habit needs a frequent **trigger → easy action → variable reward → investment** loop. The summary has a strong action and reward but a *rare* trigger and no accumulating investment — so it can't compound. This is the classic "vitamin utility that stalls."

### What a daily hook looks like for each JTBD

| JTBD | Internal trigger (felt need) | Action | Variable reward | Investment (compounds) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Symptom track & interpret | "Is this normal? Should I worry?" (fires daily) | 20-sec check-in | A grounded "you're OK / call now" answer (varies → dopamine + relief) | Personal symptom timeline → better future answers (data moat) |
| Meds | "Did I take it?" | Tap to confirm | Streak / reassurance | Adherence history + regimen model |
| Emotional + **update loved ones** | "I want them to know / I can't repeat this again" | One post | **Replies, hearts, offers of help arrive back** (social variable reward) | More contacts added → network grows |
| Info-seeking | "What does this mean for *me*?" | Ask | Personalized, grounded answer | Q&A history tied to record |

### The two compounding mechanics ditto can own

**1. Multi-player / network effects — the "keep my loved ones updated" job.** This is the only JTBD that is *inherently multi-sided*, like a tiny marketplace: a patient (supply of updates) and many loved ones (demand for them + supply of help). Each patient who joins brings 5–20 family/friends; those contacts experience the product's value (timely, no-repeat updates; a clear way to help) and become candidates to start their own circle when *they* face illness. The variable reward is *social* — replies, hearts, concrete offers of help arriving back — which is the strongest, most habit-forming reward class ([Hook Model, Tier 3](https://amplitude.com/blog/the-hook-model)). Network effects "compound once critical mass is attained," lowering CAC past the inflection point ([network-effects manual, Tier 3](https://www.nfx.com/post/network-effects-manual)). The existing behavior (WhatsApp broadcasts, CaringBridge journals) proves demand; nobody owns it well in the EU. **This is the highest-leverage flip.**

**2. Accumulating personal-data moat — the "track my symptoms & meds" job.** Even as a single-player tool, a daily symptom/med log builds a **data network effect with oneself**: each daily entry makes tomorrow's "is this normal for *you*?" answer better, and the accumulated longitudinal record becomes switching-cost and the substrate for the appointment summary (which is suddenly grounded in real between-visit data, not just the 15-minute consult). Data network effects "compound invisibly… each entry improving relevance and trust" ([network-effects analysis, Tier 3](https://www.nfx.com/post/network-effects-manual)). And unlike most quantified-self logging, this one has a **clinical** reward (the Basch survival signal), which is a far stronger retention argument than step-counting.

### How the appointment summary becomes the *investment artifact*, not the product
Reframed: the daily symptom/med/mood log is the **action**, the loved-one circle is the **social reward**, and the appointment summary becomes the periodic **pay-off + investment artifact** that (a) is now grounded in a month of real daily data and (b) can be shared into the loved-one circle in one tap. The low-frequency product stops being the whole thing and becomes the *crystallization moment* of a daily habit.

## Daily JTBD ranked by frequency × reach × hook-potential

| Rank | JTBD | Frequency | Reach | Hook / compounding potential | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Keep loved ones updated + coordinate help** (JTBD 3/6/7 converged) | Daily–weekly | Every patient × 5–20 loved ones | **Network effects (multi-player)** | Only inherently multi-sided job; social variable reward; existing behavior proves demand; lowers CAC. |
| **2** | **Symptom tracking & interpretation** | Daily (multi/day) | ~Universal in treatment (fatigue 49–80%) | **Personal data moat + clinical reward** | "Is this normal?" fires daily; longitudinal data compounds; Basch survival signal. |
| **3** | **Medication "did I take it?" / timing** | Every dose | ~50% non-adherent; ~universal regimen | Data moat (adherence history) | Highest raw frequency; acute in Parkinson's; but crowded by pill-reminder apps. |
| **4** | **Information seeking / "what does this mean for me?"** | Daily (post-Dx) | 55% EU / 77% NL search online | Q&A history tied to record | Universal reflex; grounding in own data is the unmet edge; misinformation risk. |
| **5** | **Emotional daily load / "how am I feeling"** | Daily | 1 in 4 clinical depression | Mood timeline; feeds update job | Strong felt trigger; best monetized as a layer on JTBD 1 & the update job. |
| **6** | **Lifestyle adaptation ("is it safe for me?")** | Weekly–daily | Universal; only ~11% act | Personalization | Real but lower-frequency *decision*; good static guidance already exists. |
| 7 | Practical logistics / transport | Weekly clusters | High | Coordination layer | Important but commoditized; best as a feature of JTBD 1 (the update/help circle). |

**Top of the funnel for a category-defining flip:** combine **#1 (network) + #2 (data moat)**. The daily symptom/mood/med check-in is the habit-forming *action* with a personal-data moat; the loved-one circle is the *social reward* with network effects; the appointment summary becomes the periodic *investment artifact* that ties it together and gives the existing product a daily reason to exist.
