The framework

Three concentric markets × four forces

The three markets

  • Healthcare system — the ocean: payers, providers, regulation (~$5.3T US spend)
  • Health tech / digital health — the fast-growing slice (~$28.8B global VC in 2025)
  • Consumer health — the inner ring closest to ditto ($6.8T wellness economy)

The four forces bending all three

  • 1. Patient power — consumerization; demand for understanding
  • 2. AI & data — LLMs as default; grounding as the new trust bar
  • 3. Regulation & data rights — EHDS, PGO/BGZ, ePA, AI Act, MDR, GDPR
  • 4. Capital & business models — money on the clinician side; D2C → B2B2C

→ then: where the puck is going (feature-direction traction) → implications for ditto.

The outer ring

The healthcare system is the ocean — and EU ≠ US

United States

$5.3T health spend (2024), 18% of GDP, ~$14.9k/capita — highest in the OECD. Fragmented multi-payer, fee-for-service. You sell to employers, payers, health systems.

EU (incl. NL + DACH)

Universal coverage (single-payer or regulated multi-payer like NL/DE), ~half the US per-capita spend. You navigate national reimbursement pathways. NL, DE, AT, CH among top EU spenders.

This "who pays" divergence dictates go-to-market. It's also why digital-health venture capital is so US-skewed — the US market is roughly double the per-head spend.

Sources: Health Affairs 2025; Peterson-KFF; Healthcare-NOW. Confidence: High. See findings/M1 §1, §5.

The structural split

The patient sits on the hard-to-fund side of the market

Clinician / payer (B2B & B2B2C)

Dominant share of VC. Clear ROI buyer (saves clinician time / chronic-care cost), reimbursable. All the mega-rounds and both 2025 IPOs.

Patient / consumer (D2C)

Minority of dollars. Diffuse willingness to pay, subscription churn, no automatic reimbursement. Consumer health-app funding fell ~40% in H1 2025.

The proven routes to revenue: (a) sell the clinician/system the value, surface the patient summary as a feature; or (b) anchor to a reimbursement path (Germany's DiGA; bundled NL care). Pure D2C is the hardest path.

Sources: Rock Health; Galen Growth; DrugPatentWatch (flagged). Confidence: High on direction, Med on precision. See findings/M1 §4, M2 §4.

Demand vs. trust

"Dr. Google" → "Dr. ChatGPT" — and the trust gap is the wedge

The demand is real

32% of US consumers used an AI chatbot for health in 2025 (up from 16% in 2024). 74% reached for general tools (ChatGPT, Gemini) — only 5% used provider bots, 4% payer bots.

The trust gap is the opening

LLM diagnostic accuracy is ~76–78% on benchmarks — strong, but hallucination and the patient-vs-clinician safety gap keep general LLMs positioned as aids, not patient-grade answers.

Implication: the volume proves demand; the accuracy/trust gap is the opening. A product grounded in the patient's own record + cited evidence is differentiated exactly where generic LLMs are weakest.

Sources: Rock Health 2025 Consumer Adoption; EurekAlert/The Conversation 2026; MIT Tech Review 2026. Confidence: High. See findings/M2 §2.3, M6 §1.

Trends 5 & 6 · outcomes & data

Outcomes are the unit of account — and data is a softening moat

Value-based care

US CMS targets ~all Medicare beneficiaries in value-based arrangements by 2030. The EU pursues outcomes via centralised registries (e.g. the NL Heart Registry). Anything that improves comprehension & adherence between visits becomes a measurable outcomes lever.

Data as moat

Longitudinal personal data creates lock-in and cross-sell — but the moat is softening: each LLM release narrows the proprietary-data edge. The durable asset is not model IP or a generic dataset; it's consented, patient-owned, longitudinal context.

Sources: Optum 2025; VBHC scoping review PMC 2024; Insignia 2025 (analyst). Confidence: Med-High. See findings/M2 §5, §6.

The data side

Access is being solved. Comprehension is not.

Regulation is making the data available to patients — but almost nobody opens it, because access was the easy half. Understanding is the unbuilt half.

This is the single most important fact on the data side, and it is admitted from inside the flagship national systems themselves (next two slides). It is the clearest external validation of ditto's reason to exist.

Confidence: High. See findings/M3, M4 §6.

Sources: EC EHDS; EUR-Lex 2025/327; HL7 Europe 2026; Noerr/Kennedys 2025–26. Confidence: High on dates. See findings/M3 §1.

NL & DE · the rails are built, the apps are empty

The Netherlands & Germany prove the gap

Netherlands — world-class plumbing, low use

BGZ (Basisgegevensset Zorg) — a ~26-zib, FHIR-native patient summary — is now exchangeable to PGOs; Wegiz is forcing BGZ exchange (phasing 2025–26). But PGO end-user adoption is low — and MedMij's 2026 roadmap commits to making PGOs "more understandable" (an admission they aren't).

Germany — universal coverage, near-zero use

ePA "für alle" auto-created records for ~70M insured (opt-out; mandatory provider use since Oct 2025). Yet only ~3.6% actively use it (early 2026); ~48% find digital health tools "too complex."

FHIR is the through-line (NL zibs/BGZ · DE MIOs · EU EEHRxF) — so build ditto's data model on FHIR/IPS once, map everywhere. Caregiver/proxy access is legally supported in NL (DigiD Machtigen), DE and EHDS.

Sources: Nictiz/MedMij; datavoorgezondheid; gematik; Joep Lange Institute 2026. Confidence: High on infra; Med on exact adoption %. See findings/M3 §2–4.

Competitive landscape · clinician side

Scribes have the richest signal — and the wrong user

AI medical scribes (Abridge $5.3B, Nabla, Corti, Tortus, Dragon, Heidi/Suki/Ambience) are the best-capitalised category by an order of magnitude. They capture the actual conversation — but sell to clinicians; the patient is the data source, and any "patient summary" is a clinic-bound by-product.

The 2026 twist

Scribe leaders are moving upmarket into coding, orders, prior-auth and revenue cycle — not downstream to patients. Corti is the clearest "EU-sovereign" counter-positioning ("others retreat from Europe").

The structural opening

No portable, cross-provider, patient-owned plain-language layer follows the patient across providers. That space stays vacant.

Sources: TechCrunch, STAT, HIT Consultant, PR Newswire 2025–26. Confidence: High. See findings/M4 §1, M8 V1.

Competitive landscape · patient & caregiver side

Companions are oncology-locked; caregiver tools have no clinical brain

Patient companions = oncology

Outcomes4Me (+Mika/Fosanis, German DiGA), Jasper, Belong, Vinehealth are real and EU-active — but condition-locked to cancer and focused on tracking/trials/coaching, not appointment comprehension. Heart-failure, neuro, rare-disease, multimorbid patients have no equivalent.

Caregiver tools = logistics

CaringBridge, Lotsa coordinate people but understand nothing medical; Birdie sells to agencies, not families. They tell the family that there was an appointment — not what it meant.

Tellingly, not one of the 2026 Big Tech assistants models the caregiver as a first-class user. The caregiver-comprehension cell is empty.

Sources: Outcomes4Me/PRNewswire; Caring.com; Sifted; M4 analysis. Confidence: High. See findings/M4 §3–5.

Direct competitor · deep dive

What it is

A patient's "personal health assistant": one-tap records any visit (in-person or telehealth) → plain-English summary + concrete next steps → invite family to a "care circle" (Manager vs Viewer roles). For patients with complex care and their overwhelmed relatives.

So what

Compete on geography & trust — not a feature race

Kin's structural weakness

US-anchored, English-only, not HIPAA-certified, monetised by referrals to specialists/labs — with documented questions about third-party processing of appointment audio. A referral model on health data faces stiff EU GDPR / anti-kickback scrutiny; it won't transplant cleanly to NL.

ditto's defensible edge

EU/NL data residency, GDPR-native consent, AI Act/MDR posture, multilingual by necessity, and a non-referral model ("we don't monetise your medical conversations"). The position: the EU-anchored, trust-first version of Kin.

Near-term gaps to close: match Kin's care-circle role tiers and ship structured action-item extraction.

Sources: NewsBytes, HIT Consultant 2026 (via search extract). Confidence: Med. See findings/M5 §4.

Where the puck is going

Five vectors of the market

  • 1. Capture → workflow → revenue. Scribes move from notes into coding, orders, prior-auth, RCM — and wrap patient summaries in.
  • 2. Single-utility → life-stage / multi-condition platform. Flo, Belong reuse one engine across cohorts.
  • 3. Data plumbing → comprehension. Aggregation commoditises; value moves up to understanding.
  • 4. D2C → B2B2C. Everyone routes through pharma / payer / provider distribution.
  • 5. Consumer-AI giants entering top-down. ChatGPT Health normalises "ask AI about my records."

The market is collapsing toward two poles — clinician-side scribe superplatforms reaching into patient summaries, and consumer-AI giants reaching into personal-health comprehension. ditto sits in the contested middle.

Confidence: High. See findings/M8 synthesis.

Feature-direction traction

The directions we're weighing — what's actually working

For each direction ditto is considering, the corpus asks: who's winning, what are the success cases, the cautionary tales, and the retention evidence? The short version:

Strong / open lane

Grounded & cited answers (patient-language); a daily-relevant compounding loop anchored to the appointment.

Crowded / contested

Direction · AI health assistants

General LLMs already own consumer health AI

The default is set

74% of AI-health users reach for general tools (ChatGPT 23%, Gemini 15%); only 5% use provider bots, 4% payer bots. Any new entrant competes against a free, ubiquitous default.

The wedge

Consumers use AI for quick answers, side-effect research, appointment prep — exactly ditto's adjacency. Differentiate on trust + grounding + a real-world hook, not "another chatbot."

EU regulatory note Under the AI Act, "AI for medical use" is high-risk; an information / appointment-prep companion that avoids diagnosis/triage claims has a far lighter path than a symptom-checker positioned as a device (compliance Aug 2026/2027).

Sources: Rock Health 2025; IntuitionLabs 2025. Confidence: High (adoption), Med (regulatory). See findings/M6 §1.

Direction · grounded & cited answers

The breakout model — with an open consumer lane

"Plausible" is no longer enough. The trust standard is grounded, cited, and refuses when unsure.

OpenEvidence proved it on the clinician side ($12B, >40% of US physicians) by licensing premium evidence and citing inline. Medical-RAG research converges on citation-traceability + principled refusal — but warns shallow grounding makes RAG "a dangerous medical communicator." No consumer-facing grounded engine has reached comparable scale — ditto can borrow the architecture for a patient-language register OpenEvidence deliberately doesn't serve.

Sources: CNBC 2026; OpenEvidence×NEJM/JAMA; arXiv 2510.14400 / 2502.14898 (2025). Confidence: High. See findings/M6 §2.

Direction · digital health coaches

Coaches: real but modest effect, chronic engagement decay

The traction

Lark (35M+ patient interactions, $162M raised), Omada (OmadaSpark AI agent), payer-channel distribution. Market sized ~$28B by 2032 (vendor estimate).

The unsolved problem

Systematic reviews flag "shallow, transactional engagement and retention" as the central weakness. Hybrid human+AI models retain best. Vendor outcome stats are self-reported (flagged).

Success factors: a clinical wedge with measurable outcomes, payer/employer reimbursement (not consumer subscription), and a human-in-the-loop to sustain engagement.

Sources: Frontiers Digital Health 2025; MobiHealthNews; JMIR 2024 (CKD). Confidence: High on the engagement caveat. See findings/M6 §3.

Cautionary tales

The graveyard: overreach + regulation kill products

Babylon Health

The highest-profile AI-assistant + telehealth combo. SPAC at $4.2B (2021) → bankruptcy (2023). Disputed AI-diagnosis claims; inverted unit economics (patients used it 6–7×/yr vs capitation for 2–3×).

Woebot

CBT chatbot used by 1.5M+ people; shut its app in 2025, blocked by the absence of an FDA pathway for LLM-based therapy.

Lesson for ditto: positioning as a medical device invites an AI Act + MDR high-risk burden that can kill the product. Stay an information / comprehension / preparation / companion — not diagnosis or therapy.

Sources: TechCrunch 2023; STAT 2025; IntuitionLabs 2025. Confidence: High. See findings/M6 §1, §5.

Consumer-health retention · the winners

Why cycle-tracking & meditation win

Cycle & pregnancy — the strongest category

Flo retains ~60% of MAU past one year (~2× the Health & Fitness norm); ~70M MAU. Clue passed 1M paid subscribers. Mostly EU-rooted (Flo, Clue, Natural Cycles) — so the mechanism transfers to NL.

Wins via a daily-relevant, anticipatory question whose answer changes ("where am I in my cycle / is this normal"), compounding data, and life-stage urgency.

Meditation — retains, but decays

Calm (~$596M revenue, ~$2B) and Headspace (DAU/MAU ~17%, past peak) win via ritual, streaks, immediate felt reward — but the reward is replaceable and the data doesn't compound, so it plateaus.

Sources: Flo/General Atlantic, Clue 2024–25; Sacra/GetLatka; Sequoia. Confidence: High directionally; exact MAU snippet-sourced. See findings/M7 §1.

Consumer-health retention · the lesson

Retention is a category property — here's the test

Losers (symptom/QS/disease logging) demand effort today for a deferred, abstract payoff, with weak external triggers and no compounding loop. Apply this 7-gate test to every "Living Health Companion" feature — fail the first three and it inherits the 90-day churn curve, not the cycle-tracker curve:

  • 1. Daily-relevant question the user already re-asks
  • 2. Changing answer day to day (static dashboards fail)
  • 3. Effort ≤ reward, felt today — passive capture, not manual logging
  • 4. Compounding data — each day improves tomorrow's answer
  • 5. Life-stage / salience hook (post-diagnosis, med change)
  • 6. Social / reassurance loop (caregiver/partner sharing)
  • 7. Identity, not illness — frame around agency

The translation: "given your last appointment and what's changed since, here's what to watch / do / ask today" — refreshed daily, smarter every visit.

Sources: JMIR meta-analyses; Hook Model (Eyal). Confidence: High. See findings/M7 §3.

Synthesis

Where the market is going — in one read

Demand for understanding is overwhelming (40M ChatGPT health questions a day), but capital, products and exits cluster on the clinician side, and general LLMs mop up consumer demand for free.

Regulation is finally making patient data available (EHDS, BGZ/PGO, ePA) — yet almost nobody opens it, because access was solved and comprehension was not. Big Tech validated the patient-comprehension category and is blocked from the EU on GDPR/MDR; clinician scribes and Epic reach into patient summaries from the visit (Dragon Copilot in NL, Emmie). The market is collapsing toward two poles — and the defensible space between them is a trust-anchored, EU-native, longitudinal, caregiver-inclusive patient-comprehension layer.

See: synthesis/S1. Confidence: High on the structural reading.
