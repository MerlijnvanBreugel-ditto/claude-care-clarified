2 · trust vs. accuracy

The trust gap: models are benchmark-brilliant yet unsafe for unsupervised patients

Capability is high

GPT-4 alone scored ~92% on diagnostic reasoning vs ~74–76% for physicians (JAMA Network Open RCT, 2024) — yet giving doctors the tool barely improved them.

…but patient-grade safety isn't

A 2026 npj Digital Medicine study found 5–13% of LLM answers to patient questions were outright "unsafe", plus high guideline-omission and adversarial-hallucination rates.

The gap that's "opening" is between what models can do in benchmarks and what's safe to hand an unsupervised patient — precisely the grounding/safety problem ditto must solve.

Sources: Goh et al., JAMA Netw Open 2024 · npj Digital Medicine 2026. See M11 §3.

Deep dive · the diagnostic-accuracy research, both sides

Read both halves before quoting "AI beats doctors"

The "LLMs are good" evidence

  • Goh/Rodman/Chen, JAMA Network Open 2024 (RCT, 50 physicians): GPT-4 alone ~92%; physicians with GPT-4 ~76%, with conventional resources ~74% — the AI alone beat humans, but adding it barely moved physicians (~2 pts, n.s.). The famous "usage gap, not capability gap" study.
  • Frontier LLMs reach correct diagnosis in top-3 for ~98.6% of structured cases (JMIR 2024).

The counter-evidence

  • npj Digital Medicine 2026: problematic responses 21.6% (Claude) to 43.2% (Llama); outright unsafe 5% (Claude) to 13% (GPT-4o/Llama), some judged capable of serious harm.
  • Clinical-guideline accuracy 46% (GPT-4.1) down to ~3% (DeepSeek-V3); LLMs "highly vulnerable to adversarial hallucination attacks" (Communications Medicine 2025).

Honest framing: on clean structured cases top models match/exceed physicians; on messy patient-phrased questions a non-trivial minority of answers are unsafe, and unsupervised consumers lack clinicians' guardrails.

Deep dive · is the data moat real, and does comprehension move outcomes?

Two honest, evidence-based answers

Comprehension → adherence

Meta-analyses: health literacy is positively but modestly associated with adherence (r≈0.14), stronger for non-medication regimens and cardiovascular samples; literacy interventions raise both literacy (r≈0.22) and adherence (r≈0.16). A 2025 study links higher literacy to better antihypertensive adherence via understanding-the-rationale + self-efficacy. So comprehension is a genuine, modifiable precursor — frame it as a contributing lever, not a silver bullet.

The data moat — real but softening

Real: Oura (~$11B, ~$1B revenue 2025, ~80% renewal) and Whoop (~$10B; published a study on 7.9M days of wearable data + 300k+ monthly surveys) show years of passive data create a baseline + switching cost. Softening: hardware is commoditizing (Apple Watch, cheap rings); aggregators (ChatGPT Health ingests any source) erode device lock-in; patent litigation signals a narrowing hardware edge. The durable moat is the interpreted, longitudinal, multi-source, EU-legal layer — ditto's natural ground.

Deep dive · what EHDS grants — and the scribe-summary threat

EHDS turns the patient summary into a commodity inside the record

From March 2029, standardized Patient Summaries become the first mandatory cross-border data category, surfaced in national patient portals. If a scribe (e.g. Nabla, which already auto-generates multilingual patient "Patient Instructions" written back to the EHR) writes the summary into the EHDS-linked record, the patient receives it natively — no separate app required.

So the durable edge for ditto is not generating the summary but owning the comprehension + longitudinal-continuity + patient-trust layer, and being an early EHDS data-consumer. Position as "we make your whole, cross-provider record understandable over time," not "we generate summaries."

Patient rights granted: access + portability (Arts. 3, 5–7), granular restriction (Art. 8), primary-use opt-out where legislated (Art. 10), digital representatives / caregiver access (Art. 4(2)), secondary-use opt-out anytime (Art. 71).

Deep dive · BGZ, PGO, Wegiz, ePA explained

Deep dive · why is ChatGPT Health blocked in the EU?

Three independent legal mechanisms — each sufficient to block it

  • 1. GDPR Art. 9 (special-category health data). Processing health data requires a narrow exception (explicit consent), DPIAs, and — for US transfers — "particularly strict" scrutiny even under the Data Privacy Framework. This forces EU data residency/localization.
  • 2. EU AI Act high-risk classification. Medical-use AI is auto-high-risk (Art. 6(1) via MDR/Annex III): transparency, explainability, reproducibility, human oversight, conformity assessment — hard for a probabilistic chatbot. Obligations phase in Aug 2026 / Aug 2027.
  • 3. MDR medical-device risk + liability. Individualized health advice likely meets the medical-device definition → CE marking, a Notified Body, clinical evaluation, and elevated liability. The expected pattern is a single CE mark with two declarations of conformity (MDR + AI Act).

OpenAI's own framing: Health is available "excluding the EEA, Switzerland and the UK," attributed to "more restrictive local health and data regulations." Durability: robust near-term — clearing one regime leaves the others. Realistic localized EU entry by US giants: 2027–2028, behind compliant-by-construction EU incumbents. A closing window.

Why generic trackers are low-value: two compounding problems. Accuracy — symptom-checker median triage accuracy ~55.8% (2020) vs 59.1% (2015), flat and worse in some emergency decisions; correct-diagnosis-first only ~34% (BMJ 2015). Retention — ~71% disengage within 90 days; ~3.3% 30-day retention for unguided mental-health apps. A generic tracker asks daily manual input, gives low-confidence output, and offers no accountability — the worst combination.

The exceptions, and the mechanism

  • Tied to a prediction — Flo. Cycle/ovulation forecast gives a scheduled reason to open; unicorn on a $200M raise.
  • Passive / wearable capture — Oura & Whoop. Data arrives without effort; Oura ~$1B revenue 2025; wearables now pivoting from tracking to predicting outcomes.
  • Prescribed & clinical — oncology ePRO (Basch). Prolonged survival, fewer hospitalizations; real-world retention 94%/88%/73%/67% at 3/6/9/12 months — and older patients retained better, because it's prescribed, EMR-integrated, and tied to a life-or-death outcome.

Lesson for ditto: avoid the "yet another symptom diary" trap. If tracking is in scope, anchor it to a prediction, a wearable feed, or a clinician-prescribed pathway with a human on the other end.

8 · the transferable lesson

The 7-gate retention test for every ditto feature

Fail the first three and a feature inherits the 90-day churn curve, not the cycle-tracker curve.

  • 1. Daily-relevant question the user already re-asks
  • 2. Changing answer day to day (static dashboards fail)
  • 3. Effort ≤ reward, felt today — passive capture, not manual logging
  • 4. Compounding data — each day improves tomorrow's answer
  • 5. Life-stage / salience hook (post-diagnosis, med change)
  • 6. Social / reassurance loop (caregiver/partner sharing)
  • 7. Identity, not illness — frame around agency

The translation: "given your last appointment and what's changed since, here's what to watch / do / ask today" — refreshed daily, smarter every visit. The cycle-tracker pattern, applied to clinical care.

9 · synthesis

Where the market is going — in one read

Demand for understanding is overwhelming (40M ChatGPT health questions a day), but capital, products and exits cluster on the clinician side, and general LLMs mop up consumer demand for free.

Regulation finally makes patient data available (EHDS, BGZ/PGO, ePA) — yet almost nobody opens it, because access was solved and comprehension was not. Big Tech validated the patient-comprehension category and is blocked from the EU; scribes and Epic reach into patient summaries from the visit (Dragon Copilot in NL, Emmie). The market is collapsing toward two poles — and the defensible space between them is a trust-anchored, EU-native, longitudinal, caregiver-inclusive patient-comprehension layer.
