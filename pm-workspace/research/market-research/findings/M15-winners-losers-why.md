# M15 — WINNERS & LOSERS (and the WHY) across three crowded directions

**Prepared for:** ditto.care (EU/Netherlands, patient-facing health-tech)
**Date / access date:** 2026-06-10
**Scope:** AI health assistants & symptom checkers · digital health coaches · symptom/wellness trackers
**Method:** WebSearch + WebFetch, prioritising 2024–2026 sources. Source tiers flagged inline: **T1** = primary/regulatory/peer-reviewed/company financials & SEC; **T2** = established trade press / analyst (Fierce, MobiHealthNews, TechCrunch, Sacra, PitchBook); **T3** = blogs/secondary/SEO aggregators — **explicitly FLAGGED**. Confidence tags: (High/Med/Low). US→EU applicability flags noted. Funding/retention numbers I could not verify are marked **[unconfirmed]**.

---

## Key takeaways

1. **The winning move in symptom checkers was abandoning the consumer funnel for B2B.** Ada Health (EU/Germany) reached profitability and grew revenue ~260% YoY into 2023 by selling clinically-validated triage to health systems, payers and pharma (Bayer, Novartis, Pfizer) — not by chasing consumer DAU. Infermedica did the same as a pure B2B API. **(High)**
2. **Babylon died from the exact opposite bet:** consumer-facing AI hype + capitated NHS unit economics that lost money on every patient, plus disputed accuracy claims it defended aggressively rather than fixing. SPAC at ~$4.2B → Chapter 7 in Aug 2023. **(High)**
3. **In coaching, the survivors monetise through employers/payers with hard clinical + claims outcomes, not consumer subscriptions.** Hinge Health IPO'd (May 2025), Omada IPO'd (June 2025), Sword hit a $4B valuation — all B2B2C, MSK/cardiometabolic, with retention and outcome data. Consumer-DTC Noom did rounds of layoffs as its original "Healthy Weight" program shrank. **(High)**
4. **Engagement decay is the universal failure mode:** ~70% of health-app users abandon within 100 days; mental-health-app retention can be ~4%. The apps that beat it bind engagement to (a) a reimbursed clinical outcome, (b) human-in-the-loop, or (c) a medication/condition the user is already committed to (GLP-1). **(High)**
5. **Generic symptom trackers are low-value because accuracy hasn't improved** — symptom-checker triage accuracy ~55–59% across a 5-year window — and retention is poor. **(High)**
6. **The tracker exceptions all share one trait: passive capture or a valued prediction.** Flo (cycle prediction → unicorn), Oura ($1B revenue 2025, ~80% hardware) and Whoop (subscription-first) win because data arrives passively or feeds a forecast users return for. **(High)**
7. **Clinical, B2B ePRO trackers are the "boring" success:** oncology ePRO monitoring (Basch) showed survival benefit, fewer hospitalisations, and 67–94% retention over 12 months — because it's prescribed, integrated into care, and tied to outcomes. **(High)**

## 1) AI health assistants / symptom checkers — named winners & losers + WHY

### Summary table

| **Ada Health** | 🇪🇺 Germany (EU) | **Winner / survivor** | Pivoted to B2B (pharma/payer/health-system); clinically validated; "most accurate" care-navigation positioning | Profitable end-2023, +260% YoY revenue; Series B $90M led by Leaps by Bayer; 40M+ assessments; partners incl. Bayer, Novartis, Pfizer, Jefferson Health [T2/T1] |
| **K Health** | 🇺🇸 US | **Winner** | AI intake → clinician; deep health-system & payer partnerships (Cedars-Sinai, Mayo know-how) | $50M round July 2024 at ~$900M valuation, ~$380M total raised [T2] |
| **Infermedica / Symptomate** | 🇪🇺 Poland (EU) | **Winner** | Pure B2B triage API (insurers, telemedicine, systems); Symptomate is the free consumer funnel feeding clinical credibility | Clinical engine: 800+ diseases, 1,500 symptoms; B2B-only distribution [T1/T2] |
| **Healthily (Your.MD)** | 🇬🇧 UK | **Mixed / struggling** | Pivoted UK→US, Walmart retail partnership; more lifestyle/self-care content than clinical decision support | $20–30M from existing investors; app reliability complaints reported [T2/T3] |

### Ada Health — the EU flagship winner (and most relevant comp for ditto)

Ada (Berlin) is the clearest case that **B2B distribution + clinical validation beats the consumer funnel.** Ending 2023 it reported profitability and a **~260% YoY revenue increase**, driven by partnerships with health systems, life-sciences companies, payers and governments — Jefferson Health, Novartis, Bayer, Pfizer, Orion Health ([Ada press, 2024](https://about.ada.com/press/240606-ada-health-expands-leadership-team-for-strategic-growth/); [PRNewswire, 2024](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ada-health-the-most-accurate-ai-for-care-navigation-expands-its-leadership-team-for-strategic-growth-with-product-and-business-development-hires-302166540.html)). Bayer's venture arm Leaps by Bayer led its **$90M Series B** ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/emea/ai-based-assessment-platform-ada-health-joins-forces-pharma-giant-bayer); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Health)). It markets **40M+ completed assessments** and a "most accurate AI for care navigation" claim, and in Oct 2025 partnered with Fullspan Health to cut time-to-treatment in the US ([Ada press, 2025](https://about.ada.com/press/ada-health-and-fullspan-health-join-forces-to-reduce-time-to-treatment-for-millions-of-americans/)). **EU flag: Ada is European — directly relevant to ditto's GDPR/MDR regulatory environment and EU go-to-market.** **(High)**

### K Health — AI intake feeding clinicians, via systems & payers

K Health won by **not pretending to replace the doctor**: AI does the intake/medical-chat, then routes to a clinician. The Cedars-Sinai Connect app reportedly **increased the system's primary-care capacity by ~15%**, and K Health signed a know-how agreement with Mayo Clinic on preventive cardiac care. It raised **$50M (July 2024) at ~$900M valuation, ~$380M total** ([MedCity News, 2024](https://medcitynews.com/2024/07/primary-care-funding-round-ai/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/digital-primary-care-startup-k-health-raises-50m-equity-funding-round); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240701313131/en/K-Health-The-Leading-AI-Primary-Care-Platform-Raises-%2450-Million-Equity-Funding-Round)). **US-centric** — distribution depends on US payers/systems, not directly transferable to NL. **(High)**

### Infermedica / Symptomate — the pure-B2B API play

Infermedica (Poland, EU) is the archetype of "**win by being infrastructure.**" It is "primarily a B2B solution… not directly accessible to individual clinicians" — its clinical engine (800+ diseases, 1,500 symptoms, 200 risk factors) is licensed to insurers, telemedicine providers and health systems. **Symptomate** is the free consumer-facing checker that doubles as a credibility/demo funnel ([Infermedica](https://infermedica.com/); [developer docs](https://developer.infermedica.com/)). A 2024 study explored virtual-triage AI for early detection of life-threatening conditions ([PMC, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11131945/)). **EU flag: Infermedica is EU-built — another strong regulatory/go-to-market comp.** **(High)**

### Babylon Health — the canonical loser, and WHY

Babylon is the cautionary tale on every axis ditto cares about:

- **Broken unit economics.** Under NHS **GP at Hand**, the NHS pays a **flat ~£160/patient/year** regardless of usage; Babylon's (younger, higher-utilisation) patients demanded far more appointments, so it **lost money on every UK patient in 2022** — the chatbot never "automated away" enough consultations to fix it ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/the-rise-and-fall-of-babylon); [The Week](https://theweek.com/health/babylon-health-the-failed-ai-wonder-app-that-dazzled-politicians)).
- **Overhyped, disputed accuracy.** Babylon's 2018 claim its AI could "diagnose as well as a human doctor" was rejected by *The Lancet*, which said the study offered "no convincing evidence." Oncologist David Watkins (@DrMurphy11) flagged triage errors; the UK MHRA later said his concerns were "valid" and "ones that we share" ([TechCrunch, 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/02/25/first-do-no-harm/); [TechCrunch, 2021 MHRA](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/05/uks-mhra-says-it-has-concerns-about-babylon-health-and-flags-legal-gap-around-triage-chatbots/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon_Health)).
- **Adversarial response to safety critique** — Babylon publicly attacked Watkins rather than fixing the issues, eroding clinical trust ([artificialintelligence-news](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/babylon-health-doctor-ai-chatbot-safety-concerns/)).
- **Financial collapse.** SPAC IPO 2021 at ~$4.2B; net loss **$221M on ~$1B revenue (2022)**; shares fell 99% within 18 months; **Chapter 7 in Aug 2023**, UK business sold to eMed via the bankruptcy process ([TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/31/the-fall-of-babylon-failed-tele-health-startup-once-valued-at-nearly-2b-goes-bankrupt-and-sold-for-parts/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/babylon-closes-us-business-lays-employees-after-mindmaze-take-private-deal-collapses)). **(High)**

### The pattern

- **Won on B2B/enterprise distribution:** Ada, Infermedica, K Health.
- **Won on clinical validation/regulatory lens:** Ada (validated, "most accurate"), Infermedica (clinical engine), K Health (clinician-in-the-loop).
- **Won on lightweight/free as a funnel (not a business):** Symptomate, Healthily, Docus — free checker drives credibility/leads, monetisation sits elsewhere (API, second opinions, partnerships).
- **Died from overreach + regulation + unit economics:** Babylon — consumer hype, capitated losses, disputed accuracy, regulator scrutiny.

**Implication for ditto:** the EU winners (Ada, Infermedica) monetised B2B with clinical validation, *not* consumer ad/subscription DAU. A patient-facing app should treat the consumer surface as a trust/funnel layer and anchor revenue + defensibility in clinical validation and institutional distribution.

### Why the winners scale

The IPO/scale winners share three things: **(1) B2B2C distribution paid by employers/payers** (not consumer subscriptions), **(2) hard outcomes** — clinical + medical-claims-cost — and **(3) human-in-the-loop or a committed clinical anchor.** Hinge Health: FY2025 revenue ~$574–588M, profitable, **98% client retention, 117% net-dollar retention, ~81% gross margin**, serving ~49% of the Fortune 100 ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/hinge-health/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/hinge-health-shares-jump-17-stock-market-setting-stage-digital-health-ipo-revival)). Omada: 2024 revenue **$169.8M (+38%)**, **90% customer retention**, members in cardiometabolic programs engaged **>30×/month** in year one, and members on Wegovy/Saxenda with meaningful Omada engagement lost **1.7× the weight** of low-engagement peers ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/09/virtual-chronic-care-company-omada-health-files-for-ipo-.html); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/omada-health-aims-11b-valuation-ipo); [Omada](https://www.omadahealth.com/why-omada)). Sword reached a **$4B valuation (Jun 2025)** on ~$240M run-rate and bought Kaia ($285M) to lock down Europe ([TechCrunch, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/17/sword-health-nabs-40m-at-4b-valuation-pushes-ipo-plans-to-at-least-2028/); [Galen Growth](https://www.galengrowth.com/in-the-battle-for-digital-health-supremacy-sword-healths-european-roots-fuel-a-global-power-play/)). Hello Heart's independent study found **~$1,434 PMPY** medical-cost reduction ([Hello Heart press](https://www.helloheart.com/press/new-independent-study-shows-significant-medical-claims-cost-reductions-from-hello-hearts-digital-cardiovascular-health-program)). **(High)**

### Why the strugglers struggle

**Noom** is the DTC cautionary tale: dependent on consumer subscriptions, it did **multiple rounds of layoffs in 2024** (mostly coaches/customer-service) as its original "Healthy Weight" program shrank and it leaned into GLP-1 (GLP-1 Rx hit a ~$100M run-rate in four months, but it ended up "with more coaches than it needed"). Peak valuation ~$3.7B is widely described as having declined ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/digital-weight-loss-company-noom-confirms-another-round-layoffs); [Endpoints](https://endpoints.news/noom-cuts-coaches-staff-as-glp-1-strategy-falls-short/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/noom/)). **Dario** launched a **strategic review in Sep 2025** (potential sale/merger) after sub-scale revenue ($27M, 2024) and cash-runway pressure ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/il/news-releases/dariohealth-announces-strategic-review-following-multiple-unsolicited-inbound-expressions-of-interest-302566366.html); [BHB](https://bhbusiness.com/2025/01/21/dariohealth-raises-25-6m-to-accelerate-path-to-profitability/)). **Lark** bet on fully-automated AI coaching (no humans) — maximally scalable but exposed to the engagement-decay problem; it relies on CDC DPP / pending MDPP reimbursement to anchor stickiness ([Lark MDPP](https://www.lark.com/enterprise/programs/mdpp-medicare)). **(Med-High)**

### WHY engagement decays — and what sustains it

The base rate is brutal: a 2024 *JMIR* scoping review found a **median ~70% of users abandon health apps within 100 days**, with abandonment driven by technical/UX issues, privacy concerns, weak content, cost, and "evolving user needs/goals." Mental-health-app retention can be **~4%** ([JMIR, 2024](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11694054/)).

Design/distribution choices that **defeat decay** (per the winners' data):
- **Tie engagement to a reimbursed clinical outcome** (Omada, Hinge, Virta) — the payer relationship + outcome guarantee creates institutional stickiness beyond the individual's motivation curve.
- **Human-in-the-loop** (Hinge PTs, Sword's PTs + Phoenix AI, Omada coaches) — accountability sustains behaviour better than a pure-AI nudge engine.
- **Ride a committed medication/condition** — GLP-1 users who engage most stay on therapy ~2.2× longer (Noom data) and lose 1.7× the weight (Omada); the medication is the retention scaffold ([Noom engagement report, Feb 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/02/04/3231984/0/en/The-Noom-Engagement-Report-New-Data-Shows-that-the-GLP-1-Patients-Who-Use-Noom-the-Most-Also-Lose-the-Most-Weight-and-Stay-On-Medication-Program-Longest.html)).
- **Passive capture** (Hello Heart BP cuff) lowers the daily-effort burden that drives abandonment.

**Implication for ditto:** consumer motivation decays predictably; durable engagement comes from binding the app to a clinical outcome, a clinician/human, a medication a patient is already committed to, or passive data — ideally with a payer footing the bill.

## 3) Symptom & wellness trackers — why low value, and the exceptions

### Summary table

| Player / type | Win/Lose | WHY |
|---|---|---|
| **Generic symptom checkers/trackers** | **Low value** | Triage accuracy stuck at ~55.8% (2020) vs 59.1% (2015) — no improvement in 5 yrs; ~70% abandon in 100 days [T1] |
| **Generic mental-health/wellness trackers** | **Low retention** | ~4% retention; abandonment from UX, privacy, "evolving goals" [T1] |
| **Flo Health** (cycle) | **Surprising winner** | Tied to a *valued prediction* (period/ovulation); unicorn on $200M raise; Oura integration [T2] |
| **Oura** (smart ring) | **Winner** | Passive/wearable capture; $1B revenue 2025 (~+100% YoY), ~80% hardware/20% subscription; ~74% smart-ring share H1-2025 [T2 — Sacra/Omdia] |
| **Whoop** (band) | **Winner** | Subscription-first, passive recovery analytics for committed athletes [T2/T3] |
| **Oncology ePRO** (e.g. Basch trials) | **Clinical winner** | Prescribed + EMR-integrated + tied to survival; 67–94% retention over 12 mo [T1] |

### Why generic trackers are low-value

Two compounding problems. **Accuracy:** a 5-year follow-up evaluation found symptom-checker median triage accuracy **~55.8% (2020) vs 59.1% (2015)** — flat, and *worse* in some emergency-care decisions ([PMC, symptom-checker accuracy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9131144/)). **Retention:** the ~70%/100-day abandonment and ~4% mental-health retention numbers above ([JMIR, 2024](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897)). A generic tracker asks for daily manual input, gives low-confidence output, and offers no external accountability — the worst possible combination for retention.

### The exceptions — and exactly why they work

1. **Tied to a prediction users return for — Flo Health.** Cycle/ovulation prediction gives a concrete reason to open the app on a schedule; Flo hit **unicorn status on a ~$200M raise** and integrated Oura biometrics ([Medical Device Network](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/news/femtech-app-flo-health-hits-unicorn-status-with-200m-raise/)). The forecast is the retention engine.
2. **Passive / wearable capture — Oura & Whoop.** Data arrives without daily effort. Oura did **~$1B revenue in 2025 (~+100% YoY)**, ~80% hardware / 20% subscription, ~74% smart-ring share H1-2025, pacing past 5M paid members; Whoop runs a subscription-first model for committed athletes ([Sacra — Oura](https://sacra.com/c/oura/); [Omdia](https://omdia.tech.informa.com/blogs/2025/nov/empowering-the-health-and-fitness-ecosystem-with-smart-rings); [Bloomberg, 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-05-11/fitbit-oura-whoop-see-predicting-health-outcomes-as-new-aim-for-trackers)). Wearables are now explicitly pivoting from "tracking" to **predicting health outcomes** — the next moat.
3. **B2B / clinical, prescribed — oncology ePRO.** Electronic patient-reported-outcome monitoring (Basch et al.) showed **prolonged overall survival, fewer hospitalisations, better QoL**, with real-world **retention of 94% / 88% / 73% / 67% at 3/6/9/12 months** — and *older* patients retained *better* ([PMC — large-scale ePRO implementation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9750604/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36240475/); [ASCO Educational Book](https://ascopubs.org/doi/full/10.1200/EDBK_390678)). It works because it's **prescribed, EMR-integrated, and tied to a life-or-death outcome** — the opposite of a discretionary consumer tracker.

### The pattern

Trackers succeed when **the user isn't the one doing the tracking** (passive wearables), when **tracking buys a prediction they want** (Flo), or when **a clinician prescribes it and it's tied to an outcome** (ePRO). Discretionary, manual, generic self-tracking with low-confidence output fails on both accuracy and retention.

**Implication for ditto:** avoid the "yet another symptom diary" trap. If tracking is in scope, anchor it to a prediction, a wearable feed, or a clinical pathway with a clinician on the other end.

## Deep-dive blocks (ready-to-embed overlays)

### (a) AI assistants / symptom-checkers — winners & losers

The symptom-checker market bifurcated cleanly: **clinically-validated B2B infrastructure won; consumer-facing AI hype died.** Ada Health (EU/Germany) and Infermedica (EU/Poland) both reached durability by licensing validated triage to health systems, payers and pharma — Ada hit profitability with ~260% YoY revenue growth and a $90M Bayer-led round; Infermedica is API-only with Symptomate as a free credibility funnel. K Health (US) won by keeping a clinician in the loop and partnering with Cedars-Sinai and Mayo. Babylon is the warning: consumer overreach, NHS capitation that lost money on every patient, *Lancet*- and MHRA-disputed accuracy, and an adversarial response to safety critics — SPAC at ~$4.2B to Chapter 7 in 18 months. **Lesson for ditto: anchor on clinical validation + B2B distribution; use the consumer surface as trust/funnel, not as the business model. (Ada/Infermedica are EU comps — directly relevant to MDR/GDPR.)**

### (b) Digital coaches — and why engagement decays

The coaching winners (Hinge, Omada, Sword, Virta, Hello Heart) all sell B2B2C to employers/payers, prove hard clinical + claims-cost outcomes, and keep a human or a committed medication in the loop — and it shows in retention (Hinge 98% client / 117% NDR; Omada 90% customer retention). The strugglers are DTC-exposed or sub-scale: Noom did multiple 2024 layoffs as its original program shrank; Dario opened a strategic review in 2025; Lark's fully-automated AI coach is scalable but exposed to engagement decay. Engagement decay is the universal enemy — ~70% of users quit within 100 days, mental-health apps retain ~4% — and the antidotes are reimbursed outcomes, human accountability, riding a GLP-1/condition the patient is already committed to, and passive data capture. **Lesson for ditto: durable engagement is bought with a clinical outcome, a clinician, a medication anchor, or passive data — and ideally a payer paying for it.**

### (c) Trackers — failures vs surprising successes

Generic symptom/wellness trackers are low-value on both axes that matter: accuracy hasn't improved in years (symptom-checker triage ~55.8% in 2020 vs 59.1% in 2015) and retention is dismal (~70% abandon within 100 days; ~4% for mental-health apps). The exceptions all break the "discretionary manual self-tracking" mold. Flo turned cycle data into a *prediction* users return for and became a unicorn. Oura and Whoop capture data *passively* via hardware — Oura did ~$1B revenue in 2025 (~80% hardware) — and are now pivoting from tracking to outcome prediction. Oncology ePRO is the clinical proof point: prescribed, EMR-integrated, tied to survival, with 67–94% retention over 12 months and *better* retention among older patients. **Lesson for ditto: don't ship another symptom diary; tie any tracking to a prediction, a wearable feed, or a clinician-prescribed pathway.**
