## Key takeaways

- **Q1 2026 was the strongest digital-health Q1 since the 2021 pandemic peak.** Rock Health counted **$4.0B across 110 deals** in the US, up from $3.0B in Q1 2025 (≈ **+33% YoY**), with average deal size at **$36.7M** — the highest single-quarter average since Q4 2021 ([Rock Health, 2026](https://rockhealth.com/insights/q1-2026-funding-overview-capital-continues-concentrating-and-four-other-market-signals/)). Confidence: High. ⚑US→EU.
- **Capital is concentrating, not broadening.** **12 mega-deals (≥$100M) = 59% of all Q1 2026 dollars** — one of the highest concentrations Rock Health has ever tracked ([Rock Health, 2026](https://rockhealth.com/insights/q1-2026-funding-overview-capital-continues-concentrating-and-four-other-market-signals/)). The "two markets" / haves-vs-have-nots dynamic from 2025 has intensified.
- **AI is now "table stakes."** AI-tagged companies were **54% of 2025 funding** (up from 37% in 2024); Rock Health **retired its "AI deal" tracker in Q1 2026** because the label no longer distinguishes anything ([Rock Health, 2026](https://rockhealth.com/insights/q1-2026-funding-overview-capital-continues-concentrating-and-four-other-market-signals/); [Galen Growth, 2026](https://www.galengrowth.com/digital-health-q1-2026-trends/)). Confidence: High.
- **Europe outgrew the US in 2025.** European digital-health funding rose **+15% YoY to $6.2B** (Galen Growth), capturing a **record ~26% of global** funding in H1, while US funding hit a five-year low mid-year before the late-2025/Q1-2026 rebound ([Galen Growth, 2026](https://www.galengrowth.com/digital-health-funding-2025/)). Confidence: Med (Galen and Rock Health use different geographic scopes).
- **Consumer/patient-facing is recovering, led by wearables, longevity and lab-testing — not classic wellness apps.** 2025–26 megarounds: **Oura $900M** (Oct 2025, ~$11B val), **Whoop $575M** (Mar 2026, $10.1B val), **Function Health $298M** (Nov 2025, $2.5B val), **Midi Health $100M** (Feb 2026, unicorn). Confidence: High.
- **The old "−40% consumer-app" narrative needs updating.** The decline was concentrated in **app-only femtech/wellness trackers** (femtech fell from ~14.7% of digital-health funding in 2020 to ~8.5% in 2024). Money rotated into **D2C lab-testing, hardware wearables and "longevity"** — Rock Health explicitly notes investors "began backing D2C again in 2025" ([Rock Health, 2026](https://rockhealth.com/insights/2025-year-end-digital-health-funding-overview-a-tale-of-two-markets/); [Galen Growth, 2025](https://www.galengrowth.com/femtechs-rise-and-roadblocks-digital-health-funding-growth-and-gaps/)). Confidence: Med.
- **NL/DACH context:** Dutch healthtech raised only **~$177M across 10 rounds in 2025** (Salvia $60M, Vitestro $70M Series B closed Mar 2026); DACH digital-health/equity funding rose **~+12% YoY** in early 2026. Europe's top rounds skewed German/UK/Nordic, not Dutch. Confidence: Med.

## 4. Is consumer health-app funding still falling, or recovering in 2026?

**Verdict: the "−40%" decline applied to the *narrow* app-tracker segment and is now outdated as a headline. Consumer health funding overall is recovering in 2025–26 — but the money rotated into hardware, lab-testing and clinically-validated women's health, away from pure wellness/tracking apps.**

What the data shows:

- **Femtech (the segment most associated with consumer apps) shrank as a *share*:** from a **~14.7% peak of digital-health funding in 2020 to ~8.5% in 2024** ([Galen Growth, 2025](https://www.galengrowth.com/femtechs-rise-and-roadblocks-digital-health-funding-growth-and-gaps/), T1). Galen explicitly flags that **"tracking apps and menopause supplements" are oversaturated** and expects more closures/M&A there.
- **But D2C is back:** Rock Health's 2025 year-end report states investors **"began backing D2C again in 2025, with a wave of activity around D2C lab testing"** ([Rock Health, 2026](https://rockhealth.com/insights/2025-year-end-digital-health-funding-overview-a-tale-of-two-markets/), T1). Function Health's $298M (Nov 2025) and the Oura/Whoop wearable megarounds are the proof.
- **2026 confirms recovery at the top end:** Whoop $575M and Midi $100M both closed in Q1 2026; both are consumer/patient-facing. The recovery is **K-shaped** — large, branded, AI- or hardware-backed consumer plays raise easily; undifferentiated wellness apps still struggle.
- **Reframed narrative for the deck:** *"Consumer health-app funding isn't falling — it's bifurcating. Capital fled undifferentiated tracking apps (femtech share roughly halved 2020→2024) and concentrated into wearables, D2C diagnostics and clinically-integrated women's health, which posted multiple $100M–$900M rounds across late-2025 and Q1-2026."* Confidence: Med-High (direction is well-evidenced; the original "−40% H1 2025" figure itself could not be confirmed in an accessible T1 source and should be treated as **unverified / superseded**).

### Block A — Wellness / market-sizing context
Global digital-health VC deployed **~$28.8B in 2025 (+9% YoY)**, while the US alone hit **$14.2B across 482 deals** — the best since 2022 — and opened 2026 with **$4.0B in Q1**, the strongest first quarter since the pandemic peak. The structural story is **concentration**: mega-deals were 42% of 2025 funding and 59% of Q1 2026 funding. Within consumer wellness, **femtech's share of digital-health funding roughly halved** (≈14.7% in 2020 → ≈8.5% in 2024), even as longevity/wearables and D2C lab-testing surged — a clear signal that "wellness" capital is rotating toward clinically-validated, hardware- or AI-backed products.
