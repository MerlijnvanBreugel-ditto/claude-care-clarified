Every medication app is the same three mechanics

The most-used medication apps — the global top 20 plus the NL players that matter — dismantled into the only three jobs they actually do: get the medication in, nudge you, and let you tick it off.

Real in-app flows (via Mobbin) and App Store / Google Play screenshots, mapped one-to-one against that triad. Usage figures are sourced and labelled [FACT] or [INFERENCE] — read the caveats before quoting any number.

Entry

How a medication gets into the app — database search, barcode, photo, pharmacy sync, or manual.

Reminders

How it nudges you — fixed alarms, windows, critical alerts, refill prompts, caregiver pings.

Check

How you log a dose — tap, swipe, “took all”, taken/skipped/late — or whether it bothers at all.

What repeats

Cross-cutting patterns

Entry is a database-search problem

The good apps resolve a name to a real drug (Apple, MyTherapy, Pill Reminder, Dosecast); pharmacy apps skip entry entirely by syncing the dispensed list. Manual-only entry (Pillboxie) now reads as dated.

The check is the product

Taken / skipped / “took all” / late-logging is where these apps live or die. Pharmacy and PBM apps that omit it (Express Scripts, Caremark) are ordering tools wearing a medication costume.

Reminders compete on escape velocity

Plain push is table stakes. The differentiators are Critical Alerts that break Focus, reminder windows (Round), and the caregiver escalation on a missed dose (Medisafe Medfriend, CVS).

The caregiver loop is the moat attempt

Every app reaching for defensibility adds a second person — Medfriend, CVS caregiver alerts, MyMeds’ Circle of Care, CareZone’s family list, NL’s DigiD authorisation. Reminders are commodity; the relationship is not.

Nobody sustains this on consumer subscriptions

The survivors monetise B2B — pharma patient-support programmes, payers, pharmacy fulfilment. The two best consumer apps went the other way and died as consumer products: Round folded into Alto, Mango into pharma PSP, CareZone hit 3.5M members and was absorbed and shut. Medisafe’s Jan-2026 paywall is the live test of whether consumers will pay; the EU incumbent MyTherapy stays free precisely because pharma pays for it.

So what — for Ditto

A pill reminder is a commodity. Don’t build one.

  1. The reminder rail is not a wedge.

    Dozens of interchangeable apps plus three platform sub-features already own it, and none of them owns it. Consistent with the market-deep-dive thesis: our spine is the understood conversation, not the alarm.

  2. The EU slot is taken — by MyTherapy, not Medisafe.

    Free, #1 Medical in NL, chronic-condition oriented, backed by listed Redcare Pharmacy. Benchmark against it, and assume the generic-reminder job is already done for our users.

  3. In NL the real incumbents are the pharmacy apps.

    MedGemak (1.1M) and Service Apotheek (720k) are DigiD-linked and wired into actual dispensing. That integration — not the reminder UI — is the defensibility. Integrate with the rail; don’t rebuild it.

  4. If we touch medication, our angle is the understood list, not the tick.

    What none of these do well: explain why each medication was prescribed, in the patient’s own words, tied to the conversation that started it — and carry the caregiver inside that understanding. That is adjacent to our spine and to the oncology beachhead, where regimens are dense and timing matters.
